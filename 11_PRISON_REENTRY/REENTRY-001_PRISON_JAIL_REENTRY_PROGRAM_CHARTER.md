ARTICLE I — PROGRAM NAME

The official controlled program name is:

REENTRY-001 — The Tabernacle Prison, Jail & Reentry Program

The Board may authorize separate public-facing program names for particular correctional, mentoring, education, or reentry initiatives.

⸻

ARTICLE II — PURPOSE

The purpose of REENTRY-001 is to provide religious, educational, mentoring, and charitable services to:

* Incarcerated individuals
* Individuals preparing for release
* Formerly incarcerated individuals
* Returning citizens
* Families affected by incarceration
* Other individuals facing legitimate reentry barriers

The program shall seek to strengthen:

* Education
* Character
* Employment readiness
* Life skills
* Community connections
* Faith
* Personal responsibility
* Successful reintegration

⸻

ARTICLE III — CORE OPERATING RULE

NONPROFIT STATUS ≠ PRISON ACCESS

The Tabernacle has no independent right to enter or operate within:

* Federal prisons
* State prisons
* Local jails
* Detention centers
* Residential reentry centers
* Other secure correctional facilities

Every institutional program shall operate through the approval, credentialing, supervision, security, and scheduling requirements of the applicable authority.

⸻

ARTICLE V — INSTITUTIONAL PARTNERSHIPS

The Tabernacle may seek partnerships with:

* Federal Bureau of Prisons facilities
* State departments of correction
* Local jails
* Sheriff’s offices
* Detention centers
* Reentry agencies
* Residential reentry centers
* Probation/community-supervision partners where appropriate
* Chaplaincy departments
* Education departments

Each relationship shall be treated independently.

Approval from one institution does not establish approval at another.

⸻

ARTICLE VI — FACILITY CONTACT

The designated institutional contact may include:

* Chaplain
* Volunteer coordinator
* Reentry coordinator
* Education department
* Program manager
* Community relations representative
* Warden/designee
* Other authorized official

The Tabernacle shall follow the facility’s identified chain of authority.

⸻

ARTICLE VII — VOLUNTEER AUTHORIZATION

No person shall represent themselves as an authorized correctional volunteer solely because they:

* Volunteer with The Tabernacle
* Are a minister
* Are a founder
* Are a Board member
* Have previously visited a prison
* Have passed an unrelated background check

Institutional approval is independently required.

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — VOLUNTEER CLEARANCE

Correctional volunteers may be required to complete:

* Application
* Identity verification
* Background investigation
* Fingerprinting
* Orientation
* Security training
* PREA-related training
* Volunteer training
* Facility-specific instruction
* Program-specific training
* Annual or periodic renewal

The Tabernacle shall maintain an internal record of approved status without publicly exposing sensitive clearance information.

⸻

ARTICLE IX — PROGRAM APPROVAL

Before a program begins inside a correctional institution, leadership should obtain confirmation addressing:

* Program title
* Program purpose
* Instructor/volunteer
* Curriculum
* Materials
* Schedule
* Location
* Participant eligibility
* Facility supervisor
* Security requirements
* Media/equipment permissions

⸻

ARTICLE X — CURRICULUM

Curriculum may be developed by The Tabernacle or adopted from approved sources.

Each curriculum should identify:

* Program ID
* Learning objectives
* Session structure
* Instructor
* Materials
* Duration
* Participant activities
* Assessment where appropriate
* Religious or secular classification
* Version
* Rights status

Institutional approval overrides internal curriculum approval.

⸻

ARTICLE XI — MATERIALS

Books, papers, curriculum, religious materials, photographs, electronic devices, videos, audio, artwork, educational media, and other items shall enter secure facilities only as permitted.

APPROVED PROGRAM ≠ APPROVED ITEM

Individual materials may require separate approval.

⸻

ARTICLE XII — ELECTRONIC MEDIA

The Tabernacle shall not assume that:

* Phones
* Tablets
* Laptops
* USB drives
* Cameras
* Recording devices
* Internet-connected devices
* Audio equipment

are permitted inside a correctional facility.

Facility policy controls.

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — RELIGIOUS PROGRAMMING

The Tabernacle is a Christian organization and may provide explicitly Christian programs when approved.

The organization shall respect institutional requirements protecting voluntary religious participation.

No participant shall be told that religious participation guarantees:

* Release
* Parole
* Sentence reduction
* Preferential institutional treatment
* Housing
* Employment
* Money
* Government benefits

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — EDUCATIONAL PROGRAMMING

Educational programming may include:

* Classroom instruction
* Workshops
* Lectures
* Guided discussion
* Curriculum
* Assignments
* Approved videos/media
* Mentoring
* Reentry preparation

Educational claims shall remain accurate.

The Tabernacle shall not promise academic credit unless authorized to award such credit.

⸻

ARTICLE XV — ARTS AND MEDIA

The program may use institution-approved artistic, musical, audiovisual, or other educational media where such material furthers an approved religious, educational, therapeutic, cultural, or reentry purpose.

The correctional program shall not be used primarily to advertise or commercially promote private entertainment products.

Facility approval is required for materials brought into the institution.

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — PERSONAL BOUNDARIES

Employees and volunteers shall maintain appropriate boundaries with incarcerated participants.

Unless specifically permitted by institutional policy, personnel shall not:

* Establish unauthorized personal relationships
* Conduct unauthorized business
* Exchange unauthorized gifts
* Transfer money
* Carry messages between inmates and unauthorized third parties
* Provide contraband
* Circumvent monitored communication systems
* Give unauthorized contact information
* Perform unauthorized favors

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — CONTRABAND

No Tabernacle representative shall introduce, remove, transport, conceal, or facilitate contraband.

Facility definitions of contraband control.

When uncertain whether an item is permitted:

STOP → ASK AUTHORIZED FACILITY STAFF → DOCUMENT APPROVAL IF NECESSARY

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — MONEY

Employees and volunteers shall not provide money directly to an incarcerated participant except through a mechanism expressly authorized by the institution and The Tabernacle.

Participants shall not be solicited to make payments to:

* Volunteers
* Ministers
* Employees
* Founders
* Related businesses

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — GIFTS

Personal gift exchanges with incarcerated participants shall follow institutional rules.

The Tabernacle may make approved program donations to an institution when permitted.

Institutional donations shall be documented.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — PERSONAL BUSINESS

No representative shall use access to incarcerated persons to:

* Recruit customers
* Sell products
* Solicit investments
* Promote personal businesses
* Obtain unpaid labor
* Generate private commercial benefit
* Recruit participants into unrelated private ventures

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — COMMUNICATION

Communication with incarcerated persons shall occur only through authorized methods.

Possible approved methods may include:

* Program sessions
* Institutional correspondence
* Authorized electronic communication
* Approved mentoring systems
* Authorized telephone communication
* Approved post-release transition processes

Facility policy controls.

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — CONFIDENTIALITY

Volunteers shall not promise absolute confidentiality.

Information may need to be disclosed when required by:

* Facility policy
* Law
* Safety obligations
* Mandatory-reporting rules
* Threat assessment
* Safeguarding policy

Participants should not be misled about confidentiality.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — SECURITY INFORMATION

Representatives shall not collect, publish, or distribute sensitive information concerning:

* Security procedures
* Staff deployment
* Camera locations
* Controlled movement
* Vulnerabilities
* Emergency systems
* Restricted facility layouts

unless expressly authorized for legitimate program purposes.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIV — PHOTOGRAPHY AND RECORDING

No photograph, audio recording, video recording, livestream, interview, or other recording shall occur within a correctional facility unless expressly authorized.

Consent from a participant alone is not sufficient if institutional approval is required.

⸻

ARTICLE XXV — SOCIAL MEDIA

Staff and volunteers shall not post identifiable information about incarcerated program participants merely because they participated in a Tabernacle program.

Posts shall comply with:

* Facility policy
* Privacy requirements
* Consent requirements
* Safeguarding policy
* Organizational communications policy

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — CASE INFORMATION

Public GitHub and general Discord channels shall not contain:

* Inmate numbers
* Criminal histories
* Release dates where confidential
* Case records
* Medical information
* Mental-health information
* Personal correspondence
* Family information
* Legal documents
* Sensitive reentry plans

Program governance documents may remain in GitHub.

Individual case records shall use approved secure systems.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — MENTORING

Mentoring may begin during incarceration where institutionally approved.

A mentoring plan should identify:

* Mentor
* Participant
* Program manager
* Goals
* Communication method
* Meeting frequency
* Institutional rules
* Post-release plan where applicable

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — POST-RELEASE CONTINUITY

Where permitted, mentoring may continue after release.

The transition from incarcerated participant to community participant shall be documented.

Community support may then operate under The Tabernacle’s ordinary reentry policies rather than correctional-facility rules, except where continuing supervision conditions apply.

ARTICLE XXX — NO GUARANTEES

The Tabernacle shall accurately describe assistance.

The organization shall not guarantee:

* Housing
* Employment
* Government benefits
* Transportation
* College admission
* Parole
* Early release
* Legal results

when those outcomes are controlled by another organization or government authority.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXI — LEGAL SERVICES

The Tabernacle shall not provide individualized legal advice unless services are delivered through appropriately authorized legal professionals.

The organization may provide referrals to:

* Legal aid
* Public defenders
* Licensed attorneys
* Other lawful legal-service providers

⸻

ARTICLE XXXII — MEDICAL AND MENTAL-HEALTH SERVICES

Employees and volunteers shall not exceed their credentials.

The program may refer participants to qualified providers.

Religious counseling shall not be falsely represented as licensed clinical treatment.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIII — FAMILY REINTEGRATION

Where appropriate, post-release programs may support:

* Healthy family communication
* Parenting education
* Community resources
* Family-service referrals
* Mentoring

The organization shall respect court orders, custody restrictions, protective orders, and supervision requirements.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIV — FOOD AND BASIC NEEDS

Released participants may access appropriate HHS-001 services based on program eligibility.

Reentry participants shall not receive preferential access merely because they participated in a prison program unless the charitable program legitimately targets the reentry population.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXV — EDUCATION CONNECTION

* Entrepreneurship

* Communication
* Life skills

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVI — EMPLOYMENT PARTNERSHIPS

The organization shall not promise employment.

Potential employer partners should be informed accurately about the program.

* Reentry housing

* Other housing providers
