ARTICLE I — PROGRAM NAME

The official controlled program name is:

MIN-001 — The Tabernacle 24/7 Christian Ministry & Refuge Program

Public-facing locations may simply be identified as:

THE TABERNACLE

Individual properties may later receive site identifiers such as:

TAB-001

TAB-002

TAB-003

⸻

ARTICLE II — PURPOSE

The purpose of MIN-001 is to establish Christian places where people may encounter:

* Worship
* Prayer
* Scripture
* Pastoral care
* Christian teaching
* Community
* Compassion
* Education
* Food assistance
* Resource navigation
* Peaceful refuge

The long-term objective is to maintain locations that are available 24 hours a day, seven days a week, where legally permissible, financially sustainable, appropriately staffed, insured, and safe.

⸻

ARTICLE III — FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLE

The Tabernacle shall combine:

WORSHIP

REFUGE

SERVICE

EDUCATION

RESTORATION

The physical building exists to support the mission.

The mission does not exist merely to own buildings.

⸻

ARTICLE IV — 24/7 DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY MEAN SHELTER

The Tabernacle shall distinguish between three operating modes.

MODE A — 24/7 MINISTRY ACCESS

The building remains open for activities such as:

* Prayer
* Worship
* Pastoral care
* Scripture
* Community ministry
* Education
* Food assistance
* Resource navigation
* Quiet sitting

This does not automatically include sleeping or residential occupancy.

⸻

MODE B — NON-RESIDENTIAL REFUGE

The facility may provide short-duration refuge such as:

* Warming
* Cooling
* Safety from severe weather
* Seating
* Water
* Meals
* Hygiene assistance
* Resource navigation
* Pastoral support

subject to lawful occupancy and facility rules.

This does not automatically authorize overnight sleeping.

⸻

MODE C — OVERNIGHT / SHELTER OPERATIONS

People may sleep or remain overnight as shelter residents only after The Tabernacle confirms all required:

* Land-use approvals
* Zoning
* Occupancy classification
* Fire/life-safety requirements
* Building requirements
* Shelter requirements
* Sanitation requirements
* Insurance
* Staffing
* Security
* Other applicable approvals

CONTROL RULE

OPEN 24/7 ≠ AUTHORIZED OVERNIGHT SHELTER

⸻

ARTICLE V — SITE AUTHORIZATION

Every operating location shall receive a unique site identifier.

Example:

TAB-001

A site may not advertise itself as a 24/7 location until leadership has approved the site’s operational readiness.

⸻

ARTICLE VI — SITE READINESS GATE

Before 24/7 operation begins, the site file shall address:

* Legal occupancy
* Intended use
* Zoning
* Building condition
* Fire safety
* Exits
* Emergency lighting
* Alarm systems
* Restrooms
* Water
* Sanitation
* Kitchen status
* Insurance
* Staffing
* Security
* Accessibility considerations
* Emergency procedures
* Neighbor/community considerations
* Financial sustainability

⸻

ARTICLE VII — PROPERTY USE

A building’s historical use as a church does not automatically establish that every proposed Tabernacle activity is authorized.

Each site must be evaluated according to its actual intended uses.

PROPERTY OWNED ≠ PROPERTY PERMITTED

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — HOURS

The long-term standard is:

24 HOURS PER DAY

7 DAYS PER WEEK

365 DAYS PER YEAR

unless temporary closure is necessary because of:

* Safety
* Staffing
* Weather
* Utility outage
* Maintenance
* Security
* Government order
* Insurance requirement
* Financial emergency
* Other legitimate operational reason

⸻

ARTICLE IX — STAFFING MODEL

A full 24/7 location should use defined shifts.

A working model may include:

Shift A — Morning

7:00 AM – 3:00 PM

Shift B — Evening

3:00 PM – 11:00 PM

Shift C — Overnight

11:00 PM – 7:00 AM

Specific times may be changed by site leadership.

Every open shift shall have an accountable person in charge.

⸻

ARTICLE X — SHIFT LEAD

Every shift should identify a:

SHIFT LEAD

The Shift Lead is responsible for:

* Operational control
* Opening/continuity
* Staff coordination
* Volunteer coordination
* Guest safety
* Security coordination
* Emergency escalation
* Incident documentation
* Shift handoff

⸻

ARTICLE XI — MINIMUM STAFFING

The Board or executive leadership shall establish minimum staffing for each location based on:

* Building size
* Attendance
* Program activity
* Time of day
* Security conditions
* Population served
* Overnight status
* Insurance requirements

No site shall remain open merely to satisfy a 24/7 branding objective when staffing is unsafe.

⸻

ARTICLE XII — STAFFING AGENCIES

The Tabernacle may use temporary staffing providers for lawful operational roles.

Potential functions include:

* Kitchen work
* Cleaning
* Facilities
* Administrative support
* Event support
* Other operational work

Security work shall receive separate licensing and regulatory review.

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — SECURITY PERSONNEL

Security personnel may include properly authorized:

* Employees
* Contract security
* Staffing-company personnel
* Licensed security providers

depending upon applicable law.

The Tabernacle shall verify any licensing, registration, training, insurance, or company requirements applicable to the work actually performed.

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — SECURITY MISSION

The security mission is:

PROTECT PEOPLE

DE-ESCALATE CONFLICT

PRESERVE ACCESS

PROTECT CHARITABLE PROPERTY

Security is not intended to criminalize:

* Poverty
* Homelessness
* Mental distress
* Appearance
* Lack of housing
* Inability to donate

⸻

ARTICLE XV — ACCESS

The Tabernacle intends its ministry locations to be welcoming.

Access may nevertheless be restricted when necessary because of:

* Violence
* Credible threats
* Weapons
* Harassment
* Sexual misconduct
* Drug distribution
* Destruction of property
* Serious disruption
* Health/safety emergencies
* Other conduct creating material danger

Restrictions shall focus on conduct rather than social status.

⸻

Every location should maintain clearly communicated house rules.

Core rules may include:

1. Treat people with dignity.
2. No violence.
3. No threats.
4. No harassment.
5. No sexual activity on premises.
6. No unauthorized weapons.
7. No drug distribution.
8. No theft.
9. No intentional property destruction.
10. Follow emergency instructions.
11. Respect designated private/restricted areas.
12. Follow lawful staff instructions concerning safety.

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — WORSHIP

The Tabernacle may conduct worship throughout the day.

Worship may include:

* Prayer
* Scripture reading
* Preaching
* Teaching
* Christian music
* Testimony
* Communion where appropriate
* Individual prayer
* Group prayer
* Quiet reflection

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — ALWAYS-AVAILABLE MINISTRY

A 24/7 site should strive to maintain access to at least some of the following at all times:

* Prayer
* Scripture
* Safe human contact
* Basic resource information
* Water
* Restroom access where operationally permitted
* Emergency assistance procedures

Not every program must operate continuously.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — PASTORAL CARE

Pastoral care may include:

* Prayer
* Spiritual guidance
* Scripture
* Grief support
* Encouragement
* Religious counseling
* Referral to appropriate professional services

Pastoral care shall not be falsely represented as:

* Medical treatment
* Psychiatric treatment
* Licensed psychotherapy
* Legal advice

unless appropriately qualified professionals actually provide those services.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — RELIGIOUS PARTICIPATION

The Tabernacle is openly Christian.

People entering the facility may encounter:

* Christian symbols
* Prayer
* Scripture
* Worship
* Christian teaching

Humanitarian services shall comply with applicable law, contracts, grants, correctional requirements, and Board policy regarding voluntary participation.

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — PEOPLE EXPERIENCING HOMELESSNESS

People experiencing homelessness shall be treated as ministry participants and human beings worthy of dignity.

Homelessness itself shall not constitute grounds for exclusion.

The Tabernacle may connect guests with HHS-001 services.

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — MEALS

Meals may be provided through:

HHS-001 — Meal & Homelessness Relief Program

The ministry charter does not replace:

* Food-safety procedures
* Kitchen policies
* Health requirements
* Meal-program controls

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — EDUCATION

Educational activities may be provided through:

EDU-001 — The Tabernacle Education Program

* Classes
* Lectures

* Job readiness
* Technology
* Biblical education
* History
* Entrepreneurship
* Life skills

⸻

ARTICLE XXIV — REENTRY SERVICES

Formerly incarcerated participants may receive community services through:

REENTRY-001

A Tabernacle site may function as a post-release community-support location where permitted and appropriately staffed.

⸻

ARTICLE XXV — CHILDREN

If children are present, The Tabernacle’s safeguarding requirements apply.

A 24/7 building is not automatically a childcare facility.

The organization shall not undertake unsupervised childcare merely because children accompany adult guests.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — VULNERABLE ADULTS

Programs shall account for people who may face elevated vulnerability because of:

* Disability
* Age
* Homelessness
* Cognitive impairment
* Crisis
* Trauma
* Exploitation
* Other circumstances

Staff shall follow safeguarding and escalation procedures.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — ACCESSIBILITY

The Tabernacle should seek to make its facilities and programs meaningfully accessible to people with disabilities as part of its service mission.

For each property, leadership shall separately review:

* Federal requirements applicable to the organization or funding source
* State law
* Local building/accessibility codes
* Grant requirements
* Renovation requirements
* Program-specific obligations

No single assumed exemption shall substitute for a site-specific compliance review.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — RESTROOMS

Twenty-four-hour operations require a sustainable restroom plan addressing:

* Access
* Cleaning
* Supplies
* Inspection
* Damage
* Biohazards
* Sharps
* Plumbing failures
* Privacy
* Security

Restrooms shall be inspected according to site procedures.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIX — SHOWERS

Showers may be offered only after appropriate:

* Facility approval
* Plumbing capacity
* Sanitation procedures
* Privacy controls
* Staffing
* Cleaning
* Safeguarding
* Insurance review

⸻

ARTICLE XXX — SLEEPING

Sleeping inside a Tabernacle location shall not be allowed as an organizational shelter program until Mode C has been authorized.

Incidental dozing by a visitor shall not be used to covertly operate an unapproved shelter.

If recurring overnight sleeping develops, leadership shall address the underlying use directly.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXII — BED / CAPACITY LIMITS

If overnight service is authorized, each location shall establish a lawful maximum capacity.

Capacity shall not be exceeded merely because additional people need assistance.

When full, staff should use referral and emergency protocols rather than create unsafe occupancy.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIII — PERSONAL PROPERTY

Site procedures shall address guest belongings.

The Tabernacle should clearly state:

* Storage limits
* Prohibited items
* Unattended property rules
* Disposal procedures
* Liability limitations where lawful

Staff shall not casually dispose of essential identification, medication, or clearly valuable property.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIV — MEDICATION

The Tabernacle shall not automatically assume responsibility for administering or storing medication.

Medication assistance requires separate policy and review.

Emergency medication issues shall be handled according to applicable emergency procedures and professional guidance.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXV — INTOXICATION

A person experiencing intoxication or substance-use crisis shall be evaluated according to safety and emergency procedures.

Staff shall distinguish, where reasonably possible, between:

* Disruptive conduct
* Medical emergency
* Need for emergency services
* Need for referral

The Tabernacle shall not undertake clinical detoxification without proper authorization and capability.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVI — MENTAL-HEALTH CRISIS

Staff and volunteers shall not diagnose.

When a person appears to present an immediate danger to themselves or others or another serious emergency exists:

PROTECT IMMEDIATE SAFETY

CONTACT APPROPRIATE EMERGENCY/CRISIS SERVICES

↓

FOLLOW SITE PROTOCOL

↓

DOCUMENT SECURELY

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVII — EMERGENCY RESPONSE

Every site shall maintain procedures for:

* Fire
* Medical emergency
* Violence
* Active threat
* Severe weather
* Missing child
* Utility failure
* Gas leak
* Flood
* Building evacuation
* Food emergency
* Other foreseeable hazards

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVIII — FIRE AND EVACUATION

Every operating shift shall know:

* Exit locations
* Assembly location
* Emergency contacts
* Alarm procedures
* Evacuation responsibilities

Exits shall not be obstructed.

Occupancy limits shall be respected.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIX — INCIDENT REPORTING

Material incidents shall be documented.

The secure incident record may contain:

* Date
* Time
* Location
* Persons involved
* Objective description
* Immediate response
* Emergency services contacted
* Witnesses
* Follow-up
* Supervisor review

Detailed incident records shall not be stored in public GitHub repositories.

⸻

ARTICLE XL — CAMERAS

Security cameras may be used subject to applicable law and privacy policy.

Cameras should not ordinarily be placed in areas where people reasonably expect heightened privacy, such as:

* Toilets
* Shower areas
* Changing areas

Camera retention and access shall be controlled.

⸻

ARTICLE XLI — KEYS AND ACCESS CONTROL

The facility shall maintain a controlled system for:

* Keys
* Access cards
* Alarm credentials
* Restricted rooms
* Kitchen access
* Offices
* Financial records
* Medication areas if any
* IT systems

Access shall be removed when no longer required.

* Unsanitary

* Inconsistent with program needs

ARTICLE XLVII — NIGHT OPERATIONS

The overnight shift creates elevated risk and shall receive enhanced controls.

These may include:

* Minimum staffing
* Security coverage
* Restricted-access zones
* Scheduled facility walkthroughs
* Emergency communication
* Supervisor escalation
* Controlled entrances
* Shift documentation

⸻

ARTICLE XLVIII — NO SOLO HIGH-RISK OPERATIONS

A single volunteer should not be left responsible for an entire high-risk 24/7 facility merely because no other person is available.

If safe staffing falls below the approved minimum, leadership may:

* Restrict services
* Close part of the facility
* Temporarily close the location
* Call additional staff

⸻

ARTICLE XLIX — NEIGHBORHOOD RELATIONS

Each Tabernacle location should maintain responsible relationships with surrounding communities.

Operational planning should address:

* Noise
* Trash
* Parking
* Outdoor gatherings
* Deliveries
* Lighting
* Safety
* Property maintenance

Christian ministry and community stewardship should reinforce one another.

* Police
* Fire
* EMS
* Crisis services
* Child protection
* Adult protective services
* Other authorities

ARTICLE LI — DATA AND PRIVACY

The Tabernacle shall not create an unnecessary surveillance database of vulnerable people merely because they enter a ministry location.

Collect only information reasonably necessary for:

* Services
* Safety

* Reporting

ARTICLE LII — PUBLIC COMMUNICATIONS

Staff shall not livestream or publish a person receiving charitable services without appropriate authorization.

A person’s crisis shall not become marketing material merely because it occurred at The Tabernacle.

⸻

ARTICLE LIII — MEDIA

Press and public-media access shall be controlled.

Leadership shall protect:

* Beneficiary dignity
* Children
* Confidentiality
* Security
* Staff privacy
* Correctional/reentry participants

ARTICLE LIV — INSURANCE GATE

Before a site opens continuously, leadership shall confirm that insurers have been given an accurate description of material operations.

Do not insure a location as an ordinary church while knowingly operating materially different undisclosed activities.

Potential coverage review may include:

* General liability
* Property
* Workers’ compensation
* Directors and officers
* Abuse/molestation
* Cyber
* Commercial automobile
* Food-related exposure
* Shelter/overnight exposure if applicable

⸻

ARTICLE LV — FINANCIAL SUSTAINABILITY

Before authorizing 24/7 operation, leadership should calculate the recurring cost of:

* Payroll
* Security
* Utilities
* Insurance
* Food
* Cleaning
* Repairs
* Supplies
* Technology
* Waste
* Property expenses

Twenty-four-hour operation shall not depend indefinitely upon undocumented personal spending by founders.

ARTICLE LXI — STOP AUTHORITY

A site may be partially or fully closed immediately when necessary because of:

* Fire hazard
* Structural danger
* Violence
* Utility failure
* Insufficient staffing
* Loss of insurance
* Government order
* Food/health hazard
* Serious safeguarding concern
* Other substantial threat
