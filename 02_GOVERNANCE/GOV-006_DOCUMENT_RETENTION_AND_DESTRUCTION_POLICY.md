ARTICLE I — PURPOSE

The Tabernacle, Inc. expects directors, officers, employees, ministers, contractors, volunteers, and agents to act lawfully, ethically, and consistently with the organization’s charitable mission.

This policy establishes procedures for reporting suspected wrongdoing without fear of retaliation.

⸻

ARTICLE II — REPORTABLE CONDUCT

Good-faith concerns may include suspected:

* Fraud
* Theft
* Embezzlement
* Diversion of charitable assets
* Falsification of financial records
* False invoices
* Improper reimbursements
* Misuse of restricted donations
* Undisclosed conflicts of interest
* Improper related-party transactions
* Bribery or kickbacks
* Abuse or neglect
* Sexual misconduct
* Exploitation
* Harassment
* Discrimination
* Retaliation
* Safeguarding violations
* Food-safety violations
* Serious security violations
* Improper treatment of unhoused persons
* Correctional-program violations
* Privacy breaches
* Destruction or falsification of records
* Material violations of organizational policy
* Material violations of law

⸻

ARTICLE III — WHO MAY REPORT

Reports may be made by:

* Directors
* Officers
* Employees
* Ministers
* Contractors
* Volunteers
* Program participants
* Donors
* Vendors
* Community partners
* Other persons interacting with The Tabernacle

⸻

ARTICLE IV — GOOD-FAITH STANDARD

A person does not need proof before raising a legitimate concern.

A report is made in good faith when the reporter reasonably believes that the information may indicate misconduct or a serious policy violation.

A report that ultimately proves incorrect is not automatically a bad-faith report.

⸻

ARTICLE V — REPORTING CHANNELS

Concerns may be reported to an appropriate:

* Supervisor
* Program Director
* Executive Director
* Safeguarding Officer
* Treasurer
* Secretary
* Board Chair
* Independent Director
* Board committee
* Outside professional designated by the Board

At least one reporting route shall permit the reporter to bypass the person who is the subject of the complaint.

⸻

ARTICLE VI — REPORTS CONCERNING EXECUTIVE LEADERSHIP

A complaint concerning the:

* Executive Director
* Founder
* President
* Senior officer
* Senior minister

shall be reported to an independent director, Board Chair where appropriate, governance committee, or outside counsel.

The person accused shall not control:

* Intake
* Assignment
* Investigation
* Findings
* Corrective action

⸻

ARTICLE VII — REPORTS CONCERNING BOARD CHAIR

If the Board Chair is implicated, the matter shall be directed to:

* Vice Chair if one exists
* Independent director
* Governance committee
* Independent legal counsel

The Chair shall be excluded from oversight of that matter.

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — EMERGENCIES

This policy does not replace emergency reporting.

For matters involving immediate threats such as:

* Violence
* Fire
* Medical emergency
* Child abuse
* Vulnerable-adult abuse
* Imminent danger

personnel shall follow emergency and legally required reporting procedures.

Do not wait for internal organizational review before contacting an outside authority when immediate or mandatory external reporting is required.

⸻

ARTICLE IX — SAFEGUARDING REPORTS

Reports involving:

* Child abuse
* Sexual abuse
* Exploitation
* Neglect
* Vulnerable adults

shall also be handled under SG-001.

An internal report does not automatically satisfy a person’s independent mandatory-reporting obligation.

⸻

ARTICLE X — CORRECTIONAL PROGRAM REPORTS

Where alleged conduct occurs in a prison, jail, detention center, or other correctional environment, applicable institutional reporting requirements shall also be followed.

The Tabernacle shall not conceal conduct from correctional authorities when reporting is legally or institutionally required.

⸻

ARTICLE XI — CONFIDENTIAL REPORTING

The organization shall attempt to protect the identity of a reporter to the extent reasonably possible.

Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed because information may need to be disclosed for:

* Investigation
* Legal compliance
* Safeguarding
* Insurance
* Due process
* Law enforcement
* Board oversight

⸻

ARTICLE XII — ANONYMOUS REPORTING

The Board may establish an anonymous or confidential reporting mechanism.

Possible methods include:

* Dedicated email
* Web form
* Telephone line
* Third-party reporting service
* Written submission to an independent director

Anonymous reporting shall not prevent investigation when sufficient information exists.

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — NON-RETALIATION

The Tabernacle prohibits retaliation against a person who in good faith:

* Reports suspected wrongdoing
* Participates in an investigation
* Supplies information
* Refuses to participate in unlawful conduct
* Raises a legitimate compliance concern

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — EXAMPLES OF RETALIATION

Prohibited retaliation may include:

* Termination
* Demotion
* Threats
* Intimidation
* Harassment
* Punitive scheduling
* Improper exclusion
* Reduction of duties
* Loss of volunteer opportunities
* Withholding legitimate services
* Adverse treatment because a report was made

⸻

ARTICLE XV — REPORTING RETALIATION

Suspected retaliation shall itself be reported and investigated.

The person accused of retaliation shall not determine whether their own conduct was retaliatory.

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — KNOWINGLY FALSE REPORTS

This policy protects good-faith reporting.

Knowingly fabricating accusations, evidence, or material facts may itself violate organizational policy.

A finding that an allegation was unsubstantiated does not by itself demonstrate that the reporter acted in bad faith.

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — INTAKE

Upon receiving a report, the authorized recipient should document:

* Date received
* General nature of concern
* Reporting channel
* Immediate safety concerns
* Persons implicated
* Documents/evidence identified
* External-reporting obligations
* Person assigned to review

Sensitive information shall be protected.

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — CONFLICT CHECK

Before assigning an investigator or reviewer, determine whether that person has:

* Personal involvement
* Family relationship
* Financial interest
* Supervisory conflict
* Other impairment of independence

If so, assign another qualified person.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — INVESTIGATION

An investigation should be:

* Prompt
* Impartial
* Proportionate
* Documented
* Confidential to the extent practical

The investigator may:

* Interview witnesses
* Review documents
* Review financial records
* Preserve electronic evidence
* Consult professionals
* Request additional information

⸻

ARTICLE XX — OUTSIDE INVESTIGATION

The Board may retain:

* Independent counsel
* Accountant
* Auditor
* Human-resources professional
* Safeguarding specialist
* Investigator
* Other qualified professional

when independence, complexity, privilege, or expertise warrants it.

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — RECORD PRESERVATION

Once a significant complaint is received, relevant records shall be preserved.

Do not:

* Delete messages
* Alter records
* Destroy receipts
* Modify minutes
* Erase video
* Remove relevant files

when those records may relate to the concern or investigation.

GOV-006 litigation/investigation hold controls shall apply.

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — INTERIM PROTECTIVE ACTION

The organization may take temporary protective action while a matter is investigated.

Examples include:

* Removing system access
* Changing reporting lines
* Suspending financial authority
* Removing beneficiary access
* Suspending volunteer duties
* Temporary administrative leave

Such measures are protective and do not necessarily constitute a final finding.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — FINDINGS

Investigation results should identify, as appropriate:

* Allegation
* Evidence considered
* Findings
* Policy implications
* Financial impact
* Safeguarding impact
* Required corrective action
* Reporting obligations

⸻

ARTICLE XXIV — CORRECTIVE ACTION

Appropriate responses may include:

* No further action
* Training
* Process improvement
* Restitution
* Financial correction
* Removal of authority
* Discipline
* Termination
* Contract termination
* Board action
* Insurance notification
* Regulatory reporting
* Law-enforcement reporting

⸻

ARTICLE XXV — NO PRIVATE SETTLEMENT OF CHARITABLE LOSS

An individual leader shall not privately settle or conceal suspected theft, diversion, abuse, or other material misconduct in order to protect reputation.

Material matters shall be handled through appropriate organizational authority.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — BOARD OVERSIGHT

The Board bears ultimate oversight responsibility for the effectiveness of this policy.

The Board should periodically receive aggregated information concerning:

* Number of reports
* Type of concerns
* Resolution status
* Material losses
* Corrective actions
* Retaliation allegations
* Control weaknesses

Sensitive personal information shall be limited to legitimate governance need.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — MATERIAL DIVERSION OF ASSETS

Suspected material diversion of charitable assets shall be escalated promptly to:

* Treasurer
* Appropriate independent Board leadership
* Legal/accounting professionals where appropriate

The organization shall evaluate applicable reporting and tax obligations.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — NO FOUNDER EXCEPTION

Founder status does not exempt anyone from:

* Investigation
* Recusal
* Corrective action
* Reporting requirements

FOUNDER ≠ CORPORATION

⸻

ARTICLE XXIX — POLICY COMMUNICATION

This policy shall be made reasonably available to:

* Directors
* Officers
* Employees
* Key volunteers

Relevant reporting instructions should be included in onboarding and training.

⸻

ARTICLE XXX — CONTROL PRINCIPLES

The Tabernacle adopts:

REPORT ≠ PROOF

UNSUBSTANTIATED ≠ BAD FAITH

AUTHORITY ≠ IMMUNITY

INTERNAL REPORT ≠ REQUIRED EXTERNAL REPORT

CONFIDENTIALITY ≠ CONCEALMENT

REPUTATION ≠ REASON TO SUPPRESS

REPORTING ≠ RETALIATION TARGET

PRESERVE THE EVIDENCE

PROTECT THE PEOPLE

PROTECT THE CHARITABLE ASSETS
