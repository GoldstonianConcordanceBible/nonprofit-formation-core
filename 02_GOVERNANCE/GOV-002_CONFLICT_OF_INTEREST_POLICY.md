ARTICLE I — PURPOSE

The purpose of this Conflict of Interest Policy is to protect the interests, assets, reputation, charitable mission, and tax-exempt status of The Tabernacle, Inc.

The policy establishes procedures for identifying, disclosing, evaluating, documenting, and resolving actual, potential, or perceived conflicts of interest.

The organization shall conduct transactions for the benefit of its religious, charitable, and educational mission rather than for the improper private benefit of founders, directors, officers, employees, family members, businesses, contractors, donors, or other private interests.

This policy supplements applicable federal law, state law, the Articles of Incorporation, and the Bylaws.

⸻

ARTICLE II — CORE PRINCIPLE

Every director, officer, executive, committee member, and other person exercising substantial organizational authority owes their primary organizational duty to:

THE TABERNACLE, INC.

When acting on behalf of The Tabernacle, organizational decisions must be made according to:

* The mission
* The Corporation’s charitable interests
* Fiduciary duties
* Applicable law
* The Articles
* The Bylaws
* Board-approved policy

Personal financial interests must not improperly control charitable decisions.

⸻

ARTICLE III — INTERESTED PERSON

An Interested Person includes any:

* Director
* Officer
* Founder
* Executive Director
* Chief Executive Officer
* Key employee
* Committee member exercising delegated Board authority
* Person with substantial influence over organizational decisions

who has a direct or indirect financial or personal interest related to a proposed organizational decision or transaction.

⸻

ARTICLE IV — FINANCIAL INTEREST

A person may have a financial interest when that person, directly or indirectly through business, investment, compensation, or family relationship:

1. Owns or invests in an entity doing or seeking to do business with The Tabernacle.
2. Receives compensation from The Tabernacle.
3. Receives compensation from a person or company doing or seeking to do business with The Tabernacle.
4. Has a potential ownership, investment, employment, or compensation relationship with a person or entity involved in a proposed transaction.
5. May receive a material financial benefit from an organizational decision.
6. Has a family member who may receive such a benefit.

A financial interest does not automatically establish wrongdoing.

It requires disclosure and appropriate review.

⸻

ARTICLE V — FAMILY RELATIONSHIPS

For purposes of heightened conflict review, relevant family relationships may include:

* Spouse
* Domestic partner
* Parent
* Child
* Sibling
* Grandparent
* Grandchild
* In-law
* Household member
* Other close relative whose financial interests could reasonably affect judgment

The Board may interpret this category broadly when necessary to protect the organization.

⸻

ARTICLE VI — BUSINESS RELATIONSHIPS

Potential conflicts may arise from relationships involving:

* Ownership
* Employment
* Consulting
* Investment
* Partnership
* Joint venture
* Contracting
* Lending
* Property ownership
* Intellectual property
* Licensing
* Management agreements
* Vendor relationships
* Referral arrangements
* Shared employees
* Shared facilities

⸻

ARTICLE VII — FOUNDER-RELATED TRANSACTIONS

Because founders may play multiple roles in the organization, transactions involving founders require enhanced review.

This includes transactions involving:

* A founder personally
* A founder’s spouse or family member
* A business owned or controlled by a founder
* Property owned by a founder
* Intellectual property owned by a founder
* Consulting performed by a founder
* Employment of a founder
* Reimbursement to a founder
* Loans involving a founder
* Purchases from a founder-related company

A founder shall not use founder status to bypass this policy.

FOUNDER ≠ EXEMPT FROM CONFLICT REVIEW

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — DUTY TO DISCLOSE

An Interested Person shall disclose an actual or possible conflict before the organization makes the affected decision whenever reasonably possible.

Disclosure should include all material facts known to the person.

Relevant information may include:

* Identity of the parties
* Nature of the relationship
* Ownership interest
* Expected compensation
* Financial benefit
* Family relationship
* Contract terms
* Property interest
* Alternative vendors
* Any other fact relevant to independent evaluation

Failure to disclose a known material conflict may constitute a violation of organizational policy.

⸻

ARTICLE IX — DETERMINING WHETHER A CONFLICT EXISTS

After disclosure of the relevant facts:

1. The Interested Person may answer factual questions.
2. The Interested Person shall leave the deliberation when appropriate.
3. Disinterested directors or authorized committee members shall determine whether a conflict exists.
4. The determination shall be documented.

The Interested Person shall not control the determination.

⸻

ARTICLE X — PROCEDURE FOR ADDRESSING A CONFLICT

When a conflict exists, the Board or authorized committee should:

1. Understand the proposed transaction.
2. Determine whether the transaction is necessary or advantageous to The Tabernacle.
3. Consider reasonable alternatives where appropriate.
4. Review comparable prices, compensation, rents, fees, or transaction terms when practical.
5. Determine whether the terms are fair and reasonable.
6. Determine whether the transaction advances The Tabernacle’s exempt purposes.
7. Determine whether the arrangement creates impermissible private benefit.
8. Exclude the Interested Person from voting.
9. Document the decision in the minutes.

⸻

ARTICLE XI — RECUSAL

An Interested Person shall not vote on a matter in which that person has a material conflict.

Where appropriate, that person shall leave the meeting during deliberation and voting.

The minutes shall record:

* The person’s name
* Nature of the disclosed interest
* Whether the person left the meeting
* Whether the person abstained
* The directors participating in the decision
* The final vote

⸻

ARTICLE XII — QUORUM AND CONFLICTS

An Interested Person’s presence shall be treated for quorum purposes only as permitted by applicable state law.

No conflicted director shall be used to create an artificial approval where independent approval is required.

State-specific rules shall control.

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — COMPENSATION

Compensation involving a founder, director, officer, executive, key employee, or related person requires heightened review.

No person shall vote on their own compensation.

The organization should evaluate:

* Job responsibilities
* Time commitment
* Experience
* Qualifications
* Compensation paid by comparable organizations
* Geographic market
* Organization size
* Budget
* Benefits
* Other economic benefits

The Board should rely on appropriate comparability information where reasonably available.

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — REASONABLE COMPENSATION

Compensation may include more than salary.

It may include:

* Wages
* Salary
* Bonuses
* Housing
* Vehicle benefits
* Insurance
* Retirement benefits
* Educational benefits
* Travel benefits
* Loans
* Expense payments
* Other economic benefits

All material compensation and benefits should be considered when evaluating reasonableness.

⸻

ARTICLE XV — COMPENSATION APPROVAL PROCEDURE

Compensation for a person with substantial influence over the Corporation should be approved using the following process:

1. The individual whose compensation is being determined does not vote.
2. Disinterested directors review comparable compensation information.
3. The Board determines that compensation is reasonable for services actually provided.
4. The basis for the decision is documented contemporaneously.
5. The approved compensation arrangement is recorded in Board minutes or other authorized records.

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — EMPLOYMENT OF FOUNDERS OR FAMILY MEMBERS

The Tabernacle may employ a founder or a founder’s family member when:

* There is a legitimate organizational need.
* The person is qualified.
* Duties are defined.
* Compensation is reasonable.
* The arrangement is independently reviewed.
* Required conflicts are disclosed.
* The affected person does not improperly approve their own employment or compensation.

Family employment must not become a mechanism for transferring charitable assets for private benefit.

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — VENDORS OWNED BY FOUNDERS OR DIRECTORS

The Tabernacle may enter into a transaction with a business owned by a founder, director, officer, or related person only after appropriate conflict review.

The Board should consider:

* Why the related vendor is being considered
* Alternative providers
* Market pricing
* Quality
* Availability
* Terms
* Whether the arrangement serves the organization better than reasonably available alternatives

The transaction must be fair to The Tabernacle.

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — PROPERTY TRANSACTIONS

This policy applies to:

* Purchasing property from an insider
* Selling property to an insider
* Leasing property from an insider
* Leasing property to an insider
* Using founder-owned property
* Construction contracts involving insiders
* Property-management contracts involving insiders

Independent valuation or market comparison should be obtained where appropriate.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — LOANS

The Tabernacle shall exercise extreme caution regarding loans involving:

* Directors
* Officers
* Founders
* Employees
* Family members

No loan may be made if prohibited by applicable law.

Any lawful loan or financing transaction involving an insider requires specific Board review and legal advice where appropriate.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — GIFTS AND FAVORS

Directors, officers, and employees shall not accept gifts, favors, entertainment, or benefits that could reasonably compromise their judgment.

Nominal gifts may be permitted under Board policy.

Substantial gifts from vendors, contractors, property sellers, grant applicants, or other interested parties must be disclosed.

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — DONORS

A donor’s contribution does not give that donor control over the Corporation except for lawful restrictions placed on a charitable gift that the Corporation accepts.

A donor may not purchase:

* Board authority
* Special voting rights
* Improper private benefits
* Preferential access to charitable assets
* Control over unrelated organizational decisions

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — REAL ESTATE DONORS

When a church, individual, or other party proposes donating real estate to The Tabernacle, the organization shall independently evaluate:

* Title
* Liens
* Environmental risks
* Restrictions
* Maintenance obligations
* Insurance
* Zoning
* Property condition
* Carrying costs
* Whether accepting the property advances the mission

A donated building is not automatically an advantageous gift.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — CONTRACTORS AND STAFFING COMPANIES

The Tabernacle may use:

* Employees
* Staffing agencies
* Contractors
* Vendors
* Volunteers

Any relationship involving a director, founder, officer, employee, or their related business shall be disclosed and independently reviewed.

Worker classification shall comply with applicable law and shall not be manipulated for private benefit or to avoid lawful obligations.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIV — GRANTS

A director, officer, founder, employee, or family member shall disclose any personal or financial interest in an organization being considered for a grant or charitable distribution.

The Interested Person shall not improperly influence the award.

⸻

ARTICLE XXV — PRISON AND REENTRY PROGRAMS

No person associated with The Tabernacle shall use a prison or reentry program to create unauthorized personal financial relationships with participants.

Employees and volunteers shall comply with applicable correctional rules concerning:

* Money
* Gifts
* Personal business
* Employment offers
* Communication
* Personal relationships
* Property
* Transactions with incarcerated participants

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — HOMELESSNESS PROGRAMS

No employee, officer, volunteer, or director shall exploit a person receiving homelessness, food, shelter, or other charitable services for:

* Personal labor
* Personal financial gain
* Sexual benefit
* Business recruitment
* Private fundraising
* Personal transactions

Beneficiaries must be treated as persons served by the charitable mission, not as sources of private benefit.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — EDUCATIONAL PROGRAMS

Educational programs shall not be structured primarily to funnel charitable resources toward a private business or individual.

Any use of privately owned educational content, technology, facilities, intellectual property, or services by The Tabernacle shall be evaluated under this policy when an insider has a financial interest.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — RECORDING THE DECISION

Board or committee minutes concerning a conflict should document:

* Name of Interested Person
* Nature of interest
* Material facts disclosed
* Whether a conflict was determined to exist
* Persons present for discussion
* Persons present for vote
* Recusal
* Alternatives considered
* Comparability information reviewed
* Decision
* Vote
* Basis for determining that the transaction was fair and reasonable

⸻

ARTICLE XXIX — VIOLATIONS

If the Board has reasonable cause to believe a person failed to disclose a conflict:

1. The person shall be informed of the concern.
2. The person shall have an opportunity to explain.
3. The Board may investigate.
4. If a violation occurred, corrective or disciplinary action may be taken.

Possible actions include:

* Formal warning
* Recusal
* Removal from transaction authority
* Repayment
* Contract termination
* Employment discipline
* Removal from office or Board service where permitted
* Referral to counsel
* Other lawful corrective action

⸻

ARTICLE XXX — ANNUAL DISCLOSURE

Each director, officer, and other person designated by the Board shall complete an annual Conflict of Interest Disclosure Statement.

The statement shall identify relevant:

* Employment
* Businesses
* Ownership interests
* Board positions
* Family relationships
* Vendor relationships
* Compensation arrangements
* Property relationships
* Other potential conflicts

⸻

ARTICLE XXXI — PERIODIC REVIEW

The Board shall periodically evaluate whether:

* Compensation remains reasonable
* Related-party transactions remain appropriate
* Property arrangements remain fair
* Vendor arrangements remain reasonable
* Management agreements remain appropriate
* Founder relationships are properly documented
* Private benefit risks are controlled
* Transactions continue to advance exempt purposes

⸻

ARTICLE XXXII — PROFESSIONAL ADVISORS

The Board may use:

* Attorneys
* CPAs
* Appraisers
* Compensation consultants
* Property inspectors
* Brokers
* Other independent professionals

Use of an advisor does not transfer the Board’s fiduciary responsibility.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIII — CONTROL PRINCIPLE

The Tabernacle shall apply the following rules:

DISCLOSE BEFORE DECIDING

INTEREST ≠ AUTHORITY

FOUNDER ≠ CORPORATION

FAMILY ≠ EXEMPTION

CHARITABLE ASSETS ≠ PERSONAL ASSETS

RELATED PARTY ≠ PROHIBITED

But:

RELATED PARTY = ENHANCED REVIEW
