ARTICLE I — PURPOSE

The purpose of this policy is to establish responsible stewardship of all funds and financial assets belonging to The Tabernacle, Inc.

The policy is designed to:

* Protect charitable assets
* Prevent fraud and misuse
* Maintain accurate accounting
* Separate nonprofit and personal money
* Track restricted donations
* Establish spending authority
* Document transactions
* Preserve donor trust
* Support tax and regulatory compliance
* Create an auditable financial record

⸻

ARTICLE II — CORE FINANCIAL PRINCIPLE

All money received in the name of The Tabernacle belongs to:

THE TABERNACLE, INC.

It does not belong personally to:

* A founder
* Director
* Officer
* Minister
* Employee
* Volunteer
* Donor
* Program leader

Control Rule

NONPROFIT MONEY ≠ PERSONAL MONEY

Personal and charitable funds shall remain separate.

⸻

ARTICLE III — BANK ACCOUNTS

All organizational bank accounts shall:

* Be opened in the legal name of The Tabernacle, Inc.
* Use the Corporation’s EIN
* Be authorized by the Board
* Be maintained at Board-approved financial institutions
* Have documented authorized signers

No organizational money shall be routinely maintained in a founder’s personal bank account.

⸻

ARTICLE IV — AUTHORIZED SIGNERS

The Board shall designate authorized bank signers.

The initial authorized signers may include:

* President or Executive Director
* Treasurer
* One additional Board-authorized officer

At least two individuals should have lawful access to essential financial accounts to reduce continuity risk.

⸻

ARTICLE V — SEGREGATION OF DUTIES

Whenever reasonably practicable, the financial process shall separate:

AUTHORIZE

Approval of the transaction.

CUSTODY

Possession or control of money.

RECORD

Entry into the accounting system.

RECONCILE

Independent comparison of bank records against accounting records.

One person should not exercise unchecked authority over all four functions.

⸻

ARTICLE VI — ACCOUNTING SYSTEM

The Tabernacle shall maintain an organized accounting system.

The system should track:

* Income
* Donations
* Grants
* Restricted funds
* Program expenses
* Payroll
* Contractors
* Property expenses
* Food-program expenses
* Education expenses
* Prison/reentry expenses
* Ministry expenses
* Fixed assets
* Accounts payable
* Bank balances
* Credit-card balances

The Board may approve accounting software appropriate to organizational size.

⸻

ARTICLE VII — CHART OF ACCOUNTS

The Tabernacle should establish separate accounting categories for major programs.

Suggested program structure:

100 — Administration

200 — 24/7 Ministry

300 — Homelessness & Food Relief

400 — Education

500 — Prison & Reentry

600 — Property & Facilities

700 — Development & Fundraising

800 — Grants

900 — Restricted Funds

This structure may be refined with the organization’s accountant.

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — DONATIONS

Donations may be received through:

* Check
* Bank transfer
* Online giving
* Credit/debit card
* Cash
* Donor-advised funds
* Grants
* Securities
* Property
* Gifts in kind
* Other lawful methods

All donations shall be recorded appropriately.

⸻

ARTICLE IX — CASH DONATIONS

Cash presents elevated control risk.

When practical, at least two people should participate in cash collection and counting.

A cash count record should identify:

* Date
* Event or location
* Amount
* Counters
* Deposit amount
* Deposit date

Cash should be deposited promptly.

⸻

ARTICLE X — CHECK DONATIONS

Checks payable to The Tabernacle should be deposited into an organizational account.

Checks should not be endorsed over to:

* Founders
* Employees
* Volunteers
* Personal businesses

except where a legitimate and properly documented banking procedure requires otherwise.

⸻

ARTICLE XI — ONLINE DONATIONS

Online donation systems shall be established in the Corporation’s name where possible.

Administrative access shall be limited to authorized personnel.

Passwords and authentication credentials shall not be stored in public GitHub repositories or general Discord channels.

⸻

ARTICLE XII — RESTRICTED DONATIONS

A donor may lawfully restrict a gift to a particular charitable purpose.

Examples:

* Food program
* Prison education
* Property acquisition
* Homeless outreach
* Scholarships
* Building renovation

The organization must determine whether to accept the restriction.

Once accepted, restricted funds shall be tracked and used consistently with the restriction.

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — RESTRICTED FUND PRINCIPLE

DONOR RESTRICTION ≠ SUGGESTION

When The Tabernacle accepts a lawful restriction, the organization assumes an obligation to honor it.

The organization should not accept restrictions that:

* Conflict with its mission
* Are unlawful
* Create improper private benefit
* Are impossible to administer
* Give a donor improper operational control

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — DESIGNATED DONATIONS

The organization shall distinguish between:

Donor-Restricted Funds

Legally restricted by the donor.

and

Board-Designated Funds

Funds that the Board chooses to reserve for a purpose but may later redesignate according to law.

These are not the same.

⸻

ARTICLE XV — DONATION ACKNOWLEDGMENTS

The organization shall provide donation acknowledgments when required or appropriate.

Acknowledgments should accurately describe:

* Date
* Amount of cash contribution
* Description of noncash property where applicable
* Whether goods or services were provided in exchange when relevant

The organization should not provide donors with unsupported valuations of donated property.

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — GRANTS

Grant funds shall be tracked according to:

* Grant agreement
* Approved budget
* Restricted purpose
* Reporting deadlines
* Allowable costs
* Documentation requirements

Grant money shall not be shifted to unrelated purposes without lawful authorization.

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — ANNUAL BUDGET

The Board shall approve an annual operating budget.

The budget should include:

* Expected donations
* Grants
* Program spending
* Payroll
* Property expenses
* Insurance
* Food costs
* Education costs
* Prison/reentry costs
* Technology
* Professional services
* Reserves

Leadership shall periodically report actual performance against budget.

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — SPENDING AUTHORITY

The Board shall establish dollar thresholds.

Initial framework:

Level 1 — Routine Program Spending

Authorized program leaders may approve ordinary expenses within an approved budget and delegated limit.

Level 2 — Significant Expense

Requires approval from two authorized persons.

Level 3 — Major Contract or Unbudgeted Commitment

Requires Executive Director approval plus Treasurer, Finance Committee, or Board approval.

Level 4 — Real Estate, Debt, or Major Capital Commitment

Requires formal Board approval.

Specific dollar values shall be adopted after the first operating budget is approved.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — NO SELF-APPROVAL

No individual may be the sole approver of a payment to themselves.

Examples include:

* Salary changes
* Expense reimbursement
* Contractor invoices
* Founder payments
* Related-party vendor invoices
* Travel reimbursement

An independent authorized person shall approve the transaction.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — ACCOUNTS PAYABLE

Bills should be supported by appropriate documentation.

Examples:

* Invoice
* Contract
* Receipt
* Purchase order
* Expense report

Before payment, the approver should verify:

1. The goods or services were authorized.
2. The goods or services were received.
3. The amount is accurate.
4. The expense serves an organizational purpose.
5. The correct accounting category is used.

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — PURCHASE CONTROL

For significant purchases, The Tabernacle should use a process such as:

REQUEST → APPROVE → PURCHASE → RECEIVE → VERIFY → PAY → RECONCILE

No payment should be treated as sufficient proof that goods or services were actually received.

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — DEBIT AND CREDIT CARDS

Organizational cards may be issued only to authorized persons.

Each card shall have:

* Named responsible user
* Spending limit
* Permitted uses
* Receipt requirement
* Review process

Personal purchases are prohibited.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — CARD DOCUMENTATION

Every card transaction should identify:

* Date
* Merchant
* Amount
* Purpose
* Program
* Receipt

If a receipt is unavailable, the cardholder should provide written documentation explaining the expense.

Repeated undocumented charges may result in suspension of card privileges.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIV — PERSONAL PURCHASES

A personal purchase made accidentally using an organizational card must be reported promptly.

The individual must reimburse The Tabernacle promptly.

Repeated personal use may result in:

* Loss of card privileges
* Discipline
* Repayment demand
* Further Board review

⸻

ARTICLE XXV — REIMBURSEMENTS

Authorized organizational expenses paid personally may be reimbursed.

A reimbursement request should contain:

* Date
* Amount
* Vendor
* Business purpose
* Program
* Receipt
* Approver

The person seeking reimbursement shall not solely approve their own reimbursement.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — ELISE’S FOOD PROGRAM

Food-program expenditures may include:

* Ingredients
* Food supplies
* Kitchen equipment
* Packaging
* Cleaning supplies
* Food storage
* Food-service staffing
* Approved transportation costs

Purchases should be assigned to the food-program accounting category.

Standardized recipes should eventually permit calculation of:

COST PER RECIPE

and

COST PER MEAL SERVED

This will support budgeting, grant applications, donor reporting, and operational planning.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — PETTY CASH

Petty cash should be avoided when electronic or documented purchasing methods are available.

If petty cash is established:

* Board or executive approval is required
* A fixed maximum balance shall be established
* Custodian shall be identified
* Receipts are required
* Reconciliation shall occur regularly
* Personal borrowing is prohibited

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — PAYROLL

Payroll shall be processed through an appropriate payroll system or provider.

Payroll records should identify:

* Employee
* Pay rate
* Hours where applicable
* Deductions
* Taxes
* Benefits
* Gross pay
* Net pay

Compensation must be authorized before payment.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIX — CONTRACTORS

Contractor payments shall be supported by:

* Written agreement where appropriate
* Invoice
* Scope of work
* Tax information
* Approval
* Evidence of completed work

The organization shall comply with applicable tax-reporting requirements.

⸻

ARTICLE XXX — STAFFING AGENCIES

Payments to staffing agencies should be matched against:

* Contracted rates
* Approved workers or shifts
* Hours
* Invoices
* Program assignment

Security staffing and food staffing should be tracked separately when practical.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXI — PROPERTY FUNDS

Funds designated for acquisition or renovation of church properties shall be separately identifiable in the accounting system.

Property-related expenditures may include:

* Deposits
* Inspections
* Appraisals
* Legal fees
* Surveys
* Environmental review
* Title services
* Construction
* Permits
* Insurance

No property closing shall occur merely because sufficient money exists.

Board approval and the Property Acquisition Gate remain independently required.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXII — CAPITAL ASSETS

The organization shall maintain an inventory of significant assets.

Examples:

* Real property
* Vehicles
* Kitchen equipment
* Computers
* Security equipment
* Furniture
* Educational equipment
* Major appliances

Records should identify:

* Description
* Acquisition date
* Cost
* Funding source
* Location
* Responsible program
* Disposal information

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIII — BANK RECONCILIATION

Every organizational bank account shall be reconciled regularly.

Monthly reconciliation is recommended.

The reconciliation should compare:

* Bank statement
* Accounting ledger
* Outstanding checks
* Deposits in transit
* Fees
* Electronic transfers
* Unusual transactions

A person other than the primary transaction initiator should review reconciliations when feasible.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIV — CREDIT CARD RECONCILIATION

Credit-card statements shall be reconciled regularly.

Review should verify:

* Authorized cardholder
* Receipt
* Business purpose
* Coding
* Approval
* Personal-use exceptions
* Duplicate charges

⸻

ARTICLE XXXV — ELECTRONIC TRANSFERS

Electronic transfers, ACH, wire transfers, and online payments shall be subject to access controls.

For large transfers, the organization should use dual authorization where available.

Bank credentials shall not be shared casually.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVI — FINANCIAL PASSWORDS

Financial passwords and credentials shall not be:

* Posted in Discord
* Stored in public GitHub repositories
* Shared through unsecured public channels

The organization should use:

* Unique passwords
* Multifactor authentication
* Controlled password management
* Immediate access removal when personnel leave

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVII — FRAUD REPORTING

Suspected:

* Theft
* Fraud
* Embezzlement
* Diversion
* Falsified invoices
* Unauthorized payments
* Improper reimbursements

shall be reported promptly under the Whistleblower and Incident policies.

Reports involving senior leadership should be capable of reaching independent directors.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVIII — FINANCIAL REPORTING

Leadership shall provide the Board with periodic financial reports.

Reports should include, as appropriate:

* Statement of financial position
* Statement of activities
* Budget vs actual
* Cash balance
* Restricted funds
* Major liabilities
* Property obligations
* Significant grants
* Unusual transactions

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIX — RESERVES

The Board should work toward maintaining an operating reserve appropriate to organizational size and risk.

Reserve funds may protect:

* Payroll
* Utilities
* Food service
* Insurance
* Property maintenance
* Emergency operations

⸻

ARTICLE XL — BORROWING

The Corporation shall not take on major debt without proper approval.

Real estate financing, mortgages, credit lines, and significant loans require Board authorization.

⸻

ARTICLE XLI — FINANCIAL REVIEWS AND AUDITS

The Board shall determine the level of external financial review appropriate to:

* Organizational size
* State requirements
* Grant requirements
* Lender requirements
* Donor expectations
* Risk

This may include:

* CPA consultation
* Compilation
* Review
* Audit

⸻

ARTICLE XLII — TAX FILINGS

The organization shall maintain procedures to ensure timely filing of applicable:

* Federal information returns
* Payroll filings
* State filings
* Local filings
* Charitable registrations
* Contractor reporting

Required filings shall be preserved in organizational records.

⸻

ARTICLE XLIII — DOCUMENT RETENTION

Financial records shall be retained according to the organization’s Document Retention Policy.

Permanent or long-term records may include:

* Annual tax returns
* Audited financial statements
* Major property records
* Material loan agreements
* Significant grant agreements
* Board-approved budgets

⸻

ARTICLE XLIV — PROHIBITED PRACTICES

The following are prohibited:

* Using nonprofit funds as personal cash
* Undocumented loans to founders
* Paying fictitious workers
* Falsifying receipts
* Creating fake vendors
* Splitting purchases to evade approval thresholds
* Personal use of restricted funds
* Diverting donations
* Concealing related-party transactions
* Maintaining secret organizational bank accounts

⸻

ARTICLE XLVII — CONTROL PRINCIPLES

The Tabernacle shall maintain the following financial rules:

DONATION RECEIVED ≠ MONEY AVAILABLE FOR ANY PURPOSE

RESTRICTED ≠ UNRESTRICTED

AUTHORIZED ≠ PAID

PAID ≠ VERIFIED

PURCHASED ≠ RECEIVED

NONPROFIT ACCOUNT ≠ PERSONAL ACCOUNT

FOUNDER ACCESS ≠ FOUNDER OWNERSHIP

AUTHORIZE ≠ PAY ≠ RECORD ≠ RECONCILE
