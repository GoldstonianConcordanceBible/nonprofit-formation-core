ARTICLE I — NAME

The name of the Corporation is:

THE TABERNACLE, INC.

The Corporation may publicly identify itself as:

THE TABERNACLE

The Board of Directors may establish individual ministries, facilities, educational programs, outreach programs, prison and reentry programs, and other initiatives under additional program names consistent with the Corporation’s mission.

⸻

ARTICLE II — MISSION

The mission of The Tabernacle is to establish and operate Christian places of worship, refuge, education, service, restoration, and community support.

The Tabernacle seeks to serve people spiritually and practically through Christian ministry, charitable relief, education, food assistance, outreach to people experiencing homelessness, and programs serving incarcerated and formerly incarcerated people.

The organization may establish locations operating for extended hours or 24 hours per day when legally permitted, properly insured, appropriately staffed, and approved by the Board of Directors.

⸻

ARTICLE III — EXEMPT PURPOSE

The Tabernacle shall be organized and operated exclusively for religious, charitable, and educational purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code.

Its activities may include:

* Christian worship, prayer, Scripture teaching, pastoral care, discipleship, and ministry
* Relief of people experiencing homelessness, hunger, poverty, crisis, or distress
* Food preparation and distribution
* Education, lectures, courses, mentoring, technology instruction, workforce development, entrepreneurship, history, and life-skills education
* Prison, jail, and correctional ministry
* Educational and mentoring programs for incarcerated people
* Reentry support for formerly incarcerated people
* Approved music and arts programming
* Acquisition and operation of churches and other facilities
* Community outreach
* Charitable assistance
* Other activities consistent with Section 501(c)(3)

The Corporation shall not be operated primarily for the financial benefit of its founders, directors, officers, employees, donors, families, or related businesses.

⸻

ARTICLE IV — CHRISTIAN IDENTITY

The Tabernacle is a Christian religious organization.

Its religious activities may include:

* Worship
* Prayer
* Scripture
* Bible study
* Christian teaching
* Discipleship
* Pastoral care
* Chaplaincy
* Religious education
* Christian music and arts
* Community ministry
* Prison ministry
* Reentry ministry
* Missions
* Evangelism
* Service to people in need

The Board may later adopt a separate Statement of Faith and Statement of Religious Identity.

⸻

This division may provide:

* Worship
* Prayer
* Bible teaching
* Pastoral care
* Chaplaincy
* Discipleship
* Spiritual mentorship
* Community ministry
* Safe refuge

Facilities may remain accessible 24 hours per day where lawful and operationally appropriate.

Division II — Homelessness and Food Relief

This division may provide:

* Prepared meals
* Food distribution
* Clothing
* Hygiene supplies
* Resource navigation
* Housing referrals
* Employment referrals
* Transportation support
* Identification assistance
* Community referrals
* Temporary refuge where legally authorized

Division III — Education

This division may provide:

* Lectures
* Courses

* Digital education

* Business education
* Workforce education
* History
* Public humanities
* Financial literacy

* Life-skills instruction

⸻

ARTICLE VI — MEMBERSHIP

The Tabernacle shall initially have no statutory voting members.

Participation in any of the following does not automatically create corporate membership or voting rights:

* Worship
* Ministry
* Classes
* Discord
* Volunteering
* Employment
* Donations
* Charitable services
* Homelessness programs
* Prison programs
* Reentry programs
* Online communities

Governance authority is vested in the Board of Directors.

⸻

ARTICLE VII — BOARD OF DIRECTORS

The affairs of The Tabernacle shall be governed under the direction of its Board of Directors.

The Board is responsible for:

* Protecting the mission
* Protecting charitable assets
* Financial oversight
* Executive oversight
* Property decisions
* Legal compliance
* Program oversight
* Safeguarding
* Risk management
* Major contracts
* Long-term strategy

The initial target Board size shall be at least five directors, subject to applicable state law.

The organization should maintain a meaningful number of independent directors who are not financially dependent upon the founders or founder-controlled businesses.

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — FOUNDERS

The founders may serve the Corporation as:

* Directors
* Officers
* Ministers
* Employees
* Executives
* Advisors
* Volunteers

Founder status itself does not create:

* Ownership of the Corporation
* Ownership of charitable property
* Lifetime Board membership
* Additional votes
* Unilateral control
* Automatic compensation
* A right to charitable assets

FOUNDER ≠ CORPORATION

Authority exists through lawful organizational roles.

⸻

ARTICLE IX — DIRECTOR VOTING

Each director shall have one vote.

A founder receives the same single Board vote as any other director.

No person receives additional votes because that person is:

* A founder
* An officer
* A minister
* A major donor
* An employee
* A property donor

Unless a higher threshold is required by law or these Bylaws, Board actions may be approved by a majority vote where a quorum exists.

⸻

ARTICLE X — BOARD MEETINGS

The Board shall meet at least quarterly.

An annual governance meeting shall include review of:

* Mission
* Programs
* Budget
* Financial statements
* Executive compensation
* Conflicts of interest
* Insurance
* Property
* Safety
* Safeguarding
* Compliance
* Strategic priorities

Remote meetings may be conducted when permitted by applicable law.

⸻

ARTICLE XI — OFFICERS

The Corporation shall have at least:

President or Chair

Secretary

Treasurer

The Board may additionally appoint:

* Vice Chair
* Executive Director
* Chief Executive Officer
* Chief Operating Officer
* Ministry Director
* Education Director
* Prison and Reentry Director
* Food Program Director
* Facilities Director
* Safety and Security Director

⸻

ARTICLE XII — PRESIDENT / CHAIR

The President or Chair shall:

* Support Board governance
* Preside over meetings
* Help establish strategic direction
* Ensure Board decisions are implemented
* Protect the mission
* Coordinate with executive leadership

The President or Chair does not personally own or control nonprofit assets.

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — SECRETARY

The Secretary shall oversee:

* Board minutes
* Resolutions
* Corporate records
* Notices
* Governance documentation
* Director records
* Document control

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — TREASURER

The Treasurer shall provide Board-level oversight over:

* Accounting
* Financial statements
* Budgets
* Banking controls
* Restricted funds
* Donations
* Tax filings
* Financial reporting
* Audits or reviews
* Financial risk

No person should simultaneously exercise unchecked control over authorization, custody of money, accounting, and reconciliation.

⸻

ARTICLE XV — EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR

The Board may appoint an Executive Director or Chief Executive Officer.

The Executive Director shall manage day-to-day operations subject to:

* Board oversight
* Approved budget
* Policies
* Delegated authority
* Applicable law

The Executive Director may supervise:

* Ministry
* Food programs
* Education
* Prison/reentry programs
* Facilities
* Security
* Employees
* Contractors
* Volunteers

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — COMPENSATION

Founders, officers, directors, or other related persons may receive reasonable compensation for actual services rendered where legally permissible.

Compensation must be:

* Reasonable
* Supported by appropriate comparability information
* Approved by disinterested decision-makers
* Documented contemporaneously
* Consistent with charitable purposes

No person may approve their own compensation.

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

Directors, officers, executives, and key employees must disclose material financial interests that could affect organizational decisions.

An interested person shall not improperly participate in approval of a transaction benefiting that person.

Board minutes shall document:

* The disclosed interest
* Relevant facts
* Recusal when appropriate
* Alternatives considered
* The decision
* The vote

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — PROPERTY

The Tabernacle may:

* Purchase churches
* Receive donated churches
* Lease churches
* Acquire other buildings
* Renovate property
* Restore facilities
* Establish ministry centers
* Establish classrooms
* Establish kitchens
* Establish community centers
* Establish reentry centers
* Establish lawful refuge facilities

No real-property purchase shall occur without Board authorization.

Before acquisition, the organization shall conduct appropriate:

* Title review
* Zoning review
* Use review
* Building review
* Fire review
* Environmental review
* Insurance review
* Financial analysis
* Renovation analysis

PROPERTY OWNED ≠ PROPERTY PERMITTED

A church building does not automatically have legal authorization for overnight shelter, residential use, commercial food preparation, or every proposed 24/7 activity.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — FOOD PROGRAM

The Corporation may operate charitable food programs.

Food programs may include standardized recipes and meal procedures approved by organizational leadership.

Operations shall comply with applicable food-safety and licensing requirements.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — PRISON AND REENTRY PROGRAMS

Correctional programs must comply with rules imposed by the relevant:

* Federal agency
* State agency
* Local correctional authority
* Prison
* Jail
* Detention facility

Employees and volunteers shall complete required:

* Applications
* Background screening
* Orientation
* Training
* Security procedures
* Program approval

No representative of The Tabernacle may bypass correctional security rules.

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — SAFEGUARDING

The Corporation shall maintain policies protecting:

* Children
* Vulnerable adults
* People experiencing homelessness
* Incarcerated people
* Formerly incarcerated people
* Other vulnerable beneficiaries

Abuse, exploitation, harassment, coercion, and retaliation are prohibited.

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — FINANCIAL CONTROL

The Corporation shall maintain accurate financial records.

Charitable funds shall be used only for lawful organizational purposes.

Personal funds and nonprofit funds shall remain separate.

Restricted donations shall be used according to lawful donor restrictions.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — COMMITTEES

The Board may establish committees including:

* Finance and Audit
* Governance
* Property and Facilities
* Ministry
* Education
* Homelessness and Food Relief
* Prison and Reentry
* Safety and Security
* Development and Grants

Committees remain subject to Board authority.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIV — POLITICAL CAMPAIGN ACTIVITY

The Corporation shall not participate or intervene in a political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to a candidate for public office.

Legislative activities shall remain within limits applicable to organizations exempt under Section 501(c)(3).

⸻

ARTICLE XXV — RECORDS

The Corporation shall maintain appropriate:

* Corporate records
* Board minutes
* Financial records
* Tax records
* Property records
* Policies
* Contracts
* Government filings
* Insurance records
* Program records

Sensitive beneficiary and personnel information shall be protected from inappropriate public disclosure.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — INDEMNIFICATION

To the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, the Corporation may indemnify directors, officers, employees, and authorized agents who act in good faith within the proper scope of their organizational duties.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — AMENDMENTS

These Bylaws may be amended by the Board of Directors according to applicable law.

Material amendments shall be:

1. Proposed
2. Reviewed
3. Approved through proper Board action
4. Recorded in Board minutes
5. Version-controlled
6. Preserved in the corporate record

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLE

The Tabernacle shall operate according to the following control principles:

MISSION BEFORE PROPERTY

SERVICE BEFORE SCALE

AUTHORITY BEFORE ACTION

SAFETY BEFORE ACCESS

PROPERTY OWNED ≠ PROPERTY PERMITTED

CAN ≠ MAY

FOUNDER ≠ CORPORATION

The Corporation exists to preserve its Christian mission while responsibly stewarding charitable resources for the people and communities it serves.
