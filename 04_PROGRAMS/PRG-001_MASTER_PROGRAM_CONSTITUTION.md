ARTICLE I — PURPOSE

This document establishes the program-governance system for The Tabernacle, Inc.

Every charitable, religious, educational, humanitarian, prison/reentry, or community program operated under The Tabernacle shall exist within a controlled program structure.

The purpose is to ensure that programs are:

* Mission-aligned
* Legally authorized
* Properly funded
* Safely operated
* Measurable
* Documented
* Governed
* Reviewable
* Consistent with Section 501(c)(3)

⸻

ARTICLE II — CORE RULE

IDEA ≠ PROGRAM

An idea becomes a program only after it is:

1. Defined
2. Reviewed
3. Assigned an owner
4. Given a budget
5. Given controls
6. Authorized
7. Launched
8. Measured

No employee, volunteer, founder, donor, minister, contractor, or program participant may independently create a binding program obligation on behalf of The Tabernacle without proper authority.

ARTICLE V — PROGRAM STATUS

Every program shall have one status.

CONCEPT

Idea under consideration.

DESIGN

Program is being developed.

PILOT APPROVED

Program may operate on a limited test basis.

ACTIVE

Program is formally operating.

PAUSED

Program temporarily suspended.

UNDER REVIEW

RETIRED

Program has ended.

⸻

ARTICLE VI — AUTHORIZATION LEVELS

Board Approval Required

Board approval is required for:

* New major program divisions
* Programs involving overnight shelter
* Programs involving residential occupancy
* Major government-funded programs
* Programs involving material debt
* Programs requiring major capital expenditure
* Programs carrying unusual legal or safeguarding risk
* Programs involving substantial related-party transactions

Executive Approval

Executive leadership may approve ordinary programs within:

* Board-approved mission
* Approved budget
* Existing policies
* Delegated authority

Program Leader Authority

Program leaders may manage authorized activities within the approved charter.

They may not materially expand the program beyond approved scope without further authorization.

ARTICLE VII — CHARITABLE CLASS

Every charitable-assistance program shall identify the class of persons it is intended to serve.

Examples:

* People experiencing homelessness
* People experiencing food insecurity
* Individuals in economic distress
* Incarcerated persons
* Formerly incarcerated persons
* Job seekers
* Students
* Members of the public seeking education
* Community members seeking ministry

Beneficiary selection shall not be structured primarily to benefit insiders or a preselected private group.

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria should be:

* Related to the charitable purpose
* Reasonably objective where practical
* Applied consistently
* Documented where necessary

The organization shall avoid arbitrary favoritism.

⸻

ARTICLE IX — RELIGIOUS PARTICIPATION

The Tabernacle is a Christian organization and may openly conduct religious programming.

Where required by applicable law, grant terms, contracts, or correctional policy, religious participation shall be voluntary.

Receipt of emergency humanitarian assistance shall not be used improperly to coerce religious profession.

⸻

ARTICLE X — PROGRAM BUDGET

Each material program should have a budget.

The budget should identify:

* Personnel
* Contractors
* Food
* Supplies
* Equipment
* Facility costs
* Transportation
* Technology
* Insurance
* Training
* Professional services
* Other costs

Program spending shall be coded to the appropriate accounting category.

⸻

ARTICLE XI — FUNDING SOURCE

Each program charter shall identify likely funding sources.

Examples:

* Unrestricted donations
* Restricted donations
* Foundation grants
* Corporate grants
* Church donations
* Government grants
* Gifts in kind
* Program fees where appropriate

Restricted funding shall be tracked according to the Financial Controls Policy.

⸻

ARTICLE XII — NO UNAUTHORIZED FUNDRAISING

A program leader may not independently create a public fundraising campaign in the name of The Tabernacle unless authorized.

Fundraising communications must accurately describe:

* The program
* Intended use of funds
* Whether donations are restricted
* The organization’s tax status

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — STAFFING

A program may use:

* Employees
* Contractors
* Staffing-agency personnel
* Volunteers
* Ministers
* Educators
* Community partners

The staffing model shall comply with applicable employment, licensing, training, safeguarding, and insurance requirements.

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — VOLUNTEERS

Volunteers may be required to complete:

* Application
* Orientation
* Background screening
* Safeguarding training
* Food-safety training
* Security training
* Correctional volunteer clearance
* Role-specific training

depending on the program.

Volunteer status does not authorize unrestricted access to beneficiaries, facilities, money, or confidential information.

⸻

ARTICLE XV — SAFEGUARDING

Every program serving vulnerable populations shall identify safeguarding controls.

Potential controls include:

* Background checks
* Two-adult practices
* Supervision
* Restricted one-on-one contact
* Transportation rules
* Incident reporting
* Mandatory reporting
* Confidentiality
* Volunteer boundaries
* Removal-from-duty procedures

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — DATA MINIMIZATION

Programs shall collect only information reasonably necessary for:

* Service delivery
* Eligibility
* Safety
* Reporting
* Grants
* Outcome measurement
* Legal compliance

Do not collect sensitive personal information merely because software permits it.

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — CONFIDENTIALITY

Beneficiary information shall be protected according to:

* Organizational policy
* Applicable privacy law
* Grant requirements
* Correctional rules
* Professional obligations

Public GitHub and general Discord channels shall not function as beneficiary case-management systems.

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — PROGRAM OUTCOMES

Programs should measure outcomes proportionate to their complexity.

Examples:

Ministry

* Services held
* People participating
* Counseling/mentoring sessions
* Volunteer hours

Food Relief

* Meals served
* Households served
* Cost per meal
* Food waste
* Referral connections

Education

* Learners enrolled
* Courses completed
* Lessons delivered
* Assessments
* Certificates
* Participant feedback

Prison/Reentry

* Participants served
* Sessions delivered
* Education completed
* Mentoring matches
* Reentry referrals
* Post-release connections

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — OUTPUT ≠ OUTCOME

The Tabernacle shall distinguish between:

OUTPUT

What the organization did.

Example:

5,000 meals served

and

OUTCOME

What changed for beneficiaries.

Example:

Participants connected with ongoing food or housing resources

Both may be measured.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — COST METRICS

Programs should develop cost measures where useful.

Examples:

COST PER MEAL

COST PER LEARNER

COST PER CLASS

COST PER MENTORING SESSION

COST PER REENTRY PARTICIPANT

Financial efficiency shall not override safety, dignity, or quality.

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — PILOT PROGRAMS

New programs may begin as pilots.

A pilot charter should specify:

* Pilot period
* Maximum participants
* Maximum budget
* Location
* Evaluation criteria
* Stop conditions

Pilot status does not waive legal or safety requirements.

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — PROGRAM REVIEW

Each active program should be reviewed periodically.

Review questions include:

* Does it further the mission?
* Does it serve an appropriate charitable class?
* Is it financially sustainable?
* Is it safe?
* Is it compliant?
* Are participants benefiting?
* Are there safeguarding concerns?
* Does staffing remain appropriate?
* Should the program expand, change, pause, or end?

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — STOP AUTHORITY

Leadership may immediately pause a program when necessary to address:

* Immediate safety threats
* Abuse allegations
* Licensing failures
* Insurance lapses
* Facility hazards
* Financial misconduct
* Correctional authorization problems
* Serious legal risks

Material suspensions shall be reported to the Board.

ARTICLE XXVI — EDUCATIONAL CONTENT

Educational content shall be used only when The Tabernacle has lawful authority to use it.

Possible authority includes:

* Ownership
* Written permission
* License
* Public-domain status
* Authorized linking or embedding
* Other lawful use

Public visibility alone does not prove ownership.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — CORRECTIONAL PROGRAMS

Programs inside correctional institutions require institution-specific approval.

The charter must identify:

* Facility
* Agency
* Approved curriculum
* Volunteer requirements
* Security rules
* Approved materials
* Communication rules
* Program schedule
* Institution contact

No program charter can override correctional rules.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — HOMELESSNESS PROGRAMS

Programs serving people experiencing homelessness shall emphasize:

* Dignity
* Safety
* Practical assistance
* Clear boundaries
* Appropriate referrals
* Non-exploitation
* Privacy

Program participants shall not be treated as marketing assets.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIX — FOOD PROGRAMS

Food programs shall include controls for:

* Food sourcing
* Storage
* Allergens
* Preparation
* Temperature
* Sanitation
* Portion control
* Waste
* Volunteer training
* Applicable permits

ARTICLE XXX — 24/7 PROGRAMS

No program may operate 24 hours per day until leadership confirms:

* Staffing
* Safety
* Security
* Emergency response
* Insurance
* Facility approval
* Fire/life safety
* Sanitation
* Financial sustainability

ARTICLE XXXI — PROPERTY-DEPENDENT PROGRAMS

Any program requiring use of a particular property must identify:

* Required occupancy
* Required zoning
* Required permits
* Accessibility
* Fire/life-safety needs
* Utilities
* Security
* Insurance
* Operating cost

Program approval does not substitute for property-use approval.

ARTICLE XXXVI — PROGRAM CLOSURE

When a program ends:

1. Stop new obligations.
2. Notify affected partners.
3. Resolve restricted funds appropriately.
4. Secure or dispose of records properly.
5. Return borrowed property.
6. Close contracts.
7. Prepare final financial report.
8. Prepare final impact report where appropriate.
9. Mark program RETIRED.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVII — NONPROFIT PURPOSE TEST

Every program must answer:

WHO BENEFITS?

WHAT DO WE DO?

WHY IS IT CHARITABLE, RELIGIOUS, OR EDUCATIONAL?

WHO CONTROLS IT?

WHO PAYS FOR IT?

WHO GETS PAID?

WHAT PRIVATE BENEFIT EXISTS?

HOW IS THAT PRIVATE BENEFIT CONTROLLED?
