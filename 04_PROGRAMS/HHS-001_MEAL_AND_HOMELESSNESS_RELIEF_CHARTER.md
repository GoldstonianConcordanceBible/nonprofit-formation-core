ARTICLE I — PROGRAM NAME

The official program name is:

HHS-001 — The Tabernacle Meal & Homelessness Relief Program

The Board may later approve a public-facing ministry or program name without changing the underlying controlled program ID.

⸻

ARTICLE II — PURPOSE

The purpose of HHS-001 is to relieve hunger, homelessness, poverty, food insecurity, and related forms of distress through direct charitable assistance.

The program shall treat every person served with dignity, respect, safety, and compassion.

⸻

ARTICLE III — CHARITABLE CLASS

The program is intended primarily to benefit persons experiencing:

* Homelessness
* Housing instability
* Food insecurity
* Poverty
* Unemployment
* Reentry hardship
* Displacement
* Family crisis
* Emergency financial distress
* Other circumstances creating legitimate charitable need

The program may also serve other individuals experiencing temporary food or basic-needs insecurity when resources permit.

⸻

ARTICLE V — MEAL SYSTEM

The Tabernacle shall develop a standardized meal-production system based on controlled recipes.

Recipes may originate from Elise or other approved contributors.

Once adopted for organizational use, each recipe should receive a unique identifier.

Example:

FOOD-RCP-001

Each standardized recipe should identify:

* Recipe name
* Version
* Number of servings
* Ingredients
* Ingredient quantities
* Approved substitutions
* Major allergen information
* Required equipment
* Preparation procedure
* Cooking procedure
* Holding procedure
* Portion size
* Storage instructions
* Disposal instructions
* Estimated cost
* Cost per serving

ARTICLE VII — ELISE’S ROLE

Elise may serve in a legitimate organizational role related to the food program, subject to Board approval and applicable conflict/compensation policies.

Possible roles include:

* Food Program Director
* Culinary Program Director
* Recipe Development Lead
* Kitchen Operations Lead
* Volunteer Training Lead

If compensated, her duties and compensation shall be documented and reviewed under GOV-003 — Compensation and Related-Party Transactions Policy.

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — NO PRIVATE OWNERSHIP OF CHARITABLE FOOD

Food purchased or donated to The Tabernacle for charitable distribution belongs to The Tabernacle and shall be used for organizational purposes.

It shall not be diverted for substantial personal use by:

* Founders
* Directors
* Employees
* Contractors
* Volunteers
* Donors

except as part of an authorized employee meal policy or bona fide charitable assistance.

⸻

Food may be obtained through:

* Retail purchases
* Wholesale purchases
* Food banks
* Grocery partnerships
* Restaurants
* Farms
* Community donations
* Corporate donations
* Government programs
* Other lawful sources

The organization shall reject food that appears:

* Spoiled
* Contaminated
* Unsafe
* Improperly stored
* Outside acceptable use parameters

ARTICLE XI — FOOD INVENTORY

The program should maintain reasonable inventory controls for significant food supplies.

Inventory categories may include:

* Dry goods
* Refrigerated goods
* Frozen goods
* Produce
* Beverages
* Disposable service items
* Cleaning supplies

Inventory practices should minimize:

* Spoilage
* Theft
* Waste
* Over-ordering
* Unsafe storage

⸻

ARTICLE XII — FOOD SAFETY

All food preparation shall comply with applicable:

* State requirements
* Local health requirements
* Food-service regulations
* Facility rules
* Permit requirements

Food safety procedures shall address, as applicable:

* Handwashing
* Cross-contamination
* Cooking temperatures
* Cooling
* Reheating
* Hot holding
* Cold holding
* Storage
* Cleaning
* Sanitizing
* Pest control
* Waste disposal

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — ALLERGENS

The program shall establish procedures addressing major food allergens.

When practical, meal information should identify known major allergens contained in standardized recipes.

Staff and volunteers shall not make unsupported claims that food is completely allergen-free.

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — KITCHEN AUTHORIZATION

No person shall independently operate the program kitchen merely because they are:

* A founder
* Volunteer
* Donor
* Guest
* Minister
* Employee

Kitchen access may be restricted to:

* Authorized staff
* Trained volunteers
* Approved staffing personnel
* Contractors
* Inspectors
* Other approved persons

⸻

ARTICLE XV — WORKERS

The program may use:

* Employees
* Staffing-agency personnel
* Contractors
* Volunteers

Roles may include:

* Food Program Director
* Kitchen Supervisor
* Cook
* Prep Worker
* Food Service Worker
* Dishwasher
* Sanitation Worker
* Distribution Worker
* Volunteer Coordinator

Worker classification and food-handler requirements shall comply with applicable law.

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — TEMPORARY STAFFING

Temporary staffing services may be used for meal preparation or service when appropriate.

Each staffing arrangement should identify:

* Duties
* Rates
* Supervision
* Worker classification
* Insurance
* Food-safety requirements
* Background requirements where applicable

Temporary status does not remove training or safety obligations.

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — VOLUNTEERS

Volunteers shall receive appropriate orientation before working.

Training may include:

* Mission
* Guest dignity
* Food safety
* Hygiene
* Allergens
* Kitchen safety
* De-escalation
* Confidentiality
* Incident reporting
* Safeguarding

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — SERVICE MODEL

Meals may be distributed through:

* Sit-down meal service
* Takeaway meals
* Outreach distribution
* Community events
* Mobile distribution
* Partner locations
* Emergency response

Each model shall have an approved operating procedure.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — ELIGIBILITY

The program should minimize unnecessary administrative barriers for basic food assistance.

A person should not ordinarily be required to provide extensive documentation merely to receive a meal.

Enhanced programs involving:

* Financial assistance
* Temporary lodging
* Transportation
* Case management
* Other limited resources

may require additional eligibility procedures.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — DIGNITY

Program participants shall be treated as people, not inventory, content, or marketing assets.

Staff and volunteers shall not:

* Humiliate guests
* Photograph guests without appropriate authorization
* Force testimonials
* Publicly disclose private circumstances
* Exploit participants for labor
* Use food as leverage for personal favors

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — RELIGIOUS IDENTITY

The Tabernacle is explicitly Christian.

The organization may:

* Pray
* Hold worship
* Provide Scripture
* Offer pastoral care
* Offer Bible study
* Invite guests to religious programs

Humanitarian service shall be administered according to applicable law, grant requirements, contracts, and adopted organizational policy.

Where required, religious participation shall be voluntary.

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — SECURITY

Food distribution locations shall maintain appropriate security and de-escalation procedures.

Security shall focus on:

* Protecting people
* Maintaining safe access
* Preventing violence
* De-escalating disputes
* Protecting charitable assets
* Supporting emergency response

Security shall not treat homelessness itself as misconduct.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — INCIDENTS

Examples requiring incident documentation may include:

* Injury
* Medical emergency
* Violence
* Threat
* Food contamination
* Suspected foodborne illness
* Fire
* Theft
* Abuse allegation
* Safeguarding concern
* Security event

Detailed incident records shall be stored securely rather than in public GitHub repositories.

⸻

ARTICLE XXV — REFERRALS

The Tabernacle should build a referral directory covering services such as:

* Emergency shelter
* Permanent housing
* Medical care
* Mental-health care
* Substance-use treatment
* Employment
* Veterans services
* Domestic violence services
* Transportation
* Legal aid
* Identification services
* Benefits assistance

The organization should avoid guaranteeing services controlled by another provider.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — TEMPORARY REFUGE

Food assistance does not automatically create shelter authorization.

Any temporary refuge or overnight program requires separate approval addressing:

* Zoning
* Occupancy
* Fire code
* Building code
* Sleeping arrangements
* Sanitation
* Showers
* Security
* Staffing
* Insurance
* Accessibility
* Local shelter requirements

MEAL PROGRAM ≠ SHELTER PROGRAM

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — DATA COLLECTION

For ordinary meal distribution, the program should collect only information genuinely needed.

Possible aggregate information:

* Number of meals
* Number of service encounters
* Date
* Location
* Meal type

Where individual information is unnecessary, anonymous or aggregate reporting may be preferable.

ARTICLE XXVIII — PROTECTED INFORMATION

* Public GitHub
* Public Discord
* Public social media
* Public dashboards

Sensitive records shall use an approved secure system.

ARTICLE XXX — COST PER MEAL

The primary unit-cost metric shall be:

TOTAL ALLOCABLE MEAL PROGRAM COST ÷ MEALS SERVED

A narrower food-only metric may also be maintained:

FOOD INGREDIENT COST ÷ MEALS SERVED

These numbers shall not be confused.

ARTICLE XXXII — WASTE CONTROL

Food waste should be measured where practical.

Categories may include:

* Preparation waste
* Spoilage
* Overproduction
* Returned meals
* Unsafe food disposal

The purpose is continuous improvement rather than reducing portions below reasonable standards.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIII — DONATED FOOD

Donated food shall be evaluated for:

* Condition
* Packaging
* Storage history where relevant
* Safety
* Applicability to program needs

The program is not required to accept every donation offered.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIV — CASH ASSISTANCE

HHS-001 shall not automatically distribute unrestricted cash.

Any direct financial-assistance program shall have separate controls addressing:

* Eligibility
* Approval
* Maximum assistance
* Documentation
* Fraud controls
* Conflict controls

⸻

ARTICLE XXXV — EMPLOYMENT AND LABOR

Program participants shall not be required to perform unpaid labor as a condition of receiving basic charitable food assistance.

Beneficiaries may voluntarily participate in lawful volunteer programs where appropriate and genuinely voluntary.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVI — CHILDREN AND FAMILIES

When children participate in programs, safeguarding procedures shall apply.

Food distribution involving minors should consider:

* Allergens
* Custody/supervision
* Photography
* Volunteer boundaries
* Mandatory-reporting requirements

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVII — PROPERTY

Each HHS-001 operating location must be approved for the actual activities conducted there.

A church kitchen shall not automatically be assumed to satisfy every requirement for large-scale food preparation.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVIII — INSURANCE

Before substantial operation, leadership shall review appropriate coverage for:

* General liability
* Food-related liability
* Workers’ compensation
* Volunteer activities
* Property
* Vehicles where applicable
* Other program risks
