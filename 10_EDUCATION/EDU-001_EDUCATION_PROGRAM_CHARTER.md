ARTICLE I — PROGRAM NAME

The official controlled program name is:

EDU-001 — The Tabernacle Education Program

The Board may approve public-facing educational initiatives, course brands, lecture series, academies, institutes, or other program names underneath EDU-001.

Use of such a name does not independently establish:

* Accreditation
* Degree-granting authority
* State authorization
* College or university status
* Professional licensure authority

⸻

ARTICLE II — PURPOSE

The purpose of EDU-001 is to advance education by providing instruction, training, lectures, courses, mentoring, and educational resources intended to improve individual capabilities and provide information beneficial to individuals and communities.

The program may provide both:

* Religious education

and

* Secular educational programming consistent with The Tabernacle’s charitable mission.

⸻

ARTICLE III — EDUCATIONAL MISSION

The Tabernacle intends to make useful education accessible to people who may otherwise face barriers to instruction.

* Adults seeking new skills
* Young people where appropriate safeguarding exists
* Incarcerated persons through approved programs
* Formerly incarcerated persons
* Faith communities
* Entrepreneurs
* Workers adapting to technological change
* Other charitable or educational populations

ARTICLE VI — EXISTING LECTURES

The founders and participating educators have previously delivered lectures and educational presentations at universities and other institutions.

Some recordings are presently available through publicly accessible online channels or websites.

The Tabernacle may create an educational index or learning pathway around appropriate existing lectures.

Before the organization:

* Downloads
* Reuploads
* Edits
* Republishes
* Monetizes
* Redistributes
* Incorporates a recording into a formal course package

the organization shall determine whether it has sufficient rights for that use.

⸻

ARTICLE VII — YOUTUBE AND ONLINE VIDEO

A video being publicly accessible on YouTube or another public platform does not automatically establish that The Tabernacle owns:

* The video recording
* Lecture footage
* Slides
* Photographs
* Music
* Graphics
* University branding
* Venue footage
* Third-party media contained in the presentation

Where rights are uncertain, preferred approaches include:

1. Linking to the authorized original source.
2. Embedding the authorized original video where permitted.
3. Creating original study guides referencing the lecture.
4. Obtaining written permission before rehosting or materially editing.

* Explain
* Demonstrate

* Create
* Apply

ARTICLE XVII — ACCREDITATION

The Tabernacle shall distinguish clearly between:

EDUCATION

Providing lawful instruction.

and

ACCREDITATION

Formal recognition by an authorized accrediting body or other applicable authority.

The Tabernacle shall not state or imply that it is accredited unless it has actually obtained the accreditation being represented.

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — SCHOOL, COLLEGE, OR UNIVERSITY STATUS

The Tabernacle may operate educational programs without representing itself as:

* A college
* University
* Accredited school
* Degree-granting institution

unless the applicable legal requirements have been satisfied.

Public-facing terminology shall be reviewed before using language that could reasonably imply formal higher-education status.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — DEGREES

The Tabernacle shall not award or advertise:

* Associate degrees
* Bachelor’s degrees
* Master’s degrees
* Doctoral degrees
* Academic credit

unless it has obtained every authorization required to do so.

ARTICLE XXIV — EDUCATIONAL RECORD PRIVACY

ARTICLE XXV — TUITION AND FEES

The Tabernacle may offer:

* Free programs
* Scholarship-supported programs
* Grant-funded programs
* Reasonably priced educational programs

If fees are charged, the organization shall evaluate whether the program continues to further the charitable and educational mission.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — CHARITABLE ACCESS

Where practical, fee-based educational programs may incorporate:

* Scholarships
* Fee waivers
* Sponsored seats
* Reduced-cost access
* Free community versions

The exact structure shall depend upon funding and program design.

ARTICLE XXVII — COMMERCIAL ACTIVITY

Education shall not be structured primarily as a mechanism for enriching:

* Founders
* Private educational companies
* Content owners
* Vendors
* Related businesses

Any private benefit must remain incidental to the exempt educational activity.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — EDUCATIONAL MARKETING

Marketing shall accurately state:

* Who operates the program
* What is taught
* Who teaches it
* Program cost
* Completion requirements
* Credential provided
* Accreditation status

Marketing shall not promise:

* Employment
* Income
* Promotion
* College credit
* Professional licensure

unless such outcomes are actually guaranteed by an authorized party.

ARTICLE XXIX — CORRECTIONAL EDUCATION

EDU-001 may deliver educational programming inside correctional institutions through the REENTRY division.

* Curriculum approval
* Instructor access
* Devices
* Internet access
* Books
* Videos
* Music
* Communication
* Scheduling
* Student records

Educational program approval does not grant independent correctional access.

ARTICLE XXXII — SOURCE QUALITY

Educational content should distinguish among:

* Primary sources
* Secondary sources
* Scholarly analysis
* News
* Opinion
* Religious interpretation
* Personal experience

Where factual claims are presented as educational instruction, instructors should make reasonable efforts to use reliable supporting information.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIII — RELIGIOUS EDUCATION

Because The Tabernacle is Christian, religious education may expressly teach Christian doctrine and interpretation.

Religious instruction should be identified as religious teaching rather than misrepresented as neutral academic consensus where material distinctions exist.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIV — HISTORY PROGRAMS

History instruction should make reasonable efforts to distinguish:

* Documented facts
* Primary-source evidence
* Historical interpretation
* Oral history
* Family tradition
* Personal reflection

Contested historical interpretations should be presented with appropriate context.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXV — ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE EDUCATION

AI education may include:

* AI literacy
* Workplace applications
* Prompting
* Automation
* Responsible use
* Limitations
* Verification
* Data privacy
* Human oversight

Students should be taught that AI-generated output may contain errors and requires appropriate human review.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVI — INSTRUCTOR USE OF AI

Instructors may use AI tools in educational development when consistent with:

* Privacy
* Intellectual-property requirements
* Course quality
* Organizational policy
* Human oversight
