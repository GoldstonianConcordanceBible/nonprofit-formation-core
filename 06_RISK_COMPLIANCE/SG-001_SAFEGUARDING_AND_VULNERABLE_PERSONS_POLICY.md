ARTICLE I — PURPOSE

The Tabernacle, Inc. is committed to protecting people who participate in, receive services from, work for, volunteer with, or otherwise interact with the organization.

This policy establishes safeguards against:

* Abuse
* Sexual abuse
* Sexual exploitation
* Physical abuse
* Emotional abuse
* Neglect
* Harassment
* Grooming
* Financial exploitation
* Spiritual coercion
* Retaliation
* Human trafficking
* Inappropriate relationships
* Misuse of authority

⸻

ARTICLE II — ZERO-TOLERANCE PRINCIPLE

The Tabernacle has zero tolerance for abuse or exploitation by:

* Directors
* Officers
* Founders
* Ministers
* Employees
* Contractors
* Staffing personnel
* Volunteers
* Mentors
* Teachers
* Security personnel
* Vendors
* Program partners

Religious title, founder status, seniority, generosity, or reputation shall not exempt anyone from safeguarding requirements.

⸻

ARTICLE III — PEOPLE COVERED

Enhanced safeguarding may apply when serving:

* Children
* Youth
* Vulnerable adults
* People experiencing homelessness
* People with disabilities
* People in crisis
* Incarcerated persons
* Formerly incarcerated persons
* People experiencing domestic violence
* People experiencing severe economic distress
* Persons dependent on The Tabernacle for basic assistance

⸻

ARTICLE IV — POWER IMBALANCE

Personnel shall recognize that providing:

* Food
* Shelter
* Ministry
* Employment help
* Education
* Reentry assistance
* Transportation
* Financial assistance
* Pastoral counseling

can create a substantial power imbalance.

That imbalance shall never be used to obtain:

* Sex
* Romance
* Money
* Labor
* Gifts
* Personal loyalty
* Business opportunities
* Public endorsements
* Private favors

⸻

ARTICLE V — CHILD SAFEGUARDING

Programs involving minors require enhanced safeguards.

Depending on the program, controls may include:

* Parent/guardian authorization
* Background screening
* Appropriate supervision
* Controlled check-in/check-out
* Transportation authorization
* Volunteer training
* Restricted one-on-one contact
* Photography consent
* Incident procedures
* Mandatory-reporting procedures

⸻

ARTICLE VI — TWO-ADULT PRACTICE

Where practical, programs involving minors should avoid situations in which one unrelated adult is isolated with one child.

Preferred environments include:

* Multiple adults present
* Observable spaces
* Open doors
* Windows
* Public or semi-public program areas
* Documented exceptions

This rule may be adapted for legitimate pastoral, educational, medical, or emergency situations with appropriate safeguards.

⸻

ARTICLE VII — ONE-ON-ONE MINISTRY

One-on-one ministry with adults may be appropriate.

However, when a participant is particularly vulnerable, personnel should use:

* Observable spaces
* Appropriate boundaries
* Reasonable duration
* Professional communication
* Supervisor awareness where appropriate

Pastoral confidentiality shall not be used to conceal abuse or override mandatory-reporting obligations.

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — SEXUAL BOUNDARIES

Personnel shall not use a ministry or service relationship to initiate or pursue sexual activity with a person whose dependency, vulnerability, incarceration, shelter status, or program relationship creates an inappropriate power imbalance.

Prohibited conduct includes:

* Sexual coercion
* Sexual favors in exchange for services
* Sexual harassment
* Grooming
* Sexualized threats
* Explicit messages unrelated to legitimate services
* Exploitation of dependency

⸻

ARTICLE IX — ROMANTIC RELATIONSHIPS

Romantic relationships involving employees, volunteers, mentors, ministers, or other personnel and active beneficiaries may present serious power and safeguarding concerns.

Personnel shall disclose potential conflicts to appropriate leadership before pursuing a relationship with a person currently receiving services where a material power imbalance exists.

Correctional-facility rules independently govern relationships with incarcerated persons.

⸻

ARTICLE X — INCARCERATED PERSONS

Correctional participants shall be treated according to the rules of the institution and REENTRY-001.

The Tabernacle prohibits:

* Sexual exploitation
* Unauthorized personal relationships
* Unauthorized money exchanges
* Contraband
* Secret communication channels
* Personal business solicitation
* Unauthorized gifts

Correctional volunteers must comply with the facility’s own training and supervision requirements. Federal PREA standards apply across covered confinement settings, and correctional systems impose additional requirements designed to prevent sexual abuse and misconduct.

⸻

ARTICLE XI — PEOPLE EXPERIENCING HOMELESSNESS

A person’s need for:

* Food
* Clothing
* Shelter
* Hygiene
* Transportation
* Employment assistance

shall never be exploited.

Personnel shall not demand:

* Sexual favors
* Personal labor
* Money
* Religious conversion
* Public testimonials
* Social-media participation
* Private favors

as an improper condition of emergency charitable assistance.

⸻

ARTICLE XII — SPIRITUAL ABUSE

The Tabernacle shall not use spiritual authority to intimidate, exploit, silence, or control vulnerable persons.

Examples include improperly claiming that:

* God requires a person to give money to an individual worker
* Receiving food requires conversion
* Reporting abuse is sinful
* A leader cannot be questioned because of religious title
* A participant must provide personal services to receive God’s favor

Christian teaching may be offered openly without using vulnerability as coercive leverage.

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — FINANCIAL EXPLOITATION

Personnel shall not:

* Borrow money from vulnerable beneficiaries
* Solicit personal gifts
* Control beneficiary bank accounts without lawful authority
* Use beneficiary credit/debit cards
* Pressure beneficiaries into investments
* Sell personal products to captive participants
* Redirect charitable assistance for personal benefit

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — LABOR EXPLOITATION

Receiving charitable assistance shall not ordinarily require unpaid labor for an individual’s private benefit.

Participants may voluntarily participate in legitimate volunteer programs when the arrangement is lawful and genuinely voluntary.

⸻

ARTICLE XV — BACKGROUND SCREENING

The Tabernacle may require background screening for roles involving:

* Children
* Vulnerable adults
* Overnight operations
* Transportation
* Money
* Correctional programs
* Unsupervised beneficiary contact
* Security
* Other sensitive responsibilities

Screening requirements shall reflect:

* Applicable law
* Role
* Risk
* Facility requirements
* Insurance requirements

A background check alone does not make someone safe or qualified.

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — SCREENING PRINCIPLE

BACKGROUND CHECK ≠ SAFEGUARDING SYSTEM

Safeguarding also requires:

* Training
* Supervision
* Boundaries
* Reporting
* Accountability
* Appropriate staffing
* Culture

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — RECRUITMENT

Sensitive roles should use reasonable screening that may include:

* Application
* Identity verification
* Interview
* References
* Background screening
* Credential verification
* Role-specific training

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — TRANSPORTATION

Personnel transporting beneficiaries shall comply with approved transportation policy.

Controls may include:

* Valid driver’s license
* Insurance
* Approved vehicle
* Seatbelts
* Child-restraint requirements
* Driver screening
* Parent/guardian authorization for minors
* Trip documentation

Unauthorized private transportation of incarcerated persons is prohibited.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — PRIVATE HOMES

Service delivery in a staff member’s or volunteer’s private residence requires specific authorization when the arrangement creates heightened safeguarding risk.

The Tabernacle’s charitable programs should generally operate through approved facilities and systems.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — OVERNIGHT OPERATIONS

Any approved overnight site shall maintain enhanced safeguarding procedures including:

* Minimum staffing
* Controlled access
* Sleeping-area supervision
* Privacy rules
* Bathroom/shower safeguards
* Incident escalation
* Security coverage
* Separation arrangements where appropriate

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — RESTROOMS, SHOWERS, AND CHANGING AREAS

Personnel shall respect heightened privacy in:

* Restrooms
* Showers
* Changing areas
* Sleeping areas

Cameras shall not be placed in areas where users reasonably expect bodily privacy.

Staff entry into such areas shall occur only for legitimate safety, cleaning, maintenance, assistance, or emergency purposes.

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — PHOTOGRAPHY AND MEDIA

A vulnerable person’s participation in a charitable program does not automatically authorize:

* Photography
* Video
* Livestreaming
* Interviews
* Testimonials
* Publication of personal circumstances

Appropriate consent and organizational approval shall be obtained.

Additional safeguards apply to minors and correctional participants.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — DIGITAL COMMUNICATION

Personnel shall maintain professional boundaries in:

* Text messages
* Email
* Discord
* Social media
* Direct messages
* Video calls
* Other communication platforms

Programs involving minors should establish approved communications procedures.

Secret or disappearing-message channels shall not be used to conceal inappropriate contact.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIV — MANDATORY REPORTING

The Tabernacle shall comply with all applicable mandatory-reporting laws.

Because reporting duties vary by jurisdiction and role, each operating state shall maintain a state-specific reporting appendix identifying:

* Who is a mandatory reporter
* What must be reported
* Reporting threshold
* Reporting agency
* Hotline/contact information
* Reporting deadline
* Written follow-up requirements
* Internal notification process

An internal report to The Tabernacle does not automatically satisfy a person’s legal reporting duty.

ARTICLE XXVI — REPORTING CHILD ABUSE

Suspected child abuse or neglect shall be handled according to the law of the applicable jurisdiction.

Personnel shall not delay a legally required report merely to:

* Conduct an internal investigation
* Protect the organization’s reputation
* Consult the accused
* Obtain founder approval
* Obtain Board approval

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — VULNERABLE-ADULT REPORTING

The Tabernacle shall identify applicable adult-protective-services reporting laws in each jurisdiction where it operates.

Suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a vulnerable adult shall be escalated according to applicable law and organizational policy.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — INTERNAL REPORTING

Safeguarding concerns may be reported to:

* Program supervisor
* Safeguarding Officer
* Executive Director
* Independent director
* Board Chair

The reporting structure shall always include a route that bypasses the accused person’s authority.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIX — ALLEGATIONS AGAINST SENIOR LEADERSHIP

If an allegation concerns:

* Founder
* President
* Executive Director
* Board Chair
* Senior minister
* Senior officer

the matter shall be capable of being reported directly to independent Board leadership or outside authorities.

The accused person shall not direct the investigation.

⸻

ARTICLE XXX — IMMEDIATE REMOVAL FROM DUTY

The Tabernacle may immediately remove or suspend a person from duties involving beneficiaries when credible safeguarding concerns require protective action.

Temporary removal is a protective measure.

It is not necessarily a final determination of wrongdoing.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXI — NO RETALIATION

Retaliation against a good-faith reporter is prohibited.

Prohibited retaliation may include:

* Firing
* Demotion
* Threats
* Harassment
* Loss of volunteer role
* Intimidation
* Social punishment
* Withholding legitimate services

where undertaken because the person made or assisted with a good-faith report.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXII — INVESTIGATION

The Tabernacle may investigate internal policy violations when appropriate.

However, internal investigation shall not:

* Interfere with law enforcement
* Delay mandatory reporting
* Intimidate witnesses
* Destroy evidence
* Pressure a victim to recant

Outside professionals may be engaged when appropriate.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIII — PRESERVATION OF EVIDENCE

When serious misconduct is alleged:

* Preserve relevant messages
* Preserve videos
* Preserve access records
* Preserve incident reports
* Preserve relevant employment/volunteer records
* Do not alter records

Evidence shall be secured with appropriate confidentiality.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIV — CONFIDENTIALITY

Information about safeguarding allegations shall be shared only with persons who legitimately need it for:

* Safety
* Investigation
* Legal compliance
* Insurance
* Governance
* Required reporting

The Tabernacle shall not promise absolute secrecy where disclosure is legally or operationally necessary.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXV — SUPPORT FOR AFFECTED PERSONS

Where appropriate and feasible, The Tabernacle may provide or arrange:

* Pastoral support
* Crisis referrals
* Advocacy resources
* Medical referrals
* Mental-health referrals
* Law-enforcement information
* Protective-services information

The organization shall not pressure a person to reconcile with an alleged abuser.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVI — TRAINING

Relevant personnel shall receive training appropriate to their role.

Training may include:

* Recognizing abuse
* Grooming behaviors
* Mandatory reporting
* Sexual boundaries
* One-on-one interactions
* Transportation
* Digital communication
* Human trafficking awareness
* De-escalation
* Incident reporting
* Correctional boundaries

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVII — ANNUAL REFRESHER

High-risk roles should receive periodic refresher training.

The organization should document:

* Person trained
* Training
* Date
* Instructor
* Completion

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVIII — PARTNER ORGANIZATIONS

Partners serving vulnerable populations at Tabernacle facilities may be required to demonstrate appropriate:

* Insurance
* Safeguarding policies
* Screening
* Credentials
* Training

A partnership agreement shall not reduce The Tabernacle’s obligation to address known safety risks within its control.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIX — SECURITY PERSONNEL

Security personnel must comply with safeguarding policy.

Security authority does not permit:

* Abuse
* Harassment
* Punitive humiliation
* Sexual exploitation
* Unnecessary force

Any use-of-force authority shall be defined separately according to applicable law and professional security standards.

⸻

ARTICLE XL — DISCIPLINARY ACTION

Confirmed policy violations may result in:

* Coaching
* Retraining
* Warning
* Restricted duties
* Suspension
* Removal from volunteer service
* Termination
* Contract termination
* Board removal procedures
* Reporting to authorities
* Reporting to licensing bodies

depending upon circumstances and applicable law.

⸻

ARTICLE XLI — REHIRING / RETURN TO SERVICE

A person removed because of a serious safeguarding violation shall not automatically return to service after time passes.

Any return requires documented review and shall comply with:

* Law
* Insurance
* Facility requirements
* Board policy
* Risk assessment

⸻

ARTICLE XLII — SAFEGUARDING OFFICER

The Board should designate a:

SAFEGUARDING OFFICER

or equivalent responsible leader.

Responsibilities may include:

* Training
* Policy implementation
* Report intake
* Record control
* Background-screening coordination
* Compliance review
* Board reporting

The Safeguarding Officer shall not independently suppress reports involving senior leadership.

⸻

ARTICLE XLIII — BOARD OVERSIGHT

The Board should receive periodic non-identifying safeguarding reports covering:

* Number of incidents
* Categories of concern
* Training completion
* Screening completion
* Policy gaps
* Corrective actions

Personally identifiable information shall be limited to legitimate governance need.

ARTICLE XLV — PROGRAM INTEGRATION

This policy applies across:

MIN-001 — 24/7 Christian Ministry

HHS-001 — Meal & Homelessness Relief

EDU-001 — Education

REENTRY-001 — Prison & Reentry

Each program may adopt stricter safeguards.

⸻

ARTICLE XLVI — CONTROL PRINCIPLES

The Tabernacle adopts the following safeguarding rules:

TITLE ≠ TRUST

BACKGROUND CHECK ≠ SAFETY

MINISTRY ≠ UNLIMITED ACCESS

CONFIDENTIALITY ≠ SECRECY ABOUT ABUSE

INTERNAL REPORT ≠ LEGALLY REQUIRED EXTERNAL REPORT

CHARITY ≠ LEVERAGE

VULNERABILITY ≠ CONSENT TO EXPLOITATION

FOUNDER ≠ EXEMPT

REMOVE ACCESS FIRST WHEN NECESSARY TO PROTECT PEOPLE

PROTECT THE PEOPLE
