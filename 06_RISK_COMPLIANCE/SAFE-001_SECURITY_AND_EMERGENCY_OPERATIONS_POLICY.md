SECURITY & EMERGENCY OPERATIONS POLICY

Version: 0.1

⸻

ARTICLE I — PURPOSE

The purpose of this policy is to establish a safe operating environment for:

* Guests
* People experiencing homelessness
* Children
* Families
* Employees
* Volunteers
* Ministers
* Educators
* Reentry participants
* Contractors
* Visitors

Security exists to protect people and preserve access to The Tabernacle’s charitable and religious programs.

⸻

ARTICLE II — SECURITY MISSION

The security mission is:

PROTECT PEOPLE

PREVENT HARM

DE-ESCALATE CONFLICT

CALL APPROPRIATE HELP

PRESERVE DIGNITY

DOCUMENT MATERIAL INCIDENTS

Security shall not be used to punish poverty, homelessness, mental distress, appearance, or inability to donate.

⸻

ARTICLE III — CORE PRINCIPLE

SECURITY ≠ PUNISHMENT

AUTHORITY ≠ UNLIMITED FORCE

HOMELESSNESS ≠ MISCONDUCT

MINISTRY ≠ UNCONTROLLED ACCESS

STAFF PRESENCE ≠ SECURITY READINESS

CAMERA ≠ SAFETY SYSTEM

⸻

ARTICLE IV — APPLICABILITY

This policy applies to:

* All Tabernacle facilities
* 24/7 sites
* Food-distribution events
* Educational activities
* Community outreach
* Property operations
* Special events
* Approved transportation activities
* Other organizational operations

Correctional institutions remain governed primarily by the institution’s own security procedures while Tabernacle personnel are inside those facilities.

⸻

ARTICLE V — SECURITY RESPONSIBILITY

The Board shall designate operational responsibility for security.

Roles may include:

* Safety & Security Director
* Site Director
* Shift Lead
* Licensed security personnel
* Contract security provider
* Emergency coordinator

Every open site shall have a clear chain of responsibility.

⸻

ARTICLE VI — SECURITY PERSONNEL

The Tabernacle may use:

* Direct employees
* Licensed security contractors
* Qualified security companies
* Temporary personnel where legally permitted

Before deployment, leadership shall determine which:

* Licenses
* Registrations
* Certifications
* Training
* Background checks
* Insurance

are required for the actual security duties being performed.

⸻

ARTICLE VII — TEMPORARY STAFFING

A general staffing worker shall not automatically be assigned professional security duties.

If a staffing agency supplies security personnel, The Tabernacle shall verify:

* Legal authorization
* Worker qualifications
* Provider licensing where required
* Insurance
* Scope of work
* Supervision
* Training

TEMP WORKER ≠ LICENSED SECURITY OFFICER

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — STAFF SECURITY RESPONSIBILITIES

Non-security employees and volunteers may:

* Observe
* Report
* Follow emergency procedures
* Request assistance
* Support evacuation
* Maintain calm
* Follow Shift Lead directions

They shall not be expected to perform specialized security functions for which they are not trained or authorized.

⸻

ARTICLE IX — DE-ESCALATION

When reasonably safe and appropriate, personnel should prioritize de-escalation.

This may include:

* Calm communication
* Clear boundaries
* Giving physical space
* Reducing unnecessary confrontation
* Requesting supervisory assistance
* Moving uninvolved people away
* Contacting appropriate emergency professionals

Personnel should avoid escalating disagreements merely to establish authority.

⸻

ARTICLE X — IMMEDIATE THREATS

When an immediate threat to life or serious bodily safety exists:

PROTECT IMMEDIATE LIFE SAFETY

↓

CONTACT 911 OR APPROPRIATE EMERGENCY SERVICE

↓

FOLLOW SITE EMERGENCY PROCEDURE

↓

EVACUATE / SHELTER AS APPROPRIATE

↓

NOTIFY LEADERSHIP

↓

DOCUMENT AFTER IMMEDIATE DANGER IS CONTROLLED

Life safety takes priority over routine documentation.

⸻

ARTICLE XI — USE OF FORCE

The Tabernacle does not authorize unnecessary or punitive force.

Any security professional’s use of force shall comply with:

* Applicable law
* Licensing requirements
* Employer policy
* Contract requirements
* Training
* Immediate safety circumstances

Physical intervention shall never be used merely because a person:

* Refuses prayer
* Is homeless
* Is verbally disagreeable
* Cannot donate
* Criticizes leadership

⸻

ARTICLE XII — WEAPONS

Each site shall maintain a written weapons policy consistent with applicable law.

The policy shall address:

* Employees
* Volunteers
* Security personnel
* Contractors
* Guests
* Law-enforcement officers

No employee or volunteer is authorized to carry a weapon on behalf of The Tabernacle merely because they personally possess a lawful permit.

Armed security, if ever used, requires separate Board/executive authorization, insurance review, professional-provider review, and applicable licensing compliance.

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — PROHIBITED ITEMS

A site may prohibit or restrict items presenting material safety risks according to applicable law and adopted site rules.

Staff shall not conduct intrusive searches except where lawful, appropriately authorized, and consistent with organizational policy.

Professional or law-enforcement assistance should be used where circumstances warrant.

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — ACCESS CONTROL

Facilities may distinguish among:

PUBLIC AREAS

Areas ordinarily available to guests.

PROGRAM AREAS

Areas available to participants in particular programs.

STAFF AREAS

Employee and volunteer workspaces.

RESTRICTED AREAS

Areas involving:

* Cash
* Records
* IT systems
* Kitchens
* Equipment
* Building systems
* Sensitive records
* Security equipment

Access shall correspond to legitimate organizational need.

⸻

ARTICLE XV — KEYS AND CREDENTIALS

The organization shall control:

* Physical keys
* Access cards
* Alarm credentials
* Door codes
* Electronic credentials

Maintain an access register identifying:

Person:
Role:
Site:
Credential:
Access Level:
Issued:
Returned/Disabled:
Approved By:

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — LOST CREDENTIALS

Lost or compromised:

* Keys
* Cards
* Codes
* Credentials

shall be reported promptly.

Where risk warrants, access shall be:

* Disabled
* Reissued
* Rekeyed
* Changed

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — VISITORS

Visitors entering non-public areas may be subject to:

* Sign-in
* Identification
* Escort
* Temporary credentials
* Access restrictions

depending upon risk.

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — HOUSE RULE ENFORCEMENT

Staff may intervene when conduct materially threatens:

* Safety
* Property
* Program integrity
* Other guests’ access

Examples include:

* Violence
* Credible threats
* Harassment
* Sexual misconduct
* Drug distribution
* Theft
* Intentional property destruction
* Serious disruption

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — EXCLUSION FROM PROPERTY

Removal or temporary exclusion may be appropriate when a person’s conduct creates a material safety or operational threat.

Where circumstances permit, decisions should identify:

* Conduct
* Duration
* Conditions for return
* Decision-maker
* Appeal/review route where appropriate

Exclusions shall not be based solely on a person’s poverty or housing status.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — TRESPASS ACTION

Where a person refuses to leave after lawful removal and circumstances warrant, authorized leadership may involve law enforcement.

Personnel shall not make false or exaggerated reports to obtain removal.

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — CHILD SAFETY

Security operations involving children shall coordinate with SG-001.

Security personnel shall understand procedures for:

* Missing child
* Unauthorized pickup
* Suspected abuse
* Custody disputes
* Immediate threats

Security staff shall not independently resolve complex custody disputes without appropriate authority.

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — VULNERABLE ADULTS

Security personnel shall recognize that a disruptive presentation may arise from:

* Medical emergency
* Disability
* Mental-health crisis
* Trauma
* Cognitive impairment
* Substance-use crisis

Where appropriate, the response should emphasize emergency or crisis assistance rather than punishment.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — MEDICAL EMERGENCY

For suspected medical emergencies:

CALL APPROPRIATE EMERGENCY SERVICES

↓

PROVIDE FIRST AID ONLY WITHIN TRAINING

↓

CLEAR ACCESS FOR RESPONDERS

↓

PROTECT PRIVACY

↓

DOCUMENT MATERIAL FACTS

Staff shall not diagnose beyond their professional qualifications.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIV — FIRE

Each facility shall maintain a site-specific fire and evacuation plan addressing:

* Alarms
* Exits
* Assembly points
* Occupancy
* Emergency contacts
* Staff responsibilities
* Assistance for persons requiring help

Exits shall remain unobstructed.

⸻

ARTICLE XXV — EVACUATION

Evacuation authority may be exercised when there is:

* Fire
* Gas leak
* Structural concern
* Bomb threat
* Hazardous condition
* Other substantial danger

Site personnel shall follow emergency officials’ instructions.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — SHELTER-IN-PLACE

Some emergencies may make evacuation less safe.

Each site should develop appropriate procedures for events requiring temporary shelter-in-place based on local emergency guidance and facility risks.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — SEVERE WEATHER

Sites shall establish procedures for reasonably foreseeable local weather hazards.

The procedure may address:

* Monitoring
* Safe areas
* Temporary closure
* Guest communication
* Staffing
* Utility loss
* Emergency supplies

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — UTILITY FAILURE

Procedures shall address loss of:

* Electricity
* Water
* Heat
* Cooling
* Sewer
* Internet/telephone where operationally critical

Leadership shall determine when loss of a utility makes continued operation unsafe.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIX — NIGHT OPERATIONS

Overnight and late-night operations require enhanced attention to:

* Staffing
* Controlled entrances
* Exterior lighting
* Shift communication
* Facility walkthroughs
* Emergency communication
* Security coverage

No site shall remain open solely to maintain the appearance of 24/7 operations when minimum safety coverage is unavailable.

⸻

ARTICLE XXX — MINIMUM NIGHT STAFFING

Each site shall establish a written minimum staffing level based on:

* Building size
* Guest volume
* Program activity
* Security conditions
* Overnight sleeping status
* Insurance requirements

The exact number shall be site-specific.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXI — NO SOLO HIGH-RISK SHIFT

A lone volunteer shall not be assigned sole responsibility for a high-risk 24/7 site.

If minimum staffing cannot be maintained, leadership may:

* Reduce services
* Restrict building areas
* Suspend admission
* Close temporarily

⸻

ARTICLE XXXII — CAMERAS

Security-camera use shall comply with applicable law and privacy requirements.

Cameras may support:

* Entry monitoring
* Exterior security
* Common-area safety
* Incident review

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIII — CAMERA-PROHIBITED AREAS

Cameras shall not ordinarily be located in areas of heightened bodily privacy such as:

* Toilets
* Shower areas
* Changing areas

Any exceptional surveillance practice requires legal and policy review.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIV — CAMERA ACCESS

Video access shall be limited to authorized personnel.

The organization shall establish:

* Retention period
* Export authority
* Access logs where practical
* Law-enforcement request procedures
* Legal hold procedures

Video connected to an incident shall be preserved when required.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXV — AUDIO RECORDING

Audio recording may trigger additional legal requirements.

The Tabernacle shall not assume that audio may automatically be recorded merely because video surveillance is permitted.

Site-specific legal review shall determine allowable practices.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVI — SECURITY LOG

A shift security log may record:

Site:
Date:
Shift:
Security Personnel:
Opening Status:
Door/Access Issues:
Non-confidential Safety Observations:
Facility Checks:
Emergency Service Contacts:
Incident Reference Numbers:
Handoff Notes:

Detailed sensitive information belongs in the secure incident system.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVII — INCIDENT CLASSIFICATION

Incidents may include:

* Medical
* Fire
* Injury
* Threat
* Violence
* Theft
* Property damage
* Missing person
* Safeguarding
* Food safety
* Utility failure
* Security breach
* Other material event

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVIII — INCIDENT NUMBERING

Material incidents should receive a controlled identifier.

Example:

IR-2026-001

Discord may contain:

IR-2026-001 opened — leadership notified

The full report shall remain in restricted storage.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIX — INCIDENT REPORT CONTENT

A secure incident report should document:

* Date
* Time
* Location
* Reporter
* Objective facts
* Persons involved
* Witnesses
* Immediate actions
* Emergency services
* Injuries if known
* Property damage
* Video/evidence preserved
* Notifications
* Follow-up

Avoid speculation and unsupported conclusions.

⸻

ARTICLE XL — INCIDENT REVIEW

Material incidents should be reviewed for:

* Immediate corrective action
* Policy gap
* Training need
* Facility defect
* Staffing issue
* Insurance notice
* Safeguarding implications
* Legal reporting
* Recurrence prevention

⸻

ARTICLE XLI — SERIOUS INCIDENT ESCALATION

Immediately escalate events involving:

* Death
* Serious injury
* Fire
* Weapon
* Significant violence
* Child safety
* Sexual assault
* Significant abuse
* Major property loss
* Significant data/security breach
* Law-enforcement action
* Media interest involving a serious event

to appropriate executive and Board leadership.

⸻

ARTICLE XLII — LAW ENFORCEMENT

The Tabernacle may contact law enforcement when reasonably necessary for:

* Immediate danger
* Violence
* Credible threats
* Serious theft
* Weapon incidents
* Trespass after lawful removal
* Other serious circumstances

The organization shall not use law enforcement merely as a substitute for ordinary customer-service or ministry conflict resolution.

⸻

ARTICLE XLIII — EVIDENCE

Potential evidence may include:

* Video
* Photographs
* Access records
* Messages
* Incident reports
* Physical items

Personnel shall preserve relevant evidence appropriately.

Do not alter evidence to improve appearances.

⸻

ARTICLE XLIV — MEDIA DURING INCIDENTS

Only authorized representatives shall provide official organizational statements concerning serious incidents.

Staff and volunteers shall not post:

* Victim images
* Graphic images
* Rumors
* Sensitive security information
* Unverified allegations

on personal or organizational social media.

⸻

ARTICLE XLV — CONFIDENTIALITY

Security information shall be classified according to sensitivity.

Do not publicly disclose information that would materially reveal:

* Security vulnerabilities
* Access codes
* Alarm details
* Camera blind spots
* Restricted-area controls
* Emergency vulnerabilities

⸻

ARTICLE XLVI — EMERGENCY CONTACT DIRECTORY

Every site shall maintain current contact information for:

* 911
* Fire
* EMS
* Police
* Site Director
* Shift Lead
* Safety & Security Director
* Safeguarding Officer
* Property/facilities contact
* Insurance contact
* Utility emergency contacts

Relevant contacts shall be accessible even during internet outages.

⸻

ARTICLE XLVII — EMERGENCY SUPPLIES

Sites should maintain supplies appropriate to their risks and programs.

Examples may include:

* First-aid supplies
* Flashlights
* Emergency communication capability
* Basic emergency water
* Other supplies identified by site planning

Specialized equipment shall be used only by appropriately trained personnel.

⸻

ARTICLE XLVIII — FIRST AID / CPR

The Tabernacle should seek appropriate first-aid and CPR/AED training coverage among operating staff.

Training records should identify:

* Person
* Credential/course
* Completion
* Expiration

⸻

ARTICLE XLIX — AED

Where an AED is maintained, the organization shall establish procedures for:

* Placement
* Inspection
* Maintenance
* Training
* Applicable legal requirements

⸻

ARTICLE L — DRILLS

Sites may conduct appropriate drills for:

* Fire
* Evacuation
* Severe weather
* Other significant site risks

Drills shall be conducted in a manner that does not create unnecessary panic or trauma.

⸻

ARTICLE LI — EMERGENCY COMMAND

During an emergency, authority shall be clear.

A working structure is:

INCIDENT LEAD

↓

SITE DIRECTOR / SHIFT LEAD

↓

EMERGENCY SERVICES TAKE COMMAND WHERE APPLICABLE

↓

EXECUTIVE NOTIFICATION

↓

BOARD NOTIFICATION FOR MATERIAL EVENTS

Organizational personnel shall not interfere with lawful emergency command.

⸻

ARTICLE LII — BUSINESS CONTINUITY

After an incident, leadership shall determine:

* Whether site remains open
* Whether programs continue
* Whether repairs are necessary
* Whether alternate service location is needed
* Whether insurer must be notified
* Whether authorities must approve reopening

⸻

ARTICLE LIII — REOPENING GATE

After a serious closure:

HAZARD CONTROLLED

↓

FACILITY SAFE

↓

REQUIRED AUTHORITIES CLEARED

↓

INSURANCE ISSUES ADDRESSED

↓

STAFFING RESTORED

↓

LEADERSHIP AUTHORIZATION

↓

REOPEN

⸻

ARTICLE LIV — CONTRACT SECURITY

Any material security contract should address:

* Scope
* Hours
* Personnel qualifications
* Licensing
* Insurance
* Supervision
* Reporting
* Confidentiality
* Use-of-force policy
* Incident coordination
* Termination

Related-party security arrangements trigger GOV-002 and GOV-003.

⸻

ARTICLE LV — SECURITY METRICS

Leadership may monitor:

* Security incidents
* Medical emergencies
* Police/EMS calls
* Property losses
* Exclusions
* Safety complaints
* Training completion
* Shift coverage
* Facility safety issues

Metrics shall be used for prevention and improvement, not to portray vulnerable populations as inherently dangerous.

⸻

Before 24/7 activation, each site should document:

Emergency Exits:
Restricted Areas:
Lighting Review:
Camera Review:
Key/Access System:
Night Staffing:

Sensitive technical details should remain restricted.

⸻

ARTICLE LVII — STATE-SPECIFIC APPENDIX

Because private-security regulation varies, every operating jurisdiction shall receive a legal/compliance appendix addressing:

* Security-company licensing
* Individual guard licensing
* Armed-security requirements
* Weapons law
* Training
* Background screening
* Uniform/badge restrictions
* Use-of-force requirements
* Required insurance
* Recordkeeping

⸻

ARTICLE LVIII — TRAINING

Relevant personnel should receive training appropriate to their responsibilities.

Possible training includes:

* Emergency procedures
* De-escalation
* Safeguarding
* First aid/CPR
* Fire evacuation
* Incident reporting
* Access control
* Privacy
* Role boundaries

Professional security personnel shall receive any additional training required by law or employer policy.

⸻

ARTICLE LIX — POLICY VIOLATIONS

Violations may result in:

* Retraining
* Restriction
* Suspension
* Removal from site
* Employment discipline
* Volunteer dismissal
* Contract termination
* Reporting to authorities or licensing bodies where appropriate

⸻

ARTICLE LX — CONTROL PRINCIPLES

The Tabernacle adopts:

SECURITY EXISTS TO PROTECT PEOPLE

SECURITY ≠ PUNISHMENT

BADGE ≠ UNLIMITED AUTHORITY

PERMIT ≠ ORGANIZATIONAL AUTHORIZATION

CAMERA ≠ SAFETY

INCIDENT ≠ SOCIAL MEDIA CONTENT

IMMEDIATE SAFETY BEFORE PAPERWORK

DE-ESCALATE WHEN SAFE

CALL PROFESSIONAL HELP WHEN REQUIRED

MINIMUM STAFFING MEANS MINIMUM STAFFING

24/7 ≠ OPEN AT ANY COST

PROTECT THE PEOPLE
