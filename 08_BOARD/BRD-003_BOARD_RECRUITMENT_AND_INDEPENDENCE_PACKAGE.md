PART I — INITIAL BOARD DESIGN

1. RECOMMENDED INITIAL SIZE

Working target:

5 VOTING DIRECTORS

Recommended structure:

SEAT 1 — FOUNDER / MISSION

Founder or principal organizational visionary.

SEAT 2 — FINANCE / TREASURY

Independent director with experience in:

* Accounting
* Finance
* Banking
* Nonprofit finance
* Audit
* Financial controls

SEAT 3 — LEGAL / GOVERNANCE

Independent director with experience in:

* Law
* Governance
* Compliance
* Risk
* Corporate administration

Attorney status is helpful but not required.

SEAT 4 — PROGRAMS / SAFEGUARDING

Independent director with experience in:

* Human services
* Homelessness
* Food relief
* Education
* Corrections/reentry
* Safeguarding
* Social services

SEAT 5 — PROPERTY / COMMUNITY

Independent director with experience in:

* Real estate
* Facilities
* Construction
* Church administration
* Community development
* Local partnerships

⸻

2. WHY FIVE

Five directors provides enough people to:

* Distribute responsibilities
* Create committees
* Maintain quorum when someone recuses
* Review founder compensation independently
* Review family transactions independently
* Review property transactions
* Avoid concentrating all corporate authority in one or two people

while remaining small enough to operate efficiently during formation.

⸻

3. INDEPENDENCE TARGET

The Tabernacle should seek:

AT LEAST A MAJORITY OF VOTING DIRECTORS WHO ARE INDEPENDENT

For the recommended five-person Board:

Preferred structure:

* 1 founder/insider
* 4 independent directors

This is stronger than merely having three independent directors.

⸻

4. INTERNAL INDEPENDENCE STANDARD

For internal governance purposes, a director should generally be considered independent when the director:

* Is not currently employed by The Tabernacle
* Does not receive material compensation from The Tabernacle
* Is not an immediate family member of a compensated insider
* Does not own a material vendor serving The Tabernacle
* Does not receive material economic benefit from a significant Tabernacle transaction
* Can exercise judgment independent of the founder and management

The organization’s internal definition does not replace the separate independence definition required when completing Form 990.

⸻

5. FORM 990 INDEPENDENCE

Every year, the organization shall separately determine director independence according to the then-current Form 990 instructions.

INTERNAL INDEPENDENT ≠ AUTOMATIC FORM 990 INDEPENDENT

The annual questionnaire shall support this review.

⸻

6. FOUNDER ROLE

The founder may serve as:

* Director
* Board Chair
* Officer
* Executive Director
* Minister
* Employee

subject to the Bylaws and applicable law.

However:

FOUNDER ≠ OWNER

FOUNDER ≠ PERMANENT DIRECTOR

FOUNDER ≠ EXTRA VOTE

FOUNDER ≠ SOLE GOVERNANCE AUTHORITY

⸻

7. FOUNDER COMPENSATION

If the founder receives compensation:

The founder shall not vote on their own compensation.

The process should use:

DISCLOSURE

↓

RECUSAL

↓

COMPARABILITY DATA

↓

DISINTERESTED REVIEW

↓

BOARD DECISION

↓

CONTEMPORANEOUS MINUTES

The IRS specifically warns that excessive compensation to a person exercising substantial influence can create private-benefit and excess-benefit concerns. (IRS)

⸻

8. FAMILY MEMBERS

Family members may support The Tabernacle.

They may potentially serve as:

* Employees
* Volunteers
* Program leaders
* Directors

when appropriate.

However, the Board should not be controlled by a founder’s family.

FAMILY SUPPORT ≠ FAMILY CONTROL

⸻

9. FAMILY MEMBER ON BOARD

If a founder-related person becomes a voting director, that relationship shall be disclosed.

The Board shall maintain enough unrelated, independent directors so that:

* Related persons do not constitute a voting majority
* Compensation decisions can be made independently
* Related-party transactions can be reviewed independently
* Meaningful recusals do not destroy governance functionality

⸻

10. ELISE

If Elise serves operationally within HHS-001 or another program and is compensated, she does not need a Board seat to have meaningful organizational authority within her assigned program.

If she later becomes a director and also has a family or financial relationship with the founder, the relationship shall be disclosed and independence analyzed accordingly.

⸻

11. WHEN TO EXPAND TO SEVEN

The Board should consider expanding from five to:

7 DIRECTORS

when:

* A second founder-related person joins the Board
* Multiple directors become compensated
* Multiple substantial related-party relationships exist
* The organization acquires significant real estate
* Government grants become material
* 24/7 shelter operations begin
* The organization operates multiple sites
* Board workload becomes excessive

A possible seven-person model:

* Founder / Mission
* Finance
* Governance / Legal
* Programs / Human Services
* Property / Facilities
* Community / Development
* Safeguarding / Corrections / Education

⸻

12. NO AUTOMATIC BOARD SEATS

The following do not automatically receive Board seats:

* Major donors
* Family members
* Employees
* Ministers
* Property donors
* Vendors
* Contractors
* Program partners

Board selection is based on governance need and fiduciary capacity.

⸻

13. BOARD DUTY

Every director shall act for:

THE TABERNACLE, INC.

not merely for:

* Founder
* Donor
* Employer
* Church
* Family
* Vendor
* Personal project
* Outside organization

⸻

14. FIDUCIARY RESPONSIBILITIES

Directors are expected to exercise appropriate:

DUTY OF CARE

Be informed and participate responsibly.

DUTY OF LOYALTY

Put The Tabernacle’s interests ahead of conflicting personal interests.

DUTY OF OBEDIENCE

Keep the organization aligned with:

* Mission
* Governing documents
* Tax-exempt purposes
* Applicable law

⸻

15. DIRECTOR EXPECTATIONS

Every director should normally:

* Attend Board meetings
* Read materials before meetings
* Vote independently
* Disclose conflicts
* Protect confidentiality
* Review financial information
* Support compliance
* Participate in strategic planning
* Protect vulnerable persons
* Preserve charitable assets
* Participate in annual governance review

⸻

16. BOARD MEETING EXPECTATION

The Bylaws currently contemplate at least quarterly Board meetings.

A formation-stage Board may meet more frequently.

Recommended during first year:

MONTHLY DURING FORMATION / ACQUISITION

then transition to:

AT LEAST QUARTERLY

once systems stabilize.

⸻

17. DIRECTOR TERM

The final term structure shall follow the adopted Bylaws and state law.

A recommended working model is:

3-YEAR TERMS

with staggered terms as the Board matures.

No director shall have an automatic lifetime appointment.

⸻

18. FIRST-BOARD STAGGERING

If permitted by state law and Bylaws, the initial Board may eventually stagger seats so that not all directors rotate simultaneously.

Example:

* Two initial terms ending after Year 1
* Two after Year 2
* One after Year 3

Subsequent elected terms may then be three years.

State-specific legal review is required before final adoption.

⸻

19. BOARD OFFICERS

Initial officers should include:

* President / Chair
* Secretary
* Treasurer

These offices may be held by directors if permitted by the Bylaws and state law.

⸻

20. TREASURER INDEPENDENCE

Strong preference:

The Treasurer should not be the same person who:

* Performs all bookkeeping
* Initiates all payments
* Reconciles all bank accounts
* Reviews all financial reports

The Treasurer provides oversight.

TREASURER ≠ ENTIRE FINANCE DEPARTMENT

⸻

21. SECRETARY

The Secretary is responsible for ensuring appropriate preservation of:

* Minutes
* Resolutions
* Board records
* Policy adoption records
* Corporate governance records

The Secretary supports the institutional memory of the corporation.

⸻

22. BOARD CHAIR VS. EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR

As The Tabernacle develops, governance should distinguish:

BOARD CHAIR

Leads governance.

EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR

Leads management and operations.

One person may temporarily hold multiple positions during formation if permitted, but the organization should continually evaluate whether separation would strengthen governance.

⸻

23. BOARD CANDIDATE SCREENING

Before appointment, obtain:

* Résumé or professional biography
* Relevant expertise
* Current employment
* Board experience
* Business interests
* Family relationships
* Vendor relationships
* Criminal/background screening if role or risk warrants
* Conflict disclosure
* Availability
* Mission alignment

⸻

24. BOARD CANDIDATE INTERVIEW

Ask every candidate:

1. Why are you interested in The Tabernacle?
2. Do you understand that directors owe duties to the corporation rather than to the founder?
3. Can you challenge leadership respectfully?
4. Are you willing to review financial information?
5. Are you willing to disclose conflicts?
6. Can you attend meetings consistently?
7. Do you have relationships that may create conflicts?
8. Are you comfortable governing an explicitly Christian nonprofit?
9. Are you comfortable overseeing programs serving vulnerable people?
10. Are you prepared to make a NO-GO decision on a property or program when necessary?

26. CHARACTERISTICS TO SEEK

Seek people who are:

* Dependable
* Thoughtful
* Mission-aligned
* Financially responsible
* Capable of independent judgment
* Comfortable asking difficult questions
* Respectful of governance boundaries
* Willing to document decisions
* Protective of vulnerable people

⸻

27. CHARACTERISTICS TO AVOID

Avoid selecting someone primarily because:

* They always agree with the founder
* They are family
* They donated money
* They have celebrity/status
* They are willing to sign anything
* They will never challenge a proposal

AGREEMENT ≠ GOVERNANCE

⸻

28. VENDOR DIRECTORS

A person whose business expects substantial work from The Tabernacle should generally not be recruited specifically to make approving those contracts easier.

Any vendor relationship must be disclosed and independently reviewed.

⸻

29. PROPERTY PROFESSIONALS

A real-estate professional may be extremely useful on the Board.

However, if that person’s firm may:

* Broker the purchase
* Earn commission
* Manage property
* Perform construction
* Provide financing

the financial relationship shall be disclosed and independently evaluated.

⸻

30. ATTORNEYS ON BOARD

An attorney may serve as a director.

However:

DIRECTOR ≠ AUTOMATIC ORGANIZATIONAL COUNSEL

The roles of:

* Board member
* Attorney
* Privileged legal adviser

should be distinguished where relevant.

⸻

31. BOARD SERVICE IS NOT EMPLOYMENT

Board membership alone does not create employment.

Directors should generally understand whether service is:

* Uncompensated
* Expense-reimbursed
* Separately compensated for distinct services

Any compensation requires appropriate review.

⸻

32. DIRECTOR EXPENSES

Reasonable Board expenses may be reimbursed according to organizational policy.

Examples may include approved:

* Travel
* Training
* Governance expenses

Reimbursement shall not be used as disguised compensation.

⸻

33. BOARD REMOVAL

The adopted Bylaws and applicable state law shall control removal.

Possible reasons may include:

* Repeated failure to participate
* Serious misconduct
* Breach of confidentiality
* Undisclosed conflict
* Safeguarding violation
* Breach of fiduciary duty
* Conduct materially harmful to the organization

⸻

34. RESIGNATION

Director resignation should be written and preserved in corporate records.

System and information access shall be reviewed immediately upon departure.

⸻

35. BOARD SUCCESSION

The organization shall not depend permanently on a single person.

Board development should continually identify future:

* Directors
* Officers
* Committee leaders

SUCCESSION IS A GOVERNANCE CONTROL
