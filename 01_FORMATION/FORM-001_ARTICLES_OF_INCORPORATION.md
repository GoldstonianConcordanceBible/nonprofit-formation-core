ARTICLE I — NAME

ARTICLE II — NONPROFIT CHARACTER

The Corporation is organized as a nonprofit, nonstock corporation under the laws of the State of <STATE>.

The Corporation is not organized for the private profit or financial benefit of any founder, director, officer, member, employee, donor, or other private person.

⸻

ARTICLE III — EXEMPT PURPOSE

The Corporation is organized exclusively for charitable, religious, and educational purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or the corresponding provision of any future federal tax law.

Its purposes include, but are not limited to, the activities described in these Articles, provided that all such activities remain consistent with Section 501(c)(3).

* Housing-resource navigation
* Employment assistance
* Transportation assistance
* Identification-document assistance

* Community referrals
* Mentoring

ARTICLE VIII — FACILITIES AND REAL PROPERTY

The Corporation may acquire, receive, own, lease, manage, renovate, improve, maintain, finance, sell, transfer, or otherwise lawfully deal with real and personal property when doing so furthers its exempt purposes.

Such property may include:

* Churches
* Former churches
* Ministry centers
* Educational facilities
* Community centers
* Offices
* Kitchens
* Food-distribution locations
* Reentry centers
* Training facilities
* Lawfully authorized refuge or shelter facilities
* Other property used to accomplish the Corporation’s exempt purposes

The acquisition of property shall not itself establish legal authority for any particular occupancy or use.

All facilities shall remain subject to applicable law, permitting, zoning, building, fire, occupancy, health, food-service, accessibility, insurance, and safety requirements.

⸻

ARTICLE IX — 24-HOUR OPERATIONS

Where lawful, appropriately permitted, safely staffed, and approved by the Board of Directors, the Corporation may operate facilities for extended hours or 24 hours per day.

Such facilities may provide:

* Worship
* Prayer
* Ministry
* Education
* Meals
* Community assistance
* Refuge
* Resource navigation
* Mentorship
* Other exempt-purpose programming

Nothing in this Article authorizes overnight shelter, residential occupancy, food service, or other regulated activity without required governmental approvals.

⸻

ARTICLE X — CHARITABLE DISTRIBUTIONS

The Corporation may make grants, contributions, and other distributions in furtherance of its exempt purposes.

The Corporation may also make distributions to organizations that qualify as exempt organizations under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code.

The Corporation shall adopt appropriate controls for grants and charitable distributions.

⸻

ARTICLE XI — PRIVATE INUREMENT

No part of the net earnings of the Corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to:

* Any founder
* Incorporator
* Director
* Officer
* Employee
* Donor
* Related person
* Other private individual

except that the Corporation shall be authorized and empowered to pay reasonable compensation for services actually rendered and to make payments and distributions in furtherance of the purposes stated in these Articles.

⸻

ARTICLE XII — PRIVATE BENEFIT

The Corporation shall not be organized or operated primarily for the private benefit of any individual, family, commercial business, founder-controlled enterprise, or other private interest.

Transactions involving directors, officers, founders, employees, family members, or related entities shall be subject to the Corporation’s conflict-of-interest and related-party transaction policies.

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — POLITICAL CAMPAIGN ACTIVITY

The Corporation shall not participate in, or intervene in, including through the publishing or distribution of statements, any political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for public office.

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — LEGISLATIVE ACTIVITY

No substantial part of the activities of the Corporation shall consist of carrying on propaganda or otherwise attempting to influence legislation, except to the extent permitted for an organization exempt under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code.

⸻

ARTICLE XV — PROHIBITED ACTIVITIES

Notwithstanding any other provision of these Articles, the Corporation shall not carry on any activities that are not permitted to be carried on by:

1. A corporation exempt from federal income tax under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code; or
2. A corporation contributions to which are deductible under Section 170(c)(2) of the Internal Revenue Code.

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — MEMBERSHIP

Unless otherwise authorized through an amendment to these Articles and the Bylaws, the Corporation shall have no statutory voting members.

Governance authority shall be vested in the Board of Directors.

Participation in worship, charitable services, education, volunteering, donations, Discord communities, online communities, classes, or programs shall not by itself create corporate membership or voting rights.

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — BOARD OF DIRECTORS

The affairs of the Corporation shall be managed under the direction of its Board of Directors.

The number, qualifications, terms, election, removal, powers, meetings, and procedures of directors shall be established in the Bylaws and applicable law.

The Corporation intends to maintain meaningful independent Board oversight.

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — FOUNDER STATUS

The Corporation may recognize one or more persons as founders for historical or organizational purposes.

Founder designation alone shall not:

* Create ownership of the Corporation
* Create ownership of charitable assets
* Establish permanent Board membership
* Establish unilateral authority
* Override the Bylaws
* Override Board authority
* Override conflict-of-interest requirements
* Override applicable law

A founder may exercise authority only through a lawful role held within the Corporation.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — DISSOLUTION

Upon the dissolution of the Corporation, its assets shall be distributed for one or more exempt purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or the corresponding provision of any future federal tax law, or shall be distributed to the federal government, or to a state or local government, for a public purpose.

No remaining charitable assets shall be distributed to a founder, director, officer, employee, donor, member, or other private person except for the lawful satisfaction of bona fide obligations.

This clause is intended to permanently dedicate the Corporation’s charitable assets to exempt purposes.
