ARTICLE I — PURPOSE

The purpose of this policy is to govern the solicitation, acceptance, acknowledgment, restriction, management, and disposition of charitable gifts made to The Tabernacle, Inc.

The policy protects:

* The mission
* Donors
* Beneficiaries
* Charitable assets
* Tax-exempt status
* Financial integrity
* Organizational independence

⸻

ARTICLE II — CORE RULE

OFFERED ≠ ACCEPTED

A donor’s willingness to transfer an asset does not require The Tabernacle to accept it.

The organization may reject a gift that creates excessive:

* Liability
* Cost
* Restrictions
* Compliance requirements
* Environmental risk
* Reputation risk
* Private benefit
* Mission conflict
* Operational burden

⸻

ARTICLE III — ACCEPTABLE FUNDING SOURCES

The Tabernacle may seek appropriate support through:

* Individual donations
* Church donations
* Congregational giving
* Foundation grants
* Corporate charitable contributions
* Government grants
* Online donations
* Recurring donations
* Fundraising campaigns
* Fundraising events
* Bequests
* Gifts in kind
* Securities
* Digital assets
* Vehicles
* Real estate
* Other lawful charitable gifts

Acceptance remains subject to this policy.

⸻

ARTICLE IV — TAX-EXEMPT STATUS COMMUNICATION

The Tabernacle shall accurately represent its current legal and federal tax status.

Before IRS recognition has been received, fundraising materials shall not state that IRS recognition has already been granted.

After recognition, communications may accurately describe the organization’s recognized status.

CONTROL RULE

APPLICATION ≠ DETERMINATION

⸻

ARTICLE V — DONOR DEDUCTIBILITY

The Tabernacle may provide appropriate acknowledgments but shall not provide individualized tax advice.

Donors are responsible for determining:

* Deductibility
* Fair market value
* Appraisal requirements
* Tax consequences
* Filing requirements

Donors should consult their own tax or legal advisers where appropriate.

⸻

ARTICLE VI — GIFT ACCEPTANCE AUTHORITY

Routine gifts within established procedures may be accepted by authorized personnel.

Enhanced review is required for gifts involving:

* Real estate
* Vehicles
* Significant noncash property
* Digital assets
* Securities with restrictions
* Intellectual property
* Closely held business interests
* Unusual donor restrictions
* Debt
* Environmental exposure
* Naming rights
* Significant continuing obligations
* Related parties

⸻

ARTICLE VII — BOARD-LEVEL GIFTS

Board approval should ordinarily be required before acceptance of a gift involving:

* Real estate
* Material debt
* Material environmental exposure
* Significant ongoing expense
* Unusual legal restrictions
* Significant related-party interests
* Material naming commitments
* Assets inconsistent with mission
* Transactions materially affecting organizational control

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — CASH GIFTS

Cash gifts may be accepted through authorized systems.

Permitted methods may include:

* Check
* ACH
* Credit/debit card
* Approved online donation platform
* Bank transfer
* Physical currency

All donations shall enter organizational financial controls.

⸻

ARTICLE IX — NO PERSONAL COLLECTION ACCOUNTS

Personnel shall not solicit charitable donations for The Tabernacle through an individual’s personal:

* Bank account
* Cash App
* Venmo
* PayPal
* Digital wallet
* Other financial account

unless a formally approved arrangement clearly establishes the account as an authorized organizational collection mechanism.

TABERNACLE DONATION ≠ PERSONAL MONEY

⸻

ARTICLE X — CASH COLLECTION

Physical cash donations shall use reasonable dual-control procedures.

The process should follow:

RECEIVE

↓

COUNT

↓

DOCUMENT

↓

DEPOSIT

↓

RECORD

↓

RECONCILE

Where practical, different persons should participate in receipt/counting and reconciliation.

⸻

ARTICLE XI — ONLINE GIVING

Approved online donation platforms shall:

* Deposit to organizational accounts
* Maintain transaction records
* Identify applicable fees
* Support donor acknowledgments
* Use appropriate access controls
* Limit administrative privileges

Donation-platform passwords and credentials shall not be stored in GitHub or Discord.

⸻

ARTICLE XII — RECURRING GIFTS

Recurring donations shall clearly communicate:

* Frequency
* Amount
* Cancellation mechanism
* Any designation/restriction

The organization shall process cancellation requests appropriately.

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — UNRESTRICTED GIFTS

An unrestricted contribution may generally be used by The Tabernacle for lawful organizational purposes consistent with its mission.

Unrestricted gifts provide the Board with flexibility to allocate resources according to organizational needs.

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — RESTRICTED GIFTS

A donor may restrict a gift to a specific charitable purpose accepted by The Tabernacle.

Examples:

* Meal program
* Education
* Prison/reentry
* Property acquisition
* Building renovation
* Specific site
* Emergency assistance

Once accepted, donor restrictions shall be tracked and honored.

RESTRICTED ≠ AVAILABLE FOR ANY PURPOSE

⸻

ARTICLE XV — ACCEPTING A RESTRICTION

Before accepting a material restriction, determine:

1. Is it consistent with the mission?
2. Can The Tabernacle actually perform it?
3. Can accounting track it?
4. Is there a reasonable time horizon?
5. Does it create excessive administrative cost?
6. Does it improperly benefit a private person?
7. What happens if the program becomes impossible?

Restrictions should be documented in writing.

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — DONOR CONTROL

A charitable donor may establish a lawful restriction.

A donation shall not give the donor operational ownership or control over The Tabernacle.

A donor may not purchase:

* Board votes
* Founder-like authority
* Control of charitable assets
* Improper control over beneficiary selection
* Authority inconsistent with the Articles or Bylaws

DONATION ≠ GOVERNANCE AUTHORITY

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — DESIGNATED BENEFICIARIES

The Tabernacle generally shall not accept a purported charitable donation conditioned upon transferring the funds to a specific private individual chosen solely by the donor unless an appropriately controlled charitable-assistance program permits such support under lawful criteria.

The organization, not the donor, must retain appropriate discretion and control over charitable assets.

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — NAMING RIGHTS

A substantial donor may request recognition or naming.

Any material naming arrangement should define:

* Name
* Facility/program
* Duration
* Donor obligations
* Organizational obligations
* Morals/reputation provisions where appropriate
* Modification/termination rights

Naming shall not transfer ownership or governance authority.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — ANONYMOUS GIFTS

The Tabernacle may honor reasonable requests for public anonymity.

PUBLIC ANONYMITY ≠ ORGANIZATIONAL IGNORANCE

Where legally, financially, or operationally necessary, authorized personnel may still need to know donor identity.

The organization may reject gifts where the source cannot be responsibly evaluated.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — SUSPICIOUS OR HIGH-RISK GIFTS

The organization may pause or reject a gift when there are legitimate concerns regarding:

* Illegality
* Fraud
* Stolen property
* Sanctions
* Money laundering
* Unknown ownership
* Fraudulent title
* Criminal proceeds
* Unusual intermediary arrangements
* Attempts to evade reporting or controls

Professional advice may be obtained before acceptance.

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — DONOR ACKNOWLEDGMENTS

The Tabernacle shall establish a consistent donor acknowledgment process.

An acknowledgment should include, as applicable:

* Legal name of The Tabernacle
* Date
* Amount of monetary contribution
* Description of noncash property
* Whether goods or services were provided in exchange
* Required information concerning any goods or services provided
* Other legally required information

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — NONCASH VALUATION

For donated property, The Tabernacle shall ordinarily acknowledge:

DESCRIPTION

but not provide the donor with a tax-deduction value.

Example:

Appropriate:

Received: one commercial refrigerator

Not appropriate merely because the donor requests it:

Your refrigerator is worth $12,500 for tax purposes

DONOR CLAIM ≠ TABERNACLE VALUATION

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — INTERNAL ACCOUNTING VALUE

The organization may determine an appropriate accounting value for donated property when required for its own financial statements.

That internal accounting determination is separate from representing the donor’s deductible value.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIV — FORM 8283

When a donor presents Form 8283 for qualifying donated property, only an authorized organizational representative may sign on behalf of The Tabernacle.

Signature shall be understood as acknowledgment of receipt where applicable.

It shall not be treated as organizational agreement with the donor’s appraised value.

Copies shall be retained according to the records policy.

⸻

ARTICLE XXV — FORM 8282

When applicable donated property is sold, exchanged, or otherwise disposed of within the applicable federal reporting period, The Tabernacle shall evaluate and complete any required Form 8282 reporting.

The accounting/tax calendar shall flag potentially affected donated assets.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — QUID PRO QUO GIFTS

When a donor makes a payment partly as a contribution and partly in exchange for goods or services, The Tabernacle shall comply with applicable disclosure requirements.

Examples may include:

* Fundraising dinners
* Tickets
* Merchandise packages
* Benefit events

The organization shall provide a good-faith estimate of the value of goods or services where legally required.

⸻

Every fundraising event shall have:

* Event owner
* Purpose
* Budget
* Revenue target
* Expense controls
* Donation procedures

* Sponsorship controls
* Disclosure review

minus

NET FUNDRAISING RESULT

Before accepting a material grant, review:

* Religious-activity conditions

ARTICLE XXX — GOVERNMENT GRANTS

Government funding shall be tracked separately when required.

The organization shall follow applicable requirements concerning:

* Allowable costs
* Procurement
* Religious activities
* Nondiscrimination
* Reporting
* Records
* Audits
* Program delivery

Government money shall not be commingled conceptually with unrestricted private charitable support.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXI — FOOD DONATIONS

HHS-001 may accept appropriate donated food.

Food shall be evaluated for:

* Condition
* Packaging
* Safety
* Storage
* Temperature where applicable
* Expiration/use considerations
* Program need

DONATED ≠ AUTOMATICALLY SAFE

The organization may refuse food that cannot safely or effectively be used.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXII — CLOTHING AND HOUSEHOLD GOODS

The Tabernacle may accept:

* Clothing
* Shoes
* Hygiene supplies
* Furniture
* Household goods
* Program equipment

when consistent with actual program need and storage capacity.

The organization may refuse:

* Unsafe items
* Unsanitary items
* Broken items
* Excess inventory
* Items expensive to dispose of

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIII — EQUIPMENT

Before accepting significant equipment, review:

* Condition
* Usefulness
* Installation cost
* Maintenance
* Power requirements
* Insurance
* Removal/disposal cost

FREE EQUIPMENT ≠ FREE TO OPERATE

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIV — VEHICLES

Before accepting a donated vehicle, review:

* Title
* Liens
* Condition
* Mileage
* Insurance
* Repair cost
* Intended use
* Storage
* Registration
* Disposal plan

The organization shall comply with applicable special federal acknowledgment/reporting requirements for donated vehicles.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXV — VEHICLE DECISION

For every material vehicle gift, select:

ACCEPT FOR SALE

ACCEPT FOR TRANSFER UNDER APPROVED CHARITABLE PROGRAM

DECLINE

The intended disposition should be documented.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVI — REAL ESTATE

No real-property donation shall be accepted automatically.

Every donated property goes through:

including appropriate review of:

* Title
* Liens
* Mortgages
* Easements
* Restrictions

DONATED CHURCH ≠ FREE CHURCH

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVII — REAL-ESTATE ENVIRONMENTAL REVIEW

Before accepting donated land or buildings, The Tabernacle shall consider appropriate environmental due diligence.

A gift containing contamination or other environmental liability may be rejected even when the donor assigns the property substantial value.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVIII — ENCUMBERED PROPERTY

Property subject to:

* Mortgage
* Debt
* Lien
* Lease
* Life estate
* Easement
* Operating obligation

requires enhanced legal and financial review.

The organization shall understand the obligation before accepting title.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIX — REAL-ESTATE HOLD OR SALE

Before acceptance, leadership should determine the intended strategy:

PROGRAM USE

HOLD

RENOVATE

SELL

Acceptance shall not be based solely on an assumption that the property can quickly be resold.

⸻

ARTICLE XL — SECURITIES

The Tabernacle may establish an approved brokerage mechanism for donated securities.

Unless the Board establishes a different investment strategy, marketable securities donated for general charitable support may ordinarily be converted according to a Board-approved treasury policy rather than held as speculative investments.

Restrictions shall be reviewed before acceptance.

⸻

ARTICLE XLI — CLOSELY HELD BUSINESS INTERESTS

Gifts involving:

* LLC interests
* Partnership interests
* Private-company stock
* Other closely held interests

require professional review before acceptance.

Review may include:

* Transfer restrictions
* Valuation
* Debt
* Unrelated business taxable income
* Governance rights
* Capital calls
* Liability
* Saleability

⸻

ARTICLE XLII — DIGITAL ASSETS

The Tabernacle may accept digital assets only through an approved process.

Examples may include:

* Bitcoin
* Ether
* Other approved digital assets

Digital-asset donations are treated as noncash contributions for federal tax purposes.

⸻

ARTICLE XLIII — DIGITAL-ASSET WALLET CONTROL

Organizational digital assets shall not be received into an individual’s personal wallet.

Any organizational wallet shall have:

* Documented organizational ownership
* Approved custodians
* Access controls
* Backup/recovery procedures
* Transaction records
* Accounting procedures

ORGANIZATIONAL CRYPTO ≠ PERSONAL CRYPTO

⸻

ARTICLE XLIV — DIGITAL-ASSET ACCEPTANCE LIST

Leadership may maintain an approved digital-asset acceptance list.

The Tabernacle may reject assets presenting excessive:

* Liquidity risk
* Custody risk
* Regulatory risk
* Fraud risk
* Technical risk
* Accounting burden

⸻

ARTICLE XLV — DIGITAL-ASSET LIQUIDATION

Unless the Board adopts an investment policy authorizing retention, the organization may establish a default practice of promptly converting donated digital assets into U.S. dollars or another approved treasury asset.

The purpose is charitable treasury management rather than speculation.

Any decision to retain significant volatile assets shall be governed by Board-approved investment controls.

⸻

ARTICLE XLVI — DIGITAL-ASSET DOCUMENTATION

Maintain, as applicable:

* Date/time received
* Asset
* Quantity
* Transaction identifier
* Receiving wallet/custodian
* Donor information where known
* Accounting value
* Disposition
* Proceeds
* Applicable Form 8283/8282 information

Private keys and recovery phrases shall never be placed in ordinary accounting records, GitHub, or Discord.

⸻

ARTICLE XLVII — INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

Before accepting:

* Copyright
* Trademark
* Patent
* Curriculum
* Book rights
* Media rights
* Software
* Music rights

review:

* Ownership
* Existing licenses
* Royalties
* Restrictions
* Enforcement costs
* Revenue obligations
* Tax/reporting requirements
* Mission value

⸻

ARTICLE XLVIII — SERVICES

Donated professional services may benefit The Tabernacle.

The organization may acknowledge the person’s service and hours where appropriate but shall not represent a tax-deductible value without appropriate legal basis.

Accounting treatment shall follow professional guidance.

⸻

ARTICLE XLIX — GIFT CARDS

Gift cards may be treated as controlled financial assets.

Procedures should address:

* Receipt
* Custody
* Balance
* Program assignment
* Distribution
* Reconciliation

Gift cards shall not disappear into informal staff possession.

⸻

ARTICLE L — BEQUESTS AND ESTATE GIFTS

The Tabernacle may accept appropriate:

* Bequests
* Estate distributions
* Beneficiary designations

Complex gifts involving trusts, retained interests, property, or continuing obligations shall receive professional review.

⸻

ARTICLE LI — LIFE INSURANCE

The organization may accept appropriate beneficiary designations or ownership interests after review of:

* Premium obligations
* Ownership
* Policy condition
* Restrictions
* Administrative burden

The organization shall not undertake obligations it cannot reasonably maintain.

⸻

ARTICLE LII — DONOR RESTRICTIONS INVOLVING RELIGION

A donor may support:

* Ministry
* Bible education
* Worship
* Chaplaincy
* Other religious purposes

provided the restriction remains consistent with:

* The Articles
* The Bylaws
* Tax-exempt purposes
* Applicable funding/legal requirements

⸻

ARTICLE LIII — GIFTS FOR INDIVIDUAL EMPLOYEES OR FOUNDERS

Payments intended personally for:

* Founder
* Director
* Officer
* Minister
* Employee

shall not automatically be treated as charitable contributions to The Tabernacle.

The Treasurer shall determine the correct handling in consultation with tax/accounting professionals where necessary.

⸻

ARTICLE LIV — RELATED-PARTY GIFTS

A gift from an insider does not eliminate conflicts concerning related transactions.

Example:

A founder donating money does not permit the founder to direct the nonprofit to overpay the founder’s company.

DONATION ≠ WAIVER OF CONFLICT CONTROLS

⸻

ARTICLE LV — REFUNDING GIFTS

Charitable gifts are generally not refundable merely because a donor changes their mind.

However, refunds or returns may be considered when:

* Payment was made in error
* Fraud occurred
* Organization cannot honor an accepted restriction
* Law requires return
* Board/counsel determines return is appropriate

Restricted funds shall not simply be redirected without appropriate authority.

⸻

ARTICLE LVI — FAILED RESTRICTED PURPOSE

If an accepted restriction becomes impossible, impracticable, unlawful, or no longer charitable, leadership shall obtain appropriate legal guidance before redirecting funds.

Donor communication, consent, court approval, or another legal process may be necessary depending on circumstances.

⸻

ARTICLE LVII — FUNDRAISING CLAIMS

Fundraising shall be truthful.

The Tabernacle shall not falsely claim:

* A property has been purchased when it has not
* A prison partnership has been approved when it has not
* A shelter is operating when it is not
* IRS recognition has been obtained when it has not
* Donations will be used for a restricted purpose when they will not
* A specific number of beneficiaries has been served without evidence

⸻

ARTICLE LIX — PROPERTY CAMPAIGNS

A campaign to acquire a specific church/property shall state accurately whether:

* Property is merely being considered
* Under contract
* Board approved
* Closing is conditional
* Funds may be redirected if acquisition fails

The campaign should define what happens to restricted money if the contemplated acquisition does not occur.

ARTICLE LXI — PROFESSIONAL FUNDRAISERS

Before hiring a professional fundraiser, review:

* Contract
* Compensation
* Registration requirements
* Solicitation law
* Access to donor information
* Data ownership
* Reputation
* Termination
* Financial controls

No fundraiser may direct charitable funds to an unauthorized account.

⸻

ARTICLE LXII — STATE CHARITABLE SOLICITATION

Before broadly soliciting donations, The Tabernacle shall determine which state charitable-registration and solicitation requirements apply.

Online fundraising may reach donors in multiple jurisdictions.

A state-specific compliance system shall be developed as fundraising expands.

⸻

ARTICLE LXIII — DONOR PRIVACY

The Tabernacle shall protect donor information appropriately.

Donor information shall not be sold or casually disclosed.

Access shall correspond to legitimate organizational need.

⸻

ARTICLE LXIV — DONOR LIST

Donor lists shall not be maintained in:

* Public GitHub
* Public Discord
* Public spreadsheets

Approved fundraising/accounting systems shall be used.

* Governance
* Safeguarding
* Legal compliance
