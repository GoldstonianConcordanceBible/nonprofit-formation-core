ARTICLE I — PURPOSE

This policy establishes the workforce system for The Tabernacle, Inc.

It governs:

* Directors
* Officers
* Founders
* Employees
* Ministers
* Program leaders
* Temporary workers
* Contractors
* Security personnel
* Educators
* Kitchen personnel
* Mentors
* Prison/reentry personnel
* Volunteers

The purpose is to ensure that every person working for or serving through The Tabernacle has:

* A defined role
* Proper authority
* Appropriate classification
* Appropriate screening
* Appropriate training
* Appropriate compensation where applicable
* Appropriate supervision
* Appropriate system access

⸻

ARTICLE II — CORE RULE

PERSON PRESENT ≠ PERSON AUTHORIZED

Every organizational worker or volunteer shall operate through a defined role.

The Tabernacle shall not build its workforce through informal arrangements in which people simply begin performing substantial organizational duties without:

* Classification
* Authorization
* Role definition
* Supervision

⸻

ARTICLE III — WORKFORCE CATEGORIES

The Tabernacle may use:

A. DIRECTORS

Members of the governing Board.

B. OFFICERS

Persons holding corporate offices established by the Bylaws or Board.

C. EMPLOYEES

Individuals employed by The Tabernacle.

D. MINISTERS / CHAPLAINS

Persons performing religious or pastoral functions.

Employment status shall be evaluated separately from religious title.

E. INDEPENDENT CONTRACTORS

Independent businesses or individuals properly classified under applicable law.

F. STAFFING-AGENCY PERSONNEL

Workers supplied through an approved staffing company.

G. VOLUNTEERS

Individuals freely donating services without improper expectation of compensation.

ARTICLE VII — FOUNDER EMPLOYMENT

A founder may lawfully perform genuine work for The Tabernacle.

Founder status does not automatically create:

* Employment
* Salary
* Benefits
* Expense privileges
* Permanent authority

Any compensated founder position shall have:

* Genuine organizational need
* Written duties
* Expected time commitment
* Compensation analysis
* Disinterested approval
* Conflict disclosure
* Documented Board action where required

FOUNDER ≠ AUTOMATIC EMPLOYEE

FOUNDER ≠ AUTOMATIC COMPENSATION

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — FAMILY MEMBERS

Family relationships do not automatically prohibit employment.

A family member may be hired when:

* A legitimate position exists
* The individual is qualified
* Compensation is reasonable
* Conflicts are disclosed
* Appropriate disinterested persons approve the arrangement
* Performance is actually supervised

Family relationship shall not substitute for qualifications.

⸻

ARTICLE IX — ELISE

Elise may serve in a legitimate role if the organization determines that her services further The Tabernacle’s mission.

Potential roles may include:

* Food & Relief Director
* Culinary Program Director
* Recipe Development Lead
* Kitchen Operations Lead
* Education contributor
* Other legitimate program duties

If compensated, the position shall be documented and approved pursuant to GOV-003.

Compensation shall be for actual services rather than personal relationship.

⸻

ARTICLE X — WORKER CLASSIFICATION

The Tabernacle shall classify workers according to the actual relationship.

Possible classifications include:

* Employee
* Independent contractor
* Staffing-agency worker
* Volunteer

The organization shall not determine classification merely by:

* Label
* Contract title
* Method of payment
* Worker preference
* Part-time status
* Remote status

⸻

ARTICLE XI — EMPLOYEE VS. CONTRACTOR REVIEW

Before classifying an individual as an independent contractor, review factors concerning:

Behavioral Control

Who determines:

* When work occurs
* Where work occurs
* How work is performed
* Training
* Procedures
* Supervision

Financial Control

Consider:

* Who supplies tools
* Business investment
* Unreimbursed expenses
* Payment structure
* Ability to make services available to the broader market

Relationship

Consider:

* Permanence
* Benefits
* Written agreement
* Whether services are central to The Tabernacle’s ongoing operations

No single factor automatically controls.

ARTICLE XIII — NO MISCLASSIFICATION FOR CONVENIENCE

The organization shall not classify someone as a contractor merely to avoid:

* Payroll taxes
* Overtime
* Benefits
* Workers’ compensation
* Payroll administration
* Employment law

CONTRACTOR LABEL ≠ CONTRACTOR STATUS

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — STAFFING AGENCIES

The Tabernacle may use staffing agencies when appropriate.

Before engagement, review:

* Agency identity
* Contract
* Rates
* Employer responsibilities
* Payroll responsibility
* Insurance
* Workers’ compensation
* Background screening
* Training
* Job duties
* Supervision
* Indemnification where appropriate

⸻

ARTICLE XV — STAFFING AGENCY PERSONNEL

Agency workers shall receive site-specific orientation concerning:

* Mission
* Duties
* Safety
* Safeguarding
* Confidentiality
* Emergency procedures
* Supervisor
* Restricted areas

The staffing company relationship does not eliminate The Tabernacle’s responsibility for safe site operations.

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — SECURITY STAFF

Security workers shall be treated separately from ordinary temporary labor where applicable.

Before assignment, verify:

* Provider qualifications
* Required licenses
* Guard registration
* Training
* Insurance
* Scope of duties

No worker becomes a security professional merely because management asks the person to “watch the door.”

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — MINISTERS AND CHAPLAINS

A religious title does not independently determine:

* Tax classification
* Employment classification
* Compensation
* Authority
* Access level

The organization shall separately document:

* Religious role
* Corporate role
* Employment relationship
* Compensation
* Reporting structure

where applicable.

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — RELIGIOUS EMPLOYMENT

The Tabernacle is intended to be an explicitly Christian religious organization.

Where legally applicable, the organization may establish religious qualifications for positions connected to carrying out its religious mission.

Religious qualifications shall be documented thoughtfully and applied consistently.

The organization shall obtain employment-law review before relying on any religious-organization exemption or ministerial exception in a disputed or material matter.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — NON-RELIGIOUS EMPLOYMENT PROTECTIONS

Religious status shall not be treated as a blanket exemption from all employment laws.

The organization shall comply with applicable laws concerning areas such as:

* Race
* Color
* National origin
* Sex
* Age
* Disability
* Wage/hour requirements
* Retaliation

subject to legally recognized exemptions and defenses.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — VOLUNTEER STATUS

A volunteer provides service:

* Freely
* For charitable, religious, civic, or humanitarian purposes
* Without improper expectation of compensation

Volunteer status shall not be used to disguise an ordinary employment relationship.

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — VOLUNTEER ACKNOWLEDGMENT

Each recurring volunteer should acknowledge:

* Volunteer nature of service
* No guaranteed compensation
* Role
* Supervisor
* Policies
* Confidentiality
* Safeguarding responsibilities
* Right to cease volunteering

The acknowledgment shall not be used to override employment law when the actual facts create an employment relationship.

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — EMPLOYEES VOLUNTEERING

A paid employee shall not be directed or pressured to “volunteer” unpaid time for substantially the same work the employee is employed to perform.

Volunteer service by employees shall be reviewed carefully when:

* Employer requests it
* Same duties are involved
* Service occurs during ordinary work time
* Participation could affect employment
* Pressure or expectation exists

PAID WORK ≠ VOLUNTEER WORK BECAUSE WE CALL IT MINISTRY

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — VOLUNTEERS AND REGULAR JOBS

Volunteers should generally supplement charitable capacity rather than improperly replace employees through arrangements that resemble required full-time unpaid work.

Volunteer programs shall remain genuinely voluntary.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIV — VOLUNTEER EXPENSES

The organization may reimburse legitimate approved volunteer expenses according to its reimbursement policy.

Reimbursement of actual expenses does not automatically convert a volunteer into an employee, but arrangements shall still be reviewed according to applicable law.

⸻

ARTICLE XXV — STIPENDS

Before paying a volunteer:

* Stipend
* Honorarium
* Regular allowance
* Repeated cash payment

leadership shall determine whether the payment affects:

* Volunteer status
* Tax treatment
* Employment classification

No program director shall invent an informal stipend system without approval.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — INTERNS

Any internship shall have a defined educational or organizational purpose.

Before treating an intern as unpaid, leadership shall review applicable wage-and-hour requirements.

INTERN ≠ AUTOMATICALLY UNPAID

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — RECRUITMENT

Recruitment shall seek individuals capable of performing the relevant role.

Recruitment may include:

* Applications
* Résumés
* Interviews
* References
* Credential review
* Background screening

The process shall be appropriate to the level of responsibility.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — CONFLICT DISCLOSURE DURING HIRING

Applicants for senior or financially sensitive roles should disclose relevant:

* Family relationships
* Vendor relationships
* Business interests
* Other conflicts

A conflict does not automatically prevent hiring.

It requires appropriate management.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIX — BACKGROUND CHECKS

Background checks may be required for roles involving:

* Children
* Vulnerable adults
* Overnight operations
* Correctional institutions
* Money
* Transportation
* Security
* Unsupervised beneficiary contact
* Other sensitive responsibilities

Background checks shall comply with applicable law.

⸻

ARTICLE XXX — BACKGROUND CHECK ≠ AUTOMATIC DECISION

Results shall be evaluated according to:

* Nature of position
* Nature of information
* Time elapsed
* Applicable law
* Safety
* Individualized considerations where required

The organization shall not create unsupported blanket exclusions where applicable law requires more careful consideration.

ARTICLE XXXI — CREDENTIAL VERIFICATION

Credentials shall be verified when the position depends materially upon them.

Examples:

* Professional license
* Security license
* Teaching credential
* Driver’s license
* Food certification
* Clinical credential

Do not advertise a worker as licensed when the license has not been verified.

ARTICLE XXXV — PROBATION / INTRODUCTORY PERIOD

* Conduct
* Training
* Role fit

ARTICLE XXXVI — TIMEKEEPING

Nonexempt employees shall accurately record compensable working time as required.

The organization shall not permit off-the-clock work merely because:

* Mission is charitable
* Employee is salaried
* Employee wants to help
* Work occurs by phone
* Work occurs remotely
* Work occurs after a shift

Applicable exemption status shall be determined lawfully.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVII — OVERTIME

Where overtime rules apply, The Tabernacle shall:

* Track hours
* Establish approval procedures
* Pay legally required compensation

Failure to obtain advance overtime approval may be a policy matter but shall not automatically eliminate a legal duty to compensate time actually worked where the law requires payment.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVIII — SCHEDULING

Twenty-four-hour operations require controlled scheduling.

Schedules shall account for:

* Coverage
* Rest
* Overtime
* Safety
* Night operations
* Program demand
* Applicable wage/hour rules

No site shall depend permanently on exhausted staff to maintain a 24/7 promise.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIX — COMPENSATION

Compensation shall follow GOV-003.

Compensation may include:

* Salary
* Hourly wages
* Benefits
* Approved allowances
* Other lawful compensation

Compensation decisions involving insiders require enhanced review.

* Personal transfers
* Undocumented cash

ARTICLE XLVI — HARASSMENT AND DISCRIMINATION

The Tabernacle shall establish employment practices consistent with applicable law.

Personnel shall not engage in unlawful:

* Harassment
* Discrimination
* Retaliation

Religious employment rules, where legally applicable, shall be distinguished from prohibited discrimination based upon other protected characteristics.

⸻

ARTICLE XLVII — WHISTLEBLOWER PROTECTION

Workers and volunteers may report good-faith concerns pursuant to GOV-005.

Supervisors shall not retaliate against individuals for legitimate protected reporting.

⸻

ARTICLE XLVIII — SAFEGUARDING REMOVAL

A worker or volunteer may be immediately removed from beneficiary-facing duties when credible safeguarding concerns require protective action.

Temporary removal does not necessarily represent a final factual determination.

⸻

ARTICLE XLIX — DRUGS AND IMPAIRMENT

Personnel shall not perform safety-sensitive duties while materially impaired.

The organization shall develop state-specific procedures addressing:

* Substance use
* Prescribed medication affecting safety
* Testing if contemplated
* Reasonable suspicion
* Accommodation requirements

before implementing any testing program.

* License
* Insurance
* Vehicle authorization
* Safe driving
* Participant transportation

⸻

ARTICLE LIV — PRISON VOLUNTEERS

Personnel entering correctional institutions shall also satisfy REENTRY-001 requirements.

Internal Tabernacle approval does not substitute for correctional clearance.

TABERNACLE APPROVED ≠ FACILITY APPROVED

⸻

ARTICLE LV — KITCHEN PERSONNEL

Personnel preparing or handling food shall satisfy HHS-001 requirements and applicable food-safety rules.

Recipe knowledge does not substitute for required safety training.

⸻

ARTICLE LVI — EDUCATION PERSONNEL

Instructors shall accurately represent:

* Qualifications
* Credentials
* Experience

Course participation shall not be represented as accredited instruction unless appropriate authorization exists.

ARTICLE LVII — SECURITY PERSONNEL

Security personnel shall comply with SAFE-001 and applicable licensing requirements.

⸻

ARTICLE LVIII — VOLUNTEER SUPERVISION

* Defined duties
* Boundaries
* Reporting route

ARTICLE LIX — VOLUNTEER ACCESS

* Financial systems

* Security systems

ARTICLE LX — DISCIPLINE

Corrective action may include:

* Coaching
* Retraining
* Warning
* Restricted duties
* Suspension
* Termination
* Volunteer removal

depending on circumstances and applicable law.

Serious safeguarding or safety issues may require immediate action.

ARTICLE LXI — TERMINATION AUTHORITY

The Board shall determine authority for terminating senior executive leadership.

The Executive Director or delegated management may terminate other employees within approved authority and law.

No supervisor shall terminate a worker solely to retaliate for protected reporting.

⸻

ARTICLE LXII — VOLUNTEER REMOVAL

Volunteer service is not an entitlement.

The organization may end a volunteer assignment because of:

* Safety
* Misconduct
* Boundary violations
* Performance
* Program need
* Loss of facility approval
* Other legitimate reasons

subject to applicable law.

ARTICLE LXIII — OFFBOARDING

Every separation should trigger an offboarding checklist.

Actions may include:

1. Confirm last working/service date.
2. Recover keys.
3. Recover access cards.
4. Recover organizational property.
5. Disable email.
6. Disable Discord access.
7. Disable GitHub access.
8. Disable accounting access.
9. Disable facility/security credentials.
10. Transfer files.
11. Transfer open work.
12. Preserve records.
13. Process final payroll as required.
14. Remind person of continuing confidentiality obligations.

* Security risk
* Fraud risk
* Safeguarding risk
* Data risk
