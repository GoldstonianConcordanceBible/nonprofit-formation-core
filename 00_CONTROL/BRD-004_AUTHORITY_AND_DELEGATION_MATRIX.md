ARTICLE I — PURPOSE

This document establishes who may:

* Make decisions
* Approve spending
* Sign contracts
* Hire personnel
* Fire personnel
* Open accounts
* Apply for grants
* Negotiate property
* Sign property documents
* Contact correctional institutions
* Launch programs
* Activate sites
* Speak publicly for The Tabernacle

The purpose is to ensure:

RIGHT PERSON → RIGHT AUTHORITY → RIGHT ACTION → RIGHT EVIDENCE

⸻

ARTICLE II — FUNDAMENTAL RULE

ROLE ≠ AUTHORITY

A person’s title does not automatically authorize every action associated with that title.

Authority must arise from:

* Articles
* Bylaws
* Board resolution
* Adopted policy
* Written delegation
* Approved job responsibility

⸻

ARTICLE III — AUTHORITY HIERARCHY

Authority follows:

1. APPLICABLE LAW

A lower level may not override a higher level.

⸻

ARTICLE IV — BOARD RESERVED POWERS

The Board retains authority over material matters including:

* Articles amendments
* Bylaws
* Board membership
* Officer election/removal
* Executive Director hiring/removal
* Executive compensation
* Founder compensation
* Material related-party transactions
* Annual budget
* Material debt
* Bank-account authorization
* Real-estate acquisition
* Real-estate sale
* Mortgages
* Major leases
* Major capital expenditures
* Form 1023 authorization
* Major program divisions
* Overnight shelter authorization
* Dissolution
* Merger
* Other extraordinary corporate transactions

Delegation shall not be presumed for these matters.

⸻

ARTICLE V — BOARD ≠ DAILY MANAGEMENT

The Board governs.

The Executive Director manages ordinary operations.

The Board should not normally determine:

* Individual meal menus
* Ordinary volunteer schedules
* Routine supply purchases
* Daily class schedules
* Ordinary staff scheduling
* Routine vendor coordination

unless circumstances require intervention.

⸻

ARTICLE VI — EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR

Subject to Board policy and budget, the Executive Director may:

* Manage ordinary operations
* Supervise personnel
* Implement Board-approved programs
* Sign ordinary contracts within delegated limits
* Approve ordinary expenditures
* Coordinate fundraising
* Apply for grants within authority
* Represent the organization operationally
* Develop partnerships
* Recommend personnel
* Recommend property opportunities

The Executive Director may not independently exercise powers reserved to the Board.

⸻

ARTICLE VII — TREASURER

The Treasurer provides financial oversight.

Authority may include:

* Reviewing financial reports
* Reviewing bank reconciliations
* Monitoring cash
* Monitoring restricted funds
* Reviewing budgets
* Approving transactions within delegated limits
* Reviewing unusual expenditures
* Participating in banking administration

TREASURER ≠ SOLE CONTROL OF MONEY

⸻

ARTICLE VIII — SECRETARY

The Secretary is responsible for governance-record control.

Authority includes:

* Maintaining Board records
* Maintaining resolutions
* Certifying corporate actions where appropriate
* Documenting policy adoption
* Maintaining governance registers

The Secretary does not independently create corporate authority merely by documenting something.

⸻

ARTICLE IX — PROGRAM DIRECTORS

Program Directors may operate programs already authorized within:

* Approved charter
* Approved budget
* Approved staffing
* Adopted policies
* Delegated financial authority

They may not independently:

* Create new major programs
* Purchase property
* Borrow money
* Open bank accounts
* Alter donor restrictions
* Sign major contracts
* Commit The Tabernacle outside delegated authority

⸻

ARTICLE X — DAY-ZERO SPENDING LEVELS

Recommended initial approval levels:

LEVEL A — UP TO $500

Program Director may approve an ordinary budgeted program expenditure.

Requirements:

* Within approved budget
* Within program
* No related-party conflict
* No contract exceeding authority
* Receipt/invoice required

⸻

LEVEL B — $500.01 TO $2,500

Executive Director approval required.

Program Director may request.

⸻

LEVEL C — $2,500.01 TO $10,000

Require:

Executive Director + Treasurer

or another two-person combination specifically authorized by Board policy.

⸻

LEVEL D — OVER $10,000

Board approval required unless already expressly included within an approved contract, capital project, or Board-authorized expenditure schedule.

⸻

ARTICLE XI — BUDGETED ≠ AUTOMATICALLY AUTHORIZED

An approved budget establishes planned spending.

It does not necessarily mean every expenditure may be made without transaction-level authority.

BUDGET ≠ BLANK CHECK

⸻

ARTICLE XII — UNBUDGETED EXPENDITURES

Recommended initial limits:

Up to $1,000

Executive Director may approve if necessary and funds are available.

$1,000.01–$5,000

Executive Director + Treasurer.

Above $5,000

Board approval.

Repeated unbudgeted expenditures shall not be fragmented to avoid approval thresholds.

⸻

ARTICLE XIII — NO TRANSACTION SPLITTING

A person shall not divide one purchase into multiple smaller transactions to avoid:

* Approval threshold
* Competitive review
* Board approval
* Banking controls

$12,000 PURCHASE ≠ FOUR $3,000 PURCHASES

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — CHECK SIGNING

Checks shall be signed only by authorized bank signers established by Board resolution.

Recommended control:

$0–$2,500

One authorized signer after valid underlying approval.

OVER $2,500

Two authorized approvals should be documented.

Where the banking system supports dual authorization, use it for material transactions.

⸻

ARTICLE XV — ELECTRONIC PAYMENTS

ACH, wire, and other electronic transfers follow the same approval principles as checks.

Large electronic transfers should use:

* Dual authorization
* Independent verification
* Supporting documentation

⸻

ARTICLE XVI — WIRE FRAUD CONTROL

Any unexpected change to vendor:

* Bank
* Routing
* Account
* Payment instructions

shall be independently verified using a trusted contact method.

Do not rely solely on an email requesting changed bank instructions.

⸻

ARTICLE XVII — CREDIT CARD AUTHORITY

Organizational cards shall have:

* Named cardholder
* Limit
* Approved purpose

Recommended initial individual card limit:

$2,500

unless Board/executive approval establishes another amount.

Card possession does not constitute authority to exceed transaction approval limits.

⸻

ARTICLE XVIII — PERSONAL REIMBURSEMENTS

A person shall not finally approve their own material reimbursement.

Example:

Founder submits $1,000 reimbursement.

Founder may document the expense.

Founder does not provide final independent approval.

⸻

ARTICLE XIX — RELATED-PARTY EXPENDITURES

Any transaction involving:

* Founder
* Director
* Officer
* Family member
* Related company

requires GOV-002/GOV-003 review.

Ordinary dollar thresholds do not override conflict controls.

Example:

A $300 payment to a director-owned vendor can still require conflict review.

⸻

ARTICLE XX — HIRING AUTHORITY

EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR

Board appoints and may remove.

Board approves compensation.

⸻

SENIOR DIRECTORS

Executive Director may recommend.

Recommended requirement:

Executive Director + Board Chair or designated Board committee approves senior director hiring within approved budget.

⸻

ORDINARY EMPLOYEES

Executive Director or formally delegated supervisor may hire within:

* Approved staffing plan
* Approved compensation range
* Approved budget

⸻

ARTICLE XXI — FOUNDER EMPLOYMENT

If founder is compensated:

Board of disinterested directors controls approval.

Founder shall not:

* Approve own employment terms
* Approve own salary
* Approve own bonus
* Approve own benefits

The IRS specifically supports excluding a compensated voting director from voting on their own compensation. (IRS)

⸻

ARTICLE XXII — FAMILY EMPLOYMENT

Hiring a family member of a founder/director/officer requires:

* Role description
* Qualification review
* Compensation review
* Conflict disclosure
* Independent approval

The related person shall not control their own arrangement.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIII — FIRING AUTHORITY

Executive Director

Board.

Senior Director

Executive Director within Board policy, with appropriate Board notification.

Ordinary employee

Executive Director or properly delegated supervisor.

Safeguarding or immediate-security situations may trigger temporary removal before final employment action.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIV — CONTRACT AUTHORITY

Recommended initial contract limits:

UP TO $2,500 TOTAL COMMITMENT

Executive Director.

$2,500.01–$10,000

Executive Director + Treasurer.

OVER $10,000

Board.

⸻

ARTICLE XXV — TOTAL CONTRACT VALUE

Contract authority is determined by:

TOTAL REASONABLY EXPECTED COMMITMENT

not merely monthly payment.

Example:

$1,000/month × 24 months = $24,000 contract

This is not a $1,000 transaction.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVI — LONG-TERM CONTRACTS

Board approval recommended for contracts:

* Longer than 12 months
* Automatically renewing for material commitments
* Creating significant termination penalties
* Creating substantial data/privacy exposure
* Affecting core operations

even if individual monthly payments fall below ordinary thresholds.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVII — CONTRACT SIGNER

Only a specifically authorized person may sign a contract.

Typical:

Executive Director

within delegated authority.

Contracts requiring Board approval may be signed by:

* President/Chair
* Executive Director
* Other officer

only after Board authorization.

⸻

ARTICLE XXVIII — NO APPARENT-AUTHORITY ABUSE

Personnel shall not tell a vendor:

“Just start and we’ll work out approval later.”

Unauthorized commitments expose the mission to unnecessary risk.

⸻

ARTICLE XXIX — GRANT APPLICATIONS

ROUTINE GRANTS UP TO $25,000 REQUESTED

Executive Director may authorize submission when:

* Program is already approved
* No unusual restrictions
* No required organizational match outside budget
* No debt
* No real-estate obligation

⸻

GRANTS OVER $25,000

Executive Director + Treasurer/Board Chair review.

⸻

MATERIAL OR HIGH-OBLIGATION GRANTS

Board approval required when a grant:

* Requires material matching funds
* Creates new permanent staffing
* Requires significant capital investment
* Restricts religious activity materially
* Creates repayment/clawback risk
* Creates multi-year commitments
* Creates major new program obligations

⸻

ARTICLE XXX — GRANT ACCEPTANCE ≠ GRANT APPLICATION

Authority to submit an application does not automatically authorize acceptance of every award term.

Award terms shall be reviewed before acceptance.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXI — FUNDRAISING CAMPAIGNS

Executive Director may authorize routine fundraising within adopted policy.

Board approval should be required for:

* Major capital campaigns
* Property campaigns
* Endowment campaigns
* Campaigns involving significant naming rights
* Campaigns creating major donor restrictions

⸻

ARTICLE XXXII — DONATION ACCEPTANCE

Routine gifts:

Authorized development/finance personnel.

Restricted gifts:

Treasurer/executive review.

Real estate, complex assets, business interests, major encumbered assets:

Board approval pursuant to FIN-001.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIII — BANK ACCOUNTS

Only the Board may authorize establishment or closure of organizational bank accounts.

The Board resolution shall establish:

* Bank
* Account purpose
* Signers
* Online users
* Authority limits

No employee independently opens an account in The Tabernacle’s name without corporate authority.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIV — INVESTMENTS

Material investment policy and establishment of significant investment accounts require Board approval.

Routine implementation may be delegated to authorized personnel.

Speculative investment shall not occur merely because funds are temporarily idle.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXV — DIGITAL-ASSET WALLETS

Creation of any organizational cryptocurrency wallet requires:

* Board or formally delegated treasury authorization
* Custody design
* Recovery design
* Accounting
* Named custodians

No organizational digital asset shall be kept in an individual’s personal wallet as normal operating practice.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVI — PROPERTY SEARCH

Property Director, Executive Director, founder, or authorized representatives may:

* Search listings
* Contact brokers
* Contact property owners
* Gather documents
* Arrange site visits
* Conduct preliminary research

No Board vote is required merely to investigate property.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVII — PROPERTY DUE-DILIGENCE SPENDING

Recommended:

Up to $2,500 per candidate

Executive Director + Property Director within Board-approved property-search budget.

$2,500.01–$10,000

Executive Director + Treasurer.

Above $10,000

Board approval.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXVIII — PROPERTY LOI

A Letter of Intent may be signed only by a person authorized for that specific transaction or class of transactions.

Recommended:

Board Chair or Executive Director after Board/property-committee authorization.

Counsel should review significant LOIs.

⸻

ARTICLE XXXIX — REAL-ESTATE PURCHASE CONTRACT

BOARD APPROVAL REQUIRED

No individual may independently sign a binding real-estate purchase contract unless the Board has expressly delegated authority for that specific transaction and terms.

⸻

ARTICLE XL — PURCHASE PRICE

The Board shall approve:

* Property
* Maximum purchase price
* Material financing
* Major conditions
* Authorized signer

Material increases require renewed authority.

⸻

ARTICLE XLI — MORTGAGE / DEBT

Board approval required for:

* Mortgage
* Line of credit
* Promissory note
* Material equipment financing
* Material secured debt

No officer may independently pledge charitable assets outside delegated authority.

⸻

ARTICLE XLII — PROPERTY CLOSING

Closing requires:

* Board authorization
* Due-diligence conditions satisfied
* Financing ready
* Insurance ready
* Authorized signatory

AUTHORITY TO NEGOTIATE ≠ AUTHORITY TO CLOSE

⸻

ARTICLE XLIII — PROPERTY SALE

Sale or transfer of substantial real property requires Board approval and applicable state-law compliance.

Professional legal review shall be obtained for material transactions.

⸻

ARTICLE XLIV — PROPERTY LEASES

Recommended authority:

Short-term ordinary operating lease with total commitment ≤ $10,000

Executive Director + Treasurer.

Material facility lease, long-term lease, or total commitment > $10,000

Board.

Any lease creating a primary operating site should receive Board review regardless of amount.

⸻

ARTICLE XLV — PROPERTY RENOVATION

Renovation spending follows approved capital-project budget.

Material change orders exceeding:

* Approved contingency
* Board-authorized total project amount

return to appropriate approval authority.

⸻

ARTICLE XLVI — SITE ACTIVATION

LIMITED-HOURS SITE

Executive Director may activate only after all approved launch requirements are met and Board authority permits.

24/7 SITE

Board authorization required for initial transition of a site to 24/7 operation.

OVERNIGHT / SHELTER MODE

Board authorization expressly required.

⸻

ARTICLE XLVII — CLOSING A SITE

Executive Director may temporarily close a site because of immediate:

* Safety
* Staffing
* Utility
* Weather
* Emergency

Permanent closure of a major site requires Board action.

⸻

ARTICLE XLVIII — PROGRAM CREATION

MINOR PROGRAM WITHIN EXISTING DIVISION

Executive Director may authorize if:

* Mission-aligned
* Within budget
* Low/moderate risk
* Existing policies cover it

NEW MAJOR PROGRAM DIVISION

Board.

⸻

ARTICLE XLIX — PROGRAM PILOTS

Executive Director may authorize a limited pilot within existing mission if:

* Budget ≤ $5,000
* No overnight housing
* No unusual legal risk
* No material new staffing obligation
* No related-party issue

Otherwise Board review.

⸻

* Open financial accounts

ARTICLE LI — EDUCATION

Education Director may:

* Develop courses
* Coordinate instructors
* Approve ordinary curriculum workflow
* Operate approved education programs

Cannot independently:

* Claim accreditation
* Sign material licensing contracts
* Issue degrees

⸻

ARTICLE LII — CORRECTIONAL OUTREACH

Prison & Reentry Director may:

* Research facilities
* Contact chaplains
* Contact volunteer coordinators
* Submit informational materials
* Pursue program applications

within organizational policy.

⸻

ARTICLE LIII — CORRECTIONAL AGREEMENTS

Any agreement creating material:

* Financial liability
* Indemnification
* Government contract obligation
* Data responsibility
* Long-term program obligation

shall receive executive/legal review and, when material, Board approval.

⸻

ARTICLE LIV — CORRECTIONAL REPRESENTATIONS

No one may publicly state:

“The Tabernacle is approved by [institution]”

until actual approval exists.

APPLICATION ≠ APPROVAL

⸻

ARTICLE LV — VOLUNTEER RECRUITMENT

Volunteer Coordinator may recruit volunteers within approved programs.

Volunteer Coordinator may not:

* Promise employment
* Promise compensation
* Create unauthorized stipends
* Waive background requirements
* Waive correctional clearance

⸻

ARTICLE LVI — SECURITY OPERATIONS

Safety & Security Director may manage ordinary approved security operations.

Cannot independently:

* Arm staff
* Create a weapons program
* Enter major security contracts
* Establish law-enforcement powers

⸻

ARTICLE LVII — ARMED SECURITY

Any decision to deploy armed security on behalf of The Tabernacle requires:

* Board authorization
* Legal/licensing review
* Insurance review
* Professional-provider review
* Written policy

⸻

ARTICLE LVIII — EMERGENCY AUTHORITY

During an immediate emergency, the person responsible for a site may take reasonable necessary action to protect:

* Life
* Safety
* Property

even when ordinary advance approval is impractical.

Examples:

* Call 911
* Evacuate
* Temporarily close site
* Authorize emergency repair necessary to prevent major damage

⸻

ARTICLE LIX — EMERGENCY EXPENDITURE

Recommended emergency authority:

Up to $5,000

Executive Director or Site Director when delay would materially endanger:

* Life
* Property
* Operations

with prompt Treasurer notification.

Above $5,000:

Executive Director + Treasurer/Board Chair where practicable.

All emergency spending shall be documented after the event.

⸻

ARTICLE LX — EMERGENCY ≠ LOOPHOLE

Routine poor planning shall not be relabeled as an emergency to bypass controls.

⸻

ARTICLE LXI — LEGAL COUNSEL

Executive Director or Board Chair may communicate with organizational counsel on ordinary matters within approved budget.

Material:

* Litigation
* IRS matter
* Property dispute
* Employment matter involving executive leadership
* Major regulatory investigation

shall be escalated to the Board as appropriate.

⸻

ARTICLE LXII — ACCOUNTANTS / AUDITORS

Treasurer and Executive Director may coordinate ordinary accounting services.

Appointment of an independent auditor/reviewer for a material engagement should receive Board or Audit/Finance Committee approval.

⸻

ARTICLE LXIII — TAX FILINGS

Preparation may be delegated.

Final review should include:

* Treasurer
* Executive Director
* Board as appropriate

Form 990 review procedures shall follow Board policy and then-current reporting requirements.

⸻

ARTICLE LXIV — FORM 1023

Form 1023 submission requires:

BOARD AUTHORIZATION

An authorized officer may execute and submit after Board approval.

⸻

ARTICLE LXV — STATE REGISTRATIONS

Executive Director, Secretary, Treasurer, or authorized professional may prepare and submit ordinary state registrations consistent with Board-approved formation.

Major structural changes require Board approval.

⸻

ARTICLE LXVI — PUBLIC STATEMENTS

ROUTINE PROGRAM COMMUNICATION

Program Director within assigned area.

ORGANIZATION-WIDE STATEMENT

Executive Director or designated communications representative.

MAJOR CRISIS

Executive Director / Board Chair / designated spokesperson.

⸻

ARTICLE LXVII — POLITICAL COMMUNICATION

No person may use The Tabernacle’s name or resources to endorse or oppose a candidate for public office.

Personal political activity must not be represented as action by The Tabernacle.

⸻

ARTICLE LXVIII — MEDIA

Only designated spokespersons may speak on behalf of the corporation regarding:

* Major incidents
* Litigation
* Investigations
* Property disputes
* Safeguarding cases
* Significant financial matters

Other personnel may speak personally only if they do not falsely imply organizational authority.

⸻

ARTICLE LXIX — SOCIAL MEDIA

Program staff may publish authorized routine material under communications policy.

No individual may independently publish:

* Confidential participant data
* Unapproved crisis statements
* Board-confidential information
* Correctional-sensitive material
* Legal allegations

⸻

ARTICLE LXX — WEBSITE

Executive leadership may manage routine website content.

Statements concerning:

* Tax status
* Accreditation
* Correctional approval
* Shelter licensing

* Government partnerships

must be factually verified before publication.

ARTICLE LXXI — INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

Routine organizational use may be managed by assigned personnel where rights are clear.

Acquisition, sale, or exclusive licensing of material organizational intellectual property requires higher-level review and potentially Board approval.

⸻

ARTICLE LXXII — POLICY APPROVAL

BOARD POLICIES

Board.

EXECUTIVE PROCEDURES

Executive Director within Board policy.

PROGRAM SOPs

Program Director + Executive approval according to risk level.

Lower-level procedures shall not contradict higher-level policy.

⸻

ARTICLE LXXIII — POLICY EXCEPTIONS

No employee may waive a Board policy merely for convenience.

Material exceptions require the authority specified by the policy or Board.

⸻

ARTICLE LXXIV — SAFEGUARDING

Safeguarding Officer may:

* Receive reports
* Restrict beneficiary access
* Recommend suspension
* Escalate to authorities

when safety requires.

No founder, executive, or program leader may order the Safeguarding Officer to suppress a legally required report.

⸻

* Payment authority
* Card access
* Banking access
* Accounting access

when credible fraud risk requires immediate protection.

ARTICLE LXXXV — AUTHORITY TEST

Before acting, every leader should ask:

DO I HAVE AUTHORITY?

IS IT WITHIN MY LIMIT?

IS IT WITHIN BUDGET?

IS THERE A CONFLICT?

DOES IT REQUIRE SECOND APPROVAL?

DOES IT REQUIRE BOARD ACTION?

WHAT EVIDENCE MUST BE PRESERVED?

If uncertain:

STOP AND ESCALATE

⸻

ARTICLE LXXXVI — UNAUTHORIZED COMMITMENTS

If someone makes an unauthorized commitment:

1. Do not conceal it.
2. Notify leadership.
3. Determine whether the organization is legally bound.
4. Consult counsel if necessary.
5. Decide whether to ratify, renegotiate, or reject.
6. Correct the authority/control problem.

Unauthorized action does not automatically become authorized merely because work has begun.

⸻

ARTICLE LXXXVII — RATIFICATION

The Board may ratify certain prior actions where lawful and appropriate.

Ratification shall be explicit.

SILENCE ≠ RATIFICATION

⸻

ARTICLE LXXXVIII — ANNUAL REVIEW

At least annually, the Board shall review:

* Spending thresholds
* Signers
* Banking permissions
* Contract authority
* Grant authority
* Property authority
* Staffing authority
* Technology administrators
* Emergency authority

Thresholds should grow with the organization rather than remain permanently fixed.

⸻

The Tabernacle adopts:

BANK SIGNER ≠ UNLIMITED SPENDING AUTHORITY

PROPERTY ACQUIRED ≠ SITE ACTIVATED

ADMIN ACCESS ≠ GOVERNANCE AUTHORITY

MERGE ≠ ADOPTION

EMERGENCY AUTHORITY ≠ CONTROL BYPASS

CAN ≠ MAY

AUTHORIZE ≠ PAY ≠ RECORD ≠ RECONCILE
