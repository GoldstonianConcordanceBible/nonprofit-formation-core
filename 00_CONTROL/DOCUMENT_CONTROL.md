1. Purpose

This policy establishes the official document-control system for <NONPROFIT LEGAL NAME>.

The organization shall maintain a controlled record of its:

* Articles of Incorporation
* Bylaws
* Board resolutions
* Board minutes
* Policies
* Standard operating procedures
* Program charters
* Financial controls
* Property records
* Contracts
* Government filings
* Compliance documents
* Training requirements
* Educational program standards
* Prison and reentry program standards
* Safety and safeguarding requirements

The purpose of document control is to establish one authoritative record of what the organization has approved, what remains in draft form, who has authority to change it, and which version is currently operative.

⸻

2. Core Control Rule

DRAFT ≠ ADOPTED

A document existing in GitHub, Discord, email, cloud storage, or any other system does not make it official.

ADOPTED ≠ FILED

Board adoption does not substitute for filing with a government authority when filing is required.

FILED ≠ APPROVED

Submission of an application does not mean that a government agency has approved it.

APPROVED ≠ PERMANENT

A policy or authorization may later expire, be amended, be revoked, or become legally obsolete.

CAN ≠ MAY

Technical ability to perform an action does not establish organizational or legal authority to perform that action.

⸻

3. Official Status Categories

Every controlled document shall have one of the following statuses.

DRAFT

The document is being written or reviewed.

It has no governing authority.

REVIEW

The document has been submitted for professional, committee, management, or Board review.

It has not yet been adopted.

BOARD APPROVED

The Board of Directors has formally adopted the document.

The approval shall be reflected in Board minutes or written consent.

FILED

The document has been submitted to the appropriate government agency or other authority.

EFFECTIVE

The document has completed every required approval or filing condition and is currently operative.

SUPERSEDED

A newer version has replaced the document.

The superseded version shall normally remain in the historical archive.

SUSPENDED

The Board has temporarily suspended operation of the document or policy.

RETIRED

The document is no longer used.

7. Authority Structure

The following hierarchy applies unless the Bylaws or applicable law provide otherwise.

Level 1 — Board of Directors

The Board controls:

* Articles
* Bylaws
* Major governance policies
* Executive compensation
* Related-party transactions
* Major debt
* Real estate purchases
* Real estate sales
* Mortgages
* Major leases
* Material contracts
* Annual budgets
* Major program creation or closure
* Form 1023
* Government filings requiring Board authority
* Dissolution
* Major organizational restructuring

Level 2 — Executive Leadership

Executive leadership may implement Board-approved policies and approve operational procedures within delegated authority.

Executive authority does not override Board policy.

Level 3 — Program Leadership

Program leaders may create operating instructions for their programs within approved policy and budget limits.

Level 4 — Staff and Volunteers

Staff and volunteers execute approved procedures.

They do not independently create organizational policy.

⸻

8. Founder Authority

Founder status does not independently create unlimited governance authority.

A founder may simultaneously serve as:

* Director
* Officer
* Executive
* Employee
* Minister
* Volunteer
* Advisor

Authority comes from the person’s legally recognized organizational role and delegated authority.

Control Principle

FOUNDER ≠ CORPORATION

The nonprofit owns its charitable assets.

No founder, director, officer, or employee personally owns nonprofit funds, property, donations, or restricted charitable assets.

⸻

9. Board Approval Standard

A policy requiring Board approval becomes official only after:

1. The document is circulated or presented.
2. Required conflicts are disclosed.
3. Required recusals occur.
4. A quorum exists.
5. The Board votes or executes valid written consent.
6. The action is recorded in official minutes.
7. The document status is changed to BOARD APPROVED.
8. The effective version is preserved.

⸻

11. GitHub Control

GitHub shall serve as a controlled governance, policy, provenance, and operating-document repository.

The default branch should represent the most current controlled record.

Recommended workflow:

REVIEW

15. Email Control

Email may document discussions and approvals where legally appropriate, but important governance decisions shall also be reflected in corporate records.

Material agreements should not exist solely inside personal email accounts.

17. Beneficiary Privacy

People receiving food, shelter, ministry, education, prison services, reentry services, or other assistance shall not automatically become public content.

Do not publish identifiable beneficiary information merely because a person participated in a nonprofit program.

Photography, testimonials, interviews, case studies, or public stories involving beneficiaries should follow appropriate consent, safeguarding, correctional, privacy, and institutional requirements.

⸻

18. Correctional Records

Correctional programs require enhanced control.

Do not place the following in public GitHub or ordinary Discord channels:

* Prisoner identification numbers
* Personal correspondence
* Criminal-history reports
* Facility security procedures
* Visitor-clearance information
* Protected case information
* Confidential institutional instructions
* Personal reentry case plans

Program policies may be public.

Individual case records should not be public.

⸻

19. Homelessness Program Records

The organization should separate:

Governance data

Safe for controlled policy repositories.

From:

Individual case data

Which requires protected case-management practices.

A person’s homelessness, medical circumstances, family situation, addiction history, immigration situation, mental-health information, identification records, or other sensitive information shall not be posted in a public repository.

⸻

20. Security Information

Public policies may describe general security governance.

Do not publish information that would materially compromise a facility’s security, including:

* Alarm codes
* Camera blind spots
* Access credentials
* Keys
* Detailed response weaknesses
* Staff private contact details
* Security schedules that create vulnerability

Maintain controlled copies of:

* Filed Articles
* Amendments
* Bylaws
* EIN confirmation
* IRS filings
* IRS determination letter
* State registrations
* Licenses
* Permits
* Property deeds
* Major leases
* Major contracts
* Insurance certificates
* Board resolutions

⸻

22. Records Hierarchy

Where documents conflict, use this hierarchy unless applicable law dictates otherwise:

1. Applicable law
2. Articles of Incorporation
3. Bylaws
4. Board resolutions
5. Board-approved policies
6. Executive procedures
7. Program SOPs
8. Staff instructions
9. Informal communications

A lower-level document may never override a higher-level governing document.

⸻

23. Emergency Authority

Emergency operational decisions may be made by authorized leadership where immediate action is necessary to protect:

* Human life
* Health
* Safety
* Property
* Legal compliance
* Continuity of essential services

Emergency authority does not permit permanent alteration of Board governance.

Material emergency actions shall be documented and reported to the Board promptly.

⸻

24. Annual Review

At least annually, leadership shall review the controlled document index.

Review should determine:

* Which documents remain current
* Which require legal review
* Which require Board renewal
* Which permits expire
* Which insurance policies renew
* Which government registrations renew
* Which policies require training updates
* Which documents should be superseded

26. Non-Negotiable Control Principle

The organization shall distinguish between:

IDEA

DRAFT

AUTHORITY

EXECUTION

EVIDENCE

An idea becomes a draft.

A draft becomes authorized through proper governance.

Authorized work is executed through controlled operations.

Execution produces evidence.

Evidence becomes part of the organization’s institutional record.
