ARTICLE I — PURPOSE

The purpose of this policy is to prevent The Tabernacle, Inc. from acquiring real property without understanding:

* Ownership
* Legal restrictions
* Permitted uses
* Physical condition
* Environmental condition
* Renovation requirements
* Operating costs
* Insurance
* Financing
* Community impacts
* Program suitability

No church, former church, land parcel, commercial building, residence, school, warehouse, community center, or other real property shall be acquired solely because the purchase price appears attractive.

⸻

ARTICLE II — CORE CONTROL RULE

PROPERTY IDENTIFIED ≠ PROPERTY APPROVED

PROPERTY AFFORDABLE ≠ PROPERTY SUSTAINABLE

CHURCH BUILDING ≠ APPROVED 24/7 USE

WORSHIP USE ≠ SHELTER USE

PURCHASE AGREEMENT ≠ CLOSING AUTHORITY

DONATED PROPERTY ≠ FREE PROPERTY

PROPERTY OWNED ≠ PROPERTY PERMITTED

ARTICLE VIII — RELIGIOUS LAND-USE RIGHTS

The Tabernacle may have protections under applicable federal and state religious-land-use laws.

However:

RELIGIOUS PROTECTION ≠ AUTOMATIC APPROVAL OF EVERY USE

Religious-land-use protections shall be evaluated with counsel when zoning materially burdens or treats religious use differently.

The organization shall not assume that religious status independently authorizes shelter, residential, food-service, construction, or other separately regulated activities.

ARTICLE XIII — ENVIRONMENTAL DUE DILIGENCE

The Tabernacle shall evaluate environmental risks before acquiring property.

Depending on the property, this may include:

* Phase I Environmental Site Assessment
* Historical land-use review
* Underground storage tanks
* Petroleum
* Hazardous substances
* Asbestos
* Lead-based paint
* Mold
* Radon
* Contaminated soil
* Contaminated groundwater
* Prior industrial uses
* Nearby environmental risks

⸻

ARTICLE XIV — PHASE I ENVIRONMENTAL SITE ASSESSMENT

For material acquisitions, particularly older, commercial, industrial, mixed-use, or otherwise potentially affected properties, the Board should consider obtaining a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment from a qualified environmental professional.

Environmental review should occur early enough to affect the acquisition decision.

If the Phase I identifies recognized environmental conditions or recommends further investigation, leadership shall determine whether a Phase II or other investigation is appropriate before closing.

⸻

ARTICLE XV — ENVIRONMENTAL LIABILITY

Purchase price shall not be considered in isolation from potential environmental liability.

A low-cost property may create substantial:

* Investigation costs
* Cleanup costs
* Usage limitations
* Financing problems
* Insurance problems
* Legal exposure

Environmental concerns shall be escalated to qualified professionals.

ARTICLE XXXI — INSURABILITY GATE

If intended operations cannot reasonably be insured, the Board shall treat that as a material acquisition risk.

Do not solve an insurance problem by withholding material intended uses from the insurer.

ARTICLE XL — DONATED PROPERTY

A donated church or building must undergo due diligence.

Potential liabilities may include:

* Deferred maintenance
* Environmental contamination
* Liens
* Restrictions
* Insurance cost
* Property taxes
* Code violations
* Utilities
* Demolition obligations

DONATION VALUE ≠ ECONOMIC BENEFIT

ARTICLE XLI — RELATED-PARTY PROPERTY

Any purchase, lease, donation, financing, management arrangement, or construction relationship involving:

* Founder
* Director
* Officer
* Family member
* Related business

shall trigger enhanced conflict review under GOV-002 and GOV-003.

Interested directors shall not improperly control approval.

ARTICLE XLII — PURCHASE AGREEMENT

Where appropriate, purchase agreements should include sufficient contingencies allowing The Tabernacle to investigate material issues before becoming unconditionally obligated.

Potential contingencies may address:

* Financing
* Title
* Inspection
* Environmental review
* Zoning/use
* Board approval
* Other transaction-specific requirements

Counsel shall determine appropriate contract language.

* Escrow holder
* Deadlines
* Contingencies
* Authority to sign
* Source of funds

* Purchase agreements

* Deeds
* Major leases
* Financing documents
* Material amendments

ARTICLE XLVI — RED FLAGS

Any of the following shall be specifically escalated:

* Unclear title
* Significant liens
* Major structural failure
* Unresolved environmental issue
* Prohibited intended use
* Uninsurable operations
* Major undocumented renovation
* Seller pressure to waive diligence
* Hidden related-party relationship
* Unaffordable debt
* Lack of operating reserves
* Severe code issue
* Material deed restriction
* Inability to lawfully operate core programs

ARTICLE XLVII — BOARD DECISION

After reviewing the acquisition package, the Board shall select one:

GO

Proceed under approved terms.

GO WITH CONDITIONS

Proceed only when stated conditions are satisfied.

HOLD

Additional investigation required.

RENEGOTIATE

Property may be acceptable at different terms.

NO-GO

Do not acquire.

ARTICLE XLVIII — GO WITH CONDITIONS

Conditional approval shall state:

* Condition
* Responsible person
* Deadline
* Evidence required
* Person authorized to confirm satisfaction

A condition shall not be treated as satisfied merely because closing approaches.

6. Inventory keys.

STRONG DONATIONS DO NOT CURE FIRE-SAFETY FAILURE
